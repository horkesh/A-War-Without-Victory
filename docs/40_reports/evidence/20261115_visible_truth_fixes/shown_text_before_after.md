## rs_strategic_goals option 0 consequence 3 (visible explanation)
BEFORE: The all-six platform is structurally incompatible with the Vance-Owen Peace Plan's ten-province ethnic mixing (Karadžić IT-95-5/18-T §§3471-3475; UN S/25221). The RS Assembly rejection at Bijeljina 5-6 May 1993 follows from this platform choice; Karadžić IT-95-5/18-T §§3526-3530 records the Pale leadership's acquiescence in the Assembly outcome. The same maximalist platform also produces the August 1994 rejection of the Contact Group plan that triggers Belgrade's embargo response (Karadžić IT-95-5/18-T §§4263-4310).
AFTER : The six goals cannot be reconciled with the Vance-Owen Plan. Karadžić signed it in Athens on 2 May 1993 subject to the Assembly's approval, and the Assembly rejected it; Karadžić himself told it in May that the plan was catastrophic for the Serbs, who would be left in enclaves. A referendum on 15-16 May confirmed the rejection. The same platform leads to the rejection of the Contact Group plan in July 1994, after which Belgrade blockades the Drina. (ICTY, Karadžić trial judgment, paras 372, 375, 3283 and 3295.)

## rs_strategic_goals option 0 consequence 4 (visible explanation)

The maximalist platform structurally accepts the framework even as the corridor is narrowed — the diplomatic legitimization of Republika Srpska is the value capture; the historical default (accept) follows from this platform. Pale's Assembly backed it, so accepting is the historical course and gives Republika Srpska a republic of its own; the plan fell when the Muslim side rejected it at the end of September. Rejecting it would raise the pressure from Belgrade, which was urging the Bosnian Serbs toward peace plans. (ICTY, Karadžić trial judgment, paras 378-382 and 3292.)

## rs_strategic_goals option 0 consequence 11 (visible explanation)
BEFORE: The all-six platform leads Holbrooke to bypass Pale entirely and route US pressure through Milošević in Belgrade (Holbrooke, To End a War ch. 8). The Patriarch Paper of 29 August 1995 (Karadžić IT-95-5/18-T para. 424) delegates negotiating authority to a Milošević-chaired joint Yugoslav-Bosnian Serb delegation, which then binds the RS to the Geneva Agreed Basic Principles (UN S/1995/780, 8 September 1995) and New York Further Agreed Principles (UN S/1995/827, 26 September 1995). The maximalist platform raises the political cost of acquiescence but the post-Deliberate Force military exhaustion structurally binds the decision toward compliance.
AFTER : Adopting all six goals leaves Pale little room in the autumn of 1995. On 29 August, at Dobanovci near Belgrade, the Bosnian Serb leaders agreed to a joint delegation led by Milošević to negotiate for both Belgrade and Pale, and Karadžić and Mladić were replaced in the delegation by Koljević and Buha. On 29 October Karadžić authorized the delegation to negotiate at Dayton and remained a consultative voice in the talks. Holbrooke still dealt with Karadžić directly, as he did on 14 September. The leadership must decide whether to work through Belgrade or to reject the US framework. The maximalist goals raise the political cost of giving way, but the military pressure after the NATO campaign pushes toward compliance. (ICTY, Karadžić trial judgment, paras 424, 431-432 and 4884.)
