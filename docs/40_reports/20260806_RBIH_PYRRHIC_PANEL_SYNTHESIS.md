**Date**: 2026-08-06. **Convened by**: owner instruction, "I want a panel review, BUT I also want suggestions on what we should change." **Process**: same 13-role roster as the 2026-08-05 RS panel (Game Designer, Historian, Technical Architect, Canon Compliance Reviewer, Product Manager, Architect, Gameplay Programmer, Systems Programmer, UI/UX Developer, Determinism Auditor, Formation Expert, QA Engineer, Scenario Harness Engineer), reviewed `docs/40_reports/20260806_RBIH_BEST_OUTCOME_PLAYTHROUGH.md` independently and in parallel, each blind to the others. This synthesis is the Orchestrator's — findings attributed by role; priority order is my judgment, drawing on where the panel converged.

**This panel found more wrong with the source report than the RS panel did.** Where the RS panel corrected 4 factual claims, this one overturned or heavily qualified the report's two headline claims (the strategy ranking, and the institutional-choices finding), found the evidence chain itself was confounded (two unreset module-level state carriers across strategies run in one process), and discovered a genuinely new production bug along the way. The corrected primary-source report is at the path above; read it alongside this synthesis.

## Part 2 — what survived and hardened

- **The harness porting approach itself** (extending `president_playthrough.ts` rather than a second Electron/Playwright run) — confirmed sound and correctly avoids duplicating a competing harness (Scenario Harness Engineer).
- **Srebrenica's week-162 timing is structurally impossible for RBiH to move — upgraded from "observed, not explained" to CONFIRMED, with the follow-up idea closed.** Historian: no relief capacity existed by 1995 historically (a 1993 relief attempt was assessed as hopeless; by 1995 the enclave had a single functioning artillery piece). Formation Expert, independently, from the code: the fall is a scripted event whose trigger flags read zero military state, and even setting that aside, the garrison brigades are enclave-tagged and triple-locked (cannot move outside an 11-OSID area, cannot join any operation with outside objectives, hard-capped at 600 personnel regardless of national manpower). **Do not fund an enclave-defense follow-up run; do not build a mechanic to let RBiH save Srebrenica.** Both roles independently arrived at "this is the game correctly modeling something real," not a gap.
- **Maximal political defiance genuinely devastating the composite score is the scoring thesis working, not a miscalibration** — Game Designer, Canon Compliance Reviewer, and Product Manager converge: canon's own weights (`VICTORY_AND_PYRRHIC_SCORING.md` §3.1) put RBiH's `international_standing` at the highest weight of any dimension for any faction specifically because "the survivor state the world recognizes" is RBiH's thesis; §6.3 forbids territory buying back a collapsed standing. `maximal_activist` didn't get dragged to 12.4 by an unfair penalty — it earned mediocre military/territorial grades *and* four zeros; the 12.4 is an honest weighted average.

## Part 3 — new findings the original report never had

- **A real, previously-unknown, calibration-moving engine bug: `collectEffects()` double-applies event effects.** Gameplay Programmer traced why `war_crimes_events` read 10 instead of the 7 implied by the five authored RBiH-specific events: `src/sim/events/evaluate_events.ts`'s `collectEffects()` applies both an event's singular `effect` field *and* every member of its `effects[]` array — several authored events carry the same effect in both, so it lands twice. Confirmed on multiple RS/HRHB events too (`ahmici_massacre_1993`, `hvo_detention_camps_1993`, `sovici_doljani_attack_1993`, `stupni_do_massacre_1993`, and others). This isn't scoped to war-crimes counting — it hits every effect kind on any event with the duplicated-field shape: `patron_pressure`, `morale_change`, `supply_delta`, `control_change`, and negotiation-capital dimension feeds. **This needs its own isolated, 188w-gated fix, run alone, per the sacred one-change rule** — it is not safe to bundle with anything else in this list.
- **A canon-vs-code conflict, sharper than the RS panel's queued doc gap.** Canon Compliance Reviewer: `COST_GRADE_CAPS`'s `duration_full_weeks: 156` reference means any full-length 188-week campaign saturates the duration cost sub-score by construction — grade `C`-or-worse is structurally guaranteed for RBiH on *any* strategy, including the unmodified historical path. This directly conflicts with canon's own text (`VICTORY_AND_PYRRHIC_SCORING.md` §2) stating historical RBiH "barely earned survival" — survival is grade A. Canon asserts the historical outcome earns A; code cannot currently produce A for a full-length run. Recommended fix: raise `duration_full_weeks` to match the actual campaign length (Canon Compliance suggests 208, matching `max_turns`), re-examine `casualties_full`, and re-measure — calibration-sensitive, needs `/game-designer` + `/war-or-game` sign-off, one change at a time.
- **`forceLaunch` is broken in the harness — and the identical bug ships in production.** Scenario Harness Engineer: the harness's `forceLaunch` reads `state.corps_command` instead of `state.military.corps_command`, so it always fails silently. The exact same field-path bug exists in `src/desktop/electron-main.cjs:2571` — meaning the real, shipped FORCE-LAUNCH presidential lever may be dead in the actual desktop app. This is a genuine production defect, discovered as a side effect of building the harness, independent of anything else in this report.
- **A live production risk for the silent-swallow bug, not harness-exclusive — status: reachable-but-unfired-on-any-checked-artifact.** Technical Architect and Gameplay Programmer, independently: `freezeEndgameSnapshot`'s bare-catch (`tryDerive`, `src/sim/endgame/endgame_snapshot.ts:47-53`) is called from `peace_plans.ts` and `war_termination.ts` on live pipeline state, and `scenario_runner.ts` — the actual production headless runner — carries one live `GameState` object through all 188 turns after a single setup-time deserialize, structurally identical to what broke in the harness. A quick check (Orchestrator, mid-panel) found one committed 4-week `mutual_collapse` test fixture with `historical_comparison` fully populated — but Technical Architect correctly downgraded this as weak evidence (different termination writer than the one implicated; a 4-week run has had almost no chance to accumulate the hypothesized drift). No full 188-week Dayton-resolved production artifact exists in the repo to test the exact scenario directly. **Status: P1-untested, not confirmed either way** — Technical Architect's cheapest closing method: land the `derive_failures` recording fix first (Part 5, item 2), then re-run the harness *without* the round-trip workaround; if failures appear, both this and item 5 above are answered by the same run.
- **An OOB data-integrity defect, separate from everything above.** Formation Expert: 12 of 13 `composition`-bearing brigades in `data/source/oob_brigades.json` have `composition.infantry` exceeding `initial_personnel` — all 12 are enclave-tagged RBiH formations (the five Srebrenica brigades, the Bihać pocket's seven). One outlier (`arbih_285th_light`, Žepa) is wrong in the *opposite* direction. Calibration-relevant (enclave defender power feeds the 188w floor of record); needs its own isolated lane, never bundled.
- **An engine-side silent-failure companion to the harness bug.** Gameplay Programmer: unknown `institutional_choices` keys are silently swallowed with no diagnostic and no capital charge (`bot_negotiation.ts`'s cost-computation and clamp both `continue` on an unrecognized package id) — which is exactly what let the harness's mis-keyed proposal run three full 188-week campaigns undetected. Same class of "silent degradation, no error surfaced" pattern as the `tryDerive` finding above.

## Part 5 — prioritized, bold recommendations (Orchestrator synthesis)

Ordered by (impact × confidence) ÷ effort. **[CONVERGENT]** marks findings caught by 2+ specialists independently.

### Fix the harness before trusting any number from it again

1. **Reset `requestOpCursor` per-strategy (local, not module-level) and call `resetDisplacementPressureCache()` at campaign start. [CONVERGENT — 2 specialists, different mechanisms]** One-line and few-line fixes respectively. Nothing downstream is trustworthy until both land — QA Engineer explicitly blocks trusting the harness as an evidence-producing tool until this class of fix is in.
2. **Fix the institutional_choices key mismatch** (use the six real package IDs) and **re-run the centralization question properly** before concluding anything about whether RBiH can secure it. Canon Compliance Reviewer's independent read (the confound is bundling ambitious territorial demands with the institutional ask, which arms the objection path) and Gameplay Programmer's (wrong keys entirely) both point at the same next step: a clean re-test with correct keys and a moderate bundle.
3. **Fix `forceLaunch`'s field-path bug in the harness, and file the identical `electron-main.cjs:2571` bug separately as a real production defect** — the second one matters regardless of what happens to the harness.
4. **Add a lever-isolated strategy arm** (`autonomy_level=1` + historical-op acceptance, zero presidential levers) so the territorial delta can actually be attributed between "levers" and "autonomy/historical-ops," per Scenario Harness Engineer and Formation Expert's independent recommendation (Formation Expert additionally proposes a `patron_confidence`-pinned arm to isolate a specific hypothesis: PDP-driven operation-launch hesitation, not lever usage, may be the real mechanism behind the 19-vs-10 operations gap between `faithful_activist` and `maximal_activist`).
5. **Verify reproducibility before citing any number again**: extend `DecisionLogEntry` into a tagged action log covering levers/proposals/Dayton (not just events), run one strategy twice and assert identical `finalStateHash`, then replay the action log and assert the hash again. This is the harness's original determinism guarantee; it was never actually exercised for the lever-driven path.
6. **Re-run all three strategies (plus the new isolation arms) only after 1–5 land.** Everything in the corrected primary-source report's results table should be treated as provisional until this happens.

### Real production/engine fixes surfaced along the way

7. **Fix `collectEffects()`'s double-apply.** Own isolated lane, 188w-gated, one change, run alone — this is the single highest-value *engine* fix on this list because it's calibration-moving and touches multiple effect kinds across all three factions, not just RBiH's war-crimes count.
8. **Replace `tryDerive`'s bare catch with a recorded-failure version** (`endgame_snapshot.derive_failures: string[]`, additive/observational field). This is the fix that makes the P1-untested production risk self-verifying — land it, then re-run the harness without the round-trip workaround, and the same run answers both "was this ever live in production" and "what was the real exception."
9. **Fix `state.corps_command` → `state.military.corps_command` in `electron-main.cjs:2571`** — a real, separate, player-facing bug independent of everything else here.
10. **Amend `VICTORY_AND_PYRRHIC_SCORING.md` §3.2 for `COST_GRADE_CAPS`, and specifically raise `duration_full_weeks`** so grade A/`survival` is reachable on a full-length campaign as canon's own text asserts it should be for the historical path. Calibration-sensitive, `/game-designer` + `/war-or-game` sign-off, measure before merge, one change at a time. This is a stronger, code-level version of the RS panel's already-queued doc-only fix — both should land together.
11. **Add `institutional_choices_rejected` to `DaytonResult`** (Architect) so a flipped institutional demand leaves a trace distinguishable from "no demand was made" — currently a total blackout, not just a display gap.
12. **Validate `institutional_choices` (and `competency_allocation`/`constitutional_choices`) keys at the Dayton sim boundary** rather than silently swallowing unknown IDs with no diagnostic and no cost — the same silent-degradation shape as the `tryDerive` finding, and what let the harness bug run undetected for three full campaigns.

### Test coverage (QA Engineer, largely un-contested by other roles)

13. **Add a real terminal-state test for `endgame_snapshot` derivation completeness** that runs an actual campaign to a genuine ending and asserts every `tryDerive`-guarded field is *defined*, not merely present as a key — the existing `tests/endgame_snapshot_freeze.test.ts` asserts `'field' in snapshot`, which passes identically whether the value is real or `undefined`, and is precisely the test that could not have caught this bug.
14. **Add a positive-case Dayton institutional test**: drive `resolveDaytonNegotiation` with a correctly-keyed `centralized` demand and no bot objection, assert it survives to `result.institutional_choices`. No existing test pins this — every current Dayton test either uses `{}` or asserts only territorial determinism.
15. **Add lever-wrapper contract tests**: each of the 13 new `president_playthrough.ts` exports should assert a valid call returns `{ok:true}`, an invalid call returns a stable error code (never a throw), and — the load-bearing clause — no lever mutates state when it returns `ok:false`. That's the minimum bar for trusting any future run's failure counts (like the 189 REPLACE-CO failures) as genuinely inert.

### Don't do

- **Do not surface `pyrrhic_score` above the letter grade, or promote it as a scoreboard.** Already displayed; canon-blocked (§5.1/§0/§6.7) independent of the display question. Fix the *grade* (item 10), don't hide or duplicate the score.
- **Do not fund a dedicated Srebrenica-defense follow-up run.** Answered: structurally impossible, confirmed two independent ways.
- **Do not generalize the state round-trip into a per-turn canonicalization step** (Systems Programmer) — it re-runs the full 36+ migration chain every turn, is not proven idempotent, and would paper over the real defect (the swallowed exception) rather than fixing it.
- **Do not treat any number in the original report's results table as settled** until the harness fixes in this section land and the strategies are re-run. This applies equally to citing this report in the roadmap, in future panels, or in player-facing design discussion.

## Part 6 — process retrospective

Retrospective Analyst reviewed the process itself. Verdict: **the "freeze the artifact" lesson from the RS panel's own retrospective was applied correctly — the report was untouched from before dispatch until the full correction rewrite in Part 1/5 above, with no live-edit duplicate blocks or half-applied banners. The "integration" half of that lesson (a named integrator running a contradiction sweep before any edit lands) is confirmed as still not a standing process, just something the Orchestrator happened to do properly this time** — worth making explicit for future panels rather than relying on it recurring by habit.

Independently re-verified one convergent finding in about 30 seconds (`pyrrhic_score` at `VerdictScreen.tsx:772`/`:511`) as a demonstration that this is exactly what parallel-blind fan-out is for: an author's own framing error is precisely the kind of thing a sequential review would have inherited rather than caught.

Two gaps flagged, both already substantially addressed above but worth restating as standing process items:
- **Raw evidence reproducibility recurred as a gap across both panels.** RS's retrospective flagged it; this panel's Scenario Harness Engineer and QA Engineer both independently raised it again (items 5 and 13 above already cover the mechanism, but the retrospective's sharper ask is to **commit or hash the JSONL evidence itself**, not just re-verify hashes at rerun time — a third recurrence of the same gap across a third panel would establish a real pattern worth a standing rule, not another one-off fix).
- **`war_crimes_events` identical-to-the-integer across three runs** — now fully explained by the `collectEffects()` double-apply finding (Part 3), so this specific instance is closed, but the retrospective's broader point (this kind of "suspiciously identical" signal is cheap to spot and expensive to leave unexplained) stands as a general QA habit worth keeping.

**Scale observation, worth carrying into future panels**: a fixed 13-role roster copy-pasted from the prior panel is defensible as cheap insurance but not optimal — this report had essentially no militia/formation content in its own text, yet Formation Expert's seat still produced two of this synthesis's more important findings (Srebrenica confirmation, the OOB composition defect) by reading the underlying code rather than the report's framing. UI/UX Developer's seat, by contrast, was load-bearing specifically because the report made a renderer claim. Retrospective's recommendation: **compose the panel from what the report's own sections imply, not by reusing the previous panel's roster** — in this case it happened to still be the right set, but that shouldn't be assumed automatically next time.

**Process recommendation for future panels, stated plainly**: assign a named integration role up front (could be the Orchestrator explicitly, or a dedicated pass) whose job is a contradiction sweep across all reports against the frozen artifact *before* any correction edit lands — not implicitly, as happened to work out here.
