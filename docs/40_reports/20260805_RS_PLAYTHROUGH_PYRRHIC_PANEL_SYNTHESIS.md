## Part 2 — what survived review and hardened

- **All 13 counterfactual political decisions were accepted by the engine with no silent reversion.** Every specialist who touched this agreed it's real and it's the strongest evidence of genuine political agency in the game. Historian: two of these are *too* cheap (see Part 4), one is exactly right, one is partially wrong on causal channel — but the underlying fact (the engine let 13-for-13 through) is undisputed.
- **`pending_dayton` never clearing at game-over is real, and the root cause is now nailed down with unusual confidence** — four independent investigations (Technical Architect, Systems Programmer, Gameplay Programmer, Determinism Auditor) converged on the exact same lines of code:
  - The only code that deletes `pending_dayton` is `resolvePendingDaytonCloseOut` (`src/sim/negotiation/dayton_negotiation.ts:216-231`), gated on `state.meta.dayton_close_out !== true` (line 217) — a flag only `scenario_runner.ts` ever sets (headless-only). Its sole call site is `scenario_runner.ts:2880`.
  - The real player path, `resolveDaytonNegotiation` (called from `electron-main.cjs:3272-3290`), writes `dayton_result`, sets `game_over`, freezes the endgame snapshot — and **never deletes `pending_dayton` itself**. Request and receipt are written by two different owners; nothing enforces that they travel together.
  - Technical Architect's sharpest addition: because `advance-turn` checks `listBlockingPlayerDecisions` *before* running the turn pipeline (`electron-main.cjs:2118` before `:2134`), once `pending_dayton` dangles, the uncleaned state holds shut the only gate through which any future pipeline-based cleanup could ever run. It is a self-sealing deadlock, not just a missed cleanup.
  - Determinism Auditor's addition: this is **player-reachable, not automation-only** — "Decline Talks" in the real Dayton modal only clears the client's local copy, never the server state, so a player who declines and resubmits can hit the same dangling packet.
  - `src/state/player_decision_manifest.ts` contains **zero references to `game_over` anywhere** (QA Engineer, Systems Programmer, Technical Architect all independently grepped and confirmed this) — the manifest that decides what blocks `advance-turn` is entirely terminal-state-blind, by construction, for every decision family, not just Dayton.
- **`op_directive_rejection` is computed, written, and projected to the client — and has zero UI/adapter readers anywhere in the codebase.** Confirmed independently by **four** specialists (Architect, UI/UX Developer, Game Designer, Gameplay Programmer), each finding the same dead field via independent greps. This is now the single most-corroborated finding in the whole review. A president who orders an attack that fails is charged 25 CA and told nothing — not because the game doesn't know why, but because the explanation is computed and then thrown away.
- **The `vrs_main_staff` REPLACE-CO gap is real, but smaller than the original report thought.** Multiple specialists (Formation Expert, Gameplay Programmer, Systems Programmer, Scenario Harness Engineer) found that the "field corps vs. supreme command" type distinction **already exists in the data** — `kind: "army_hq"` in `data/source/oob_corps.json`, `EXEMPT_CORPS_IDS`/`isSectorAssignmentExemptCorpsId` in `corps_front_sectors_constants.ts`, and `main_staff_formation_id` in `army_co_lifecycle.ts`. `co_replacement.cjs` simply never reads any of them. The fix is a filter, not a new data model.
- **RS's endgame grade (C, `pyrrhic_success`, every soft-power dimension F except territorial D) is the scoring system working correctly, not a display bug** — confirmed by Canon Compliance Reviewer, who traced it to an undocumented (but functioning) `COST_GRADE_CAPS` mechanism in `scoring.ts` that caps the grade based on a war-cost index (RS's 209k casualties, 188-week duration, and 4 war-crimes events all saturate their respective ceilings). Game Designer independently confirmed the political-dimension scoring (five Fs) is the consequence system firing as designed. The gap is that `VICTORY_AND_PYRRHIC_SCORING.md` §3.2 never documents the cap, so canon doesn't currently let a reader predict the grade.

## Part 3 — new findings the original report never had

- **A real, unrelated correctness bug: no mutation serialization in the Electron IPC layer.** Systems Programmer found that every mutating `ipcMain.handle` follows read-modify-write against one module-global JSON string with no locking; Electron does not serialize concurrent `invoke`s. Two overlapping presidential actions can both pass their "not already pending" guard, both compute a valid mutation, and the later `writeCanonicalCurrentState` silently overwrites the earlier one — losing an order and its CA debit with no error to either caller. Not found by the playthrough itself; found by a specialist reading the code the playthrough exercises.
- **A likely root cause for RS's `combat_effective_brigades: 11` (and a documented prior mystery).** Formation Expert pulled the untouched 188w calibration baseline as a control and found RS at 7/53 combat-effective there too (worse than this run's 11/N) — meaning constant presidential lever-spam did not cause this, and the interventionist run actually did *better* than the bot-only baseline. The real cause: `morale_drift.ts`'s victory/defeat multipliers are asymmetric (RS 0.8x victory / 1.3x defeat vs RBiH 1.3x / 0.7x) with no bound on compounding over 188 weeks, driving RS's median brigade morale to 37 against RBiH's 97 — despite RS's *personnel* numbers sitting squarely inside the historical VRS band. Formation Expert connects this directly to the already-documented, previously-unexplained `rs_brigade_destruction_asymmetry_engine_flaw` finding from a prior session (0% ARBiH vs. 61-63% RS permanent brigade loss at 188w) and to the `operations_launched: 0` finding (corps-operation launch readiness folds in mean subordinate morale at 40% weight, so RS's suppressed morale structurally suppresses launch readiness for every RS corps, every turn — a second, independent contributor alongside Gameplay Programmer's authorization-gate finding).
- **A falsified premise, caught and resolved.** The report claimed "RS never had a live operation to STOP, all game." Formation Expert found RS's turn-0 startup snapshot actually has 6 of 8 corps carrying named pre-planned operations. This is reconciled, not contradictory: Gameplay Programmer's Gate A (`deferUnauthorizedHistoricalOperationsForPlayer`, called from `desktop_sim.ts:291` during `startNewCampaign`, before this script's first state read) strips exactly those turn-0 ops into `pending_proposal_reviews` immediately — so both specialists are right about different moments in the same sequence.
- **Prior art the original report should have used and didn't.** Scenario Harness Engineer found `tools/ai_play/president_playthrough.ts` already exists in the repo: a typed, determinism-*proven* player-decision-seam harness with a working `replayDecisionLog()` that asserts byte-identical final-state hashes. The new Playwright script re-solved a problem this project had already solved more rigorously, and lost the determinism guarantee in the process.

## Part 4 — where specialists gave genuinely different judgment calls (present both, not just one)

- **Should removing Mladić be free?** Historian: no — Karadžić tried exactly this on 4 Aug 1995 and was forced to reverse it 7 days later after Main Staff officers sided with Mladić (BB1 p.75, p.434, p.463); the `back_down` historical default exists *because the alternative was tried and failed in reality*, and the engine currently has no institutional-veto layer to model that. Canon Compliance Reviewer, working the canon-silence question independently: keep it mechanical — no `protected_officer` concept exists anywhere in canon, `Rulebook_v0_9_0.md` §5.8 explicitly treats officer war-crimes records as "informational, does not affect gameplay mechanics," and special-casing one named ICTY figure would break the documented faction-symmetric-mechanism rule for no simulation benefit. **These are not actually contradictory** — Historian is arguing VRS Main Staff loyalty/insubordination should be a modeled institutional actor (a general mechanism), not that Mladić specifically should be protected; Canon Compliance is arguing against a Mladić-specific hardcode. Both would likely endorse "model Main Staff as a political actor with a loyalty check," neither endorses "hardcode Mladić." Route to Game Designer + Historian per canon's own §8 sign-off requirement for scoring/officer-mechanic changes.
- **Command Authority spend fairness.** Product Manager explicitly declined to treat the 3.1-point territorial shortfall as a real signal ("confounded three ways... ignore it until a clean run exists") and generally argued against opening new lanes. Formation Expert and Systems Programmer, working from source rather than roadmap discipline, both independently proposed a real new workstream (morale-asymmetry rebalancing; new engine invariants). This is a legitimate tension, not an error on either side — Product Manager is applying this project's own sequencing discipline (§10 finding-routing, calibration-last, one-change-per-run), while the domain specialists are reporting what the code actually shows. My synthesis in Part 5 keeps PM's sequencing discipline but does not let it suppress the morale finding, since two independent specialists reached it from different angles and it plausibly explains three other findings at once.

## Part 5 — prioritized, bold recommendations (Orchestrator synthesis)

Ordered by (impact × confidence) ÷ effort, not by which specialist said it. Convergent findings (caught by 2+ specialists independently) are marked **[CONVERGENT]** and should be treated as near-certain.

### Ship this week

1. **Wire `op_directive_rejection` to the player. [CONVERGENT — 4 specialists]** Export it through `player_visible_state.cjs`, render it as a Desk inbox / dispatch card in the CO's own voice ("*1st Krajina Corps: your directive on Tuzla cannot be executed — no axis reaches the objective from our positions*" — Game Designer's phrasing, `plainReason()` prose mapping already exists at `src/ui/map/data/opDirectiveObjection.ts:216`). 2. **Stop charging CA for orders the engine already knows will be discarded.** `buildDirectiveObjection` already computes `issuable: false` with a doc comment saying the UI must not stage or debit in that case (Architect, `opDirectiveObjection.ts:64-71`) — the contract exists on paper and isn't enforced. Either refund on rejection or block staging when `issuable: false`.
3. **Fix `pending_dayton` at its actual root, not its symptom. [CONVERGENT — 4 specialists, same root cause]** Two-part fix, both cheap:
   - Add a `game_over` guard to `player_decision_manifest.ts`'s blocking-decision computation (Technical Architect, QA Engineer, Systems Programmer, Determinism Auditor all independently recommend this exact fix) — one line, closes this family and every future one, the only fix that also rescues already-saved states.
   - Move `delete neg.pending_dayton` into `resolveDaytonNegotiation` itself, in the same mutation that writes `dayton_result` (Gameplay Programmer, Determinism Auditor, Technical Architect all specify this same location). Add a re-entry guard (`if (neg.dayton_result || game_over) return neg.dayton_result;`) so repeat calls are provably first-write-wins (Determinism Auditor).
   - QA Engineer: write the test first — `game_over: true` + present `pending_dayton` ⇒ `listBlockingPlayerDecisions` returns `[]`. It fails today; that's the point.
4. **Filter Main Staff (and any `kind: 'army_hq'`/`jna_herzegovina_command`-shaped entity) out of corps-scoped presidential levers. [CONVERGENT — 5 specialists]** The type data already exists in three different places in the codebase; none of them are consulted by REPLACE-CO/STOP-OP/REQUEST-OP/ELITE-DEPLOY. Filter at both the IPC handler and the UI eligibility list (UI/UX Developer, Gameplay Programmer, Formation Expert). Small, mechanical, zero calibration impact.

### Don't do (explicit, with reasons — the panel earned the right to say this)

- **Do not make political counterfactuals move territory more.** Game Designer and Canon Compliance Reviewer both independently landed here from different angles: the current design (politics move the *score*, not the *map*) is the thesis working ("meaningful but constrained agency" — Game Bible l.11), and an "offensive effort" scoring term would be conquest-scoreboard drift the canon explicitly rejects.
- **Do not build a paramilitary → Srebrenica lever.** It would be building the exact thing canon's sensitive-history gate exists to prevent (atrocity as a pullable lever rather than an emergent consequence) — and per Part 1, the causal link this report proposed doesn't even exist in the trigger code today. If a follow-up run is done, frame it as falsification of a now-corrected hypothesis, not confirmation of a real mechanism (Canon Compliance Reviewer's explicit framing).
- **Do not promote the ad hoc Playwright script into a full campaign-QA harness as-is.** Scenario Harness Engineer: port the lever-driving logic onto the existing determinism-proven `president_playthrough.ts` instead of building a second, weaker campaign harness. The `.mjs` script's real value is as a short (~10 turn) real-Electron IPC-contract smoke test, not a 188-week investigative tool — it currently cannot tell its own bugs (the Dayton echo) apart from the engine's.
- **Do not couple any of this to calibration.** Every specialist who touched calibration risk (Systems Programmer, Scenario Harness Engineer, Game Designer) agreed items 1-4 and 8-9 are UI/IPC/tooling-layer only and should be provably calibration-flat; nothing here should be bundled with a match-% push, per this project's own calibration-last posture.

## Part 6 — process retrospective

Retrospective Analyst reviewed the process itself — and caught a real, second-order failure in it. Verdict: **the 12-way fan-out worked as a detector and failed as an integrator.** It read the primary report fresh (not this synthesis doc, which didn't exist yet when it started) and found that after my first correction pass, only 1 of the 4 claimed corrections had actually landed in the body text — the other 3 were banner-noted at the top but the contradicted claims still stood further down, plus an accidental duplicated block and a live self-contradiction (the new `vrs_main_staff` correction sitting a few lines above a bug entry that still called the same thing a "possible missing-officer-assignment bug"). It also independently re-derived the `pending_dayton` root cause in about five minutes from the two early-return guards in `dayton_negotiation.ts` — which is itself a strong outside confirmation that the panel's converged finding is correct and cheap to verify.

Its sharpest structural point: **breadth of seats did not guarantee coverage.** Twelve specialists read the `vrs_main_staff` claim, including Formation Expert and Gameplay Programmer who were squarely in scope for it, and none flagged that the correction itself needed a contradiction-sweep against the rest of the document — that only surfaced because Retrospective Analyst read the artifact fresh, with no framing carried over from the rest of the review. It also flagged a gap none of the 12 specialists raised: no one asked about **reproducibility** of the run itself, despite Determinism Auditor holding a seat — the evidence lives in an ephemeral scratchpad, is not committed, and (as Scenario Harness Engineer separately noted) no other person could currently re-derive any of these numbers.

Process recommendation, taken directly: **parallel-blind fan-out is right for discovery, wrong as the whole process.** What was missing wasn't specialist coverage or sequencing — it was a **named integration owner** running a contradiction sweep after the fan-out landed, against a **frozen artifact** (the report was being live-edited while the panel was still reading it, which is the direct cause of the duplicate block). This document, plus the follow-up cleanup pass on the primary report done immediately after receiving this retrospective, is that integration step, done late rather than built into the process from the start. Recommended for future multi-specialist panels: freeze the artifact under review before dispatching, and route the fan-out's output through one named reconciliation pass before calling it done — not fold silently into a live-edited document.
