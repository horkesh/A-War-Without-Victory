## Player-First Assessment

### What Works

- RS has a strong opening military identity. Six distinct historical authorizations make the command hierarchy and simultaneous theaters visible immediately.
- The map is now a credible primary play surface: counters are present, stacks are discoverable, exact units can be inspected, and Krajina is not empty.
- The Decision Room's flat card/dossier model is understandable once duplicate Sitreps and inert Review routing are removed.
- Historical operations now provide the requested goals, command ownership, committed strength, commanders where known, axes, and historical context instead of just a name and an authorization button.
- Advance blockers clear when resolved. The campaign can be played through 52 weeks without a modal or stale decision trapping the player.
- Army HQ, Records, Chronicle, and operation AARs now agree on lifecycle categories and causal outcomes closely enough to support learning from the campaign.

### Residual Product Observations

- Several mid/late-year weeks contain little presidential choice beyond reading the aftermath and advancing. This is not a hidden or broken control: the run had no pending decisions and no suppressed required content. It is a campaign-content/pacing observation. Adding new decisions or consequences requires an authored, canon-reviewed content lane rather than fabricating busywork during QA.
- Dense weeks can produce a large briefing inventory, including a 50-item/9-critical state. Existing filters and nonblocking handling keep it usable, but future content/design work should prefer aggregation by theater and decision consequence over adding more raw cards.
- RS is more operations-heavy than the prior RBiH acceptance run, while RBiH carries more political/diplomatic pressure. This asymmetry is appropriate; parity should mean equally understandable agency, not identical event counts.

The prior RBiH v47 replay used an earlier dirty-tree/startup fingerprint, so its final `85/255/372` control result is a cross-faction UX comparison, not a same-input calibration comparison. Both campaigns reached exact turn 52 with zero unresolved blockers and zero unlocated active combat formations after remediation. RS required 468 screenshots/59 sheets versus RBiH 334/42 because the RS audit deliberately exercised more historical operation and exact-map surfaces.
