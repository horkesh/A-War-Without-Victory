# Exhaustion & Scoring Redesign — Full Pyrrhic Team Brainstorm Synthesis

**Date**: 2026-08-06. **Status**: brainstorm synthesis only — no code, canon, or docs changed by this investigation. 17 Pyrrhic specialists (Game Designer, Historian, Technical Architect, Architect, Product Manager, Gameplay Programmer, Systems Programmer, Canon Compliance Reviewer, QA Engineer, Determinism Auditor, Scenario/Harness Engineer, Narrative Designer, Modern Wargame Expert, War-or-Game, Gap-Finder, Railroad Hunter, Formation Expert) dispatched in parallel, each blind to the others, grounded in a shared briefing (the 2026-05-22 exhaustion forensics memo, this week's RBiH/RS playthroughs, and direct source reads). Synthesized by one reconciler per the "freeze the artifact, one integrator" lesson from the 2026-08-05 RS panel retrospective.

**Purpose**: the owner asked to "change how both exhaustion and scoring works," using the RBiH and RS presidential playthroughs as starting points. This document integrates 17 independent specialist analyses into one coherent picture of what's actually wrong, why it keeps recurring, and what to do about it.

## Part 0 — the headline, for anyone reading only this section

- **Four separate, independently-discovered production bugs** are compounding to destroy exhaustion's signal well before any "design" question is even reached. All four are fixable today, independent of any redesign direction.
- **The §6 atrocity bright line is currently arithmetically inert** at full campaign length — confirmed independently by three specialists (War-or-Game, Game Designer, Canon Compliance Reviewer) from three different angles. This is the single highest-priority finding in the whole brainstorm.
- **There is a real canon-vs-code conflict**, not just a tuning gap: canon states historical RBiH "barely earned survival" (grade A); the engine cannot produce grade A, A+, or B on any full-length campaign. The Railroad Hunter and Canon Compliance Reviewer both independently proved this is structural, not incidental.
- **The 10000 exhaustion ceiling was never actually canon-ratified.** It's still an open "pending validation" item in `FORAWWV.md` that was explicitly *not* promoted in the last promotion batch. Both the original clamp and the 2026-05-22 rescale shipped against an open canon question.
- **A third "raise the cap" rescale is proven mathematically incapable of fixing the grade**, even before considering whether it would fix exhaustion (it wouldn't — see Part 1). The Railroad Hunter did the arithmetic: even a perfectly-fixed exhaustion term leaves the index at 0.88, still capped at C.
- **Strong convergence on a redesign shape**: 4 specialists (Systems Programmer, Technical Architect, Gameplay Programmer, Determinism Auditor) independently proposed the same fix — replace the hard-clamped accumulator with an *unbounded raw accumulator* whose *consumers* read a *pure-arithmetic saturating curve* (`raw / (raw + K)`, not `exp()`, for cross-platform determinism). This is as close to a consensus engineering answer as this brainstorm produced.
- **The exhaustion/scoring "two separable workstreams" premise is contested.** Product Manager recommends shipping a scoring-only fix first (cheap, provably calibration-flat). Gap-Finder and Canon Compliance both independently argue they can't actually be measured independently — duration alone already floors the cost index at a B-cap before a single casualty counts, so either fix shipped alone will read as a no-op and get misdiagnosed as ineffective.

## Part 1 — four newly-found production bugs (fix regardless of redesign direction)

These were each found independently by at least one specialist, several by two or three working blind to each other — that cross-validation is why confidence is high.

1. **A third, unclamped, faction-agnostic exhaustion writer** (`src/scenario/baseline_ops_scheduler.ts:52-60`, `applyBaselineOpsExhaustion()`). Runs *after* the pipeline's `Math.min(10000, ...)` clamp, writes the *same* delta to every faction regardless of their actual state, with no cap at all. This is what actually produces the byte-identical `10000.0012...` terminal value across all three factions — not the clamp alone. **Found independently by**: Systems Programmer, Technical Architect, QA Engineer.
2. **A runaway command-friction feedback loop** (`src/sim/combat/command_friction.ts`). `FRICTION_PER_EXHAUSTION = 0.01` was authored for the old 0–100 exhaustion scale and was missed in the 2026-05-22 100x rescale sweep. On the new 0–10000 scale it pins friction at its maximum multiplier (10x) within the first ~5-10 turns for every faction. That friction multiplier then feeds *back into* the exhaustion accumulator, compressing RBiH's 4:1 static-front advantage over HRHB down to roughly 1:1 at the point the per-turn clip applies. This is likely the single biggest reason the 2026-05-22 fix's "HRHB will never saturate" prediction failed. **Found independently by**: Gameplay Programmer, Gap-Finder.
3. **Three more gates missed in the same rescale sweep**, each now either permanently dead or permanently active from turn ~1: `STORM_COMBINED_EXHAUSTION = 60` (Operation Storm's exhaustion precondition, should be 6000 — currently satisfied at turn ~1, a dead gate), `EXHAUSTION_FATIGUE_STRAIN_THRESHOLD = 80` in `formation_fatigue.ts` (should be 8000 — always true from turn 1), and `B1_HIGH_EXHAUSTION_THRESHOLD = 500` in `political_directive_producer.ts` (crossed ~turn 5 — every faction spends ~183 of 188 turns in a political-directive state meant to be rare). **Found by**: Gap-Finder (sole finder — this required reading across files no single lens-scoped specialist owned).
4. **A third exhaustion surface nobody was briefed on**: `corps_exhaustion` (0–100 scale, `commander_state.ts`, `corps_operation_readiness.ts`), which actually gates whether a corps will launch an operation (`MAX_EXHAUSTION_FOR_OPERATION = 30`). Any redesign that only touches the two surfaces named in the original briefing (`political.war_exhaustion` and `factions[].profile.exhaustion`) will not change corps-level operational behavior at all. **Found by**: Gap-Finder.

Additionally, two Engine Invariants §8 monotonicity violations exist today in `factions[].profile.exhaustion` writers (`src/sim/early_war/control_strain.ts:145` and `baseline_ops_scheduler.ts:60`, both floor at zero instead of at the previous value, meaning a negative delta can technically decrease exhaustion) — currently safe by a wide margin but unguarded, per Determinism Auditor and Technical Architect independently. Cheap, isolated, canon-required fixes regardless of any redesign.

## Part 2 — the §6 finding (highest priority)

Three specialists independently reached the same conclusion from different directions:

- **War-or-Game**: `computeWarCostIndex()` adds the atrocity penalty *to* the base index and clamps the result to 1.0. Since the base index is already 1.0 on any full campaign (see Part 3), the atrocity term has nothing left to move — it's mathematically absorbed by the clamp before it can do anything.
- **Game Designer**: confirmed the same mechanism independently, citing the code's own comment that atrocity is meant to make territorial gains from cleansing "NET-NEGATIVE at the verdict" — today it's net-*zero*, not negative. Flagged this explicitly as a `SENSITIVE_HISTORY_DESIGN_GATE` precondition on any further work in this area.
- **Canon Compliance Reviewer**: confirmed via canon text — §6 Non-Goal #3 ("no combination of war crimes + territory produces a better outcome than the same territory without them") holds only *vacuously* today, because nothing is distinguishable at saturation, not because atrocity is actually penalized. Ruled this §6-adjacent, requiring full Pyrrhic-panel sign-off (Historian + scenario-tester/calibration + Engine/systems + Red-team) for any de-saturation work — explicitly rejecting "this is just numeric retuning, lighter tier" as a framing, since there's no canon-stated intent for the current reference values to retune "inside."

**Consequence for sequencing**: no de-saturation work on either exhaustion or the cost-index references should proceed without a panel re-establishing atrocity dominance explicitly, ideally with the Red-team lens confirming the boundary. Several specialists (Gameplay Programmer, War-or-Game) independently warned against one specific trap: don't normalize casualty/duration references against historical baselines the same way you might normalize the *atrocity* term's references — the atrocity references (`war_crimes_full`, `refugees_full`, `civilian_casualties_full`) are deliberately set far below real historical magnitudes specifically so atrocity dominates; normalizing them to historical scale would weaken the bright line by roughly 8x.

## Part 3 — the canon conflict and the "why a third rescale can't work" proof

**Canon Compliance Reviewer's verdict**: yes, a real conflict, not a misreading. `VICTORY_AND_PYRRHIC_SCORING.md` states historical RBiH "barely earned survival" (canon-bound to grade A). But `A`, `A+`, and `B` are structurally unreachable on any canonical-length (~188-week) campaign — three of the engine's seven declared outcome classes are dead code. Separately, the entire `war_cost_index`/`capGradeByCost` mechanism that produces this is **undocumented in canon** — a formal STOP-AND-ASK under the reviewer's own protocol, independent of which numeric direction any fix takes.

**Railroad Hunter's verdict**: RAILROAD CONFIRMED, but precisely scoped — the exhaustion clamp itself is a legitimate bound (a monotonic accumulator needs one), not a railroad. The railroad is one layer up, in `scoring.ts`'s absolute reference ceilings. Proof that a third cap rescale cannot fix the *grade* even if it perfectly fixed exhaustion: `duration_full_weeks: 156` alone, at weight 0.2, is saturated by *any* run that reaches Dayton (~188 weeks) — that's an unconditional +0.2 no player action can avoid. Casualties (weight 0.4) saturate independently. Even setting the exhaustion sub-score to zero, the index still computes to at least 0.4(0)+0.4(1)+0.2(1) = 0.60, which caps at B — never better. A third rescale changes *when* the wall arrives, not *whether* it's there.

**Also found**: `classifyOutcome()` takes the composite `pyrrhic_score` — the one number that demonstrably varies across strategies (12.4 to 73.6 in this week's four RBiH runs) — as an explicitly unused, underscore-prefixed parameter. It's decorative by design, not merely underweighted.

## Part 4 — convergent engineering recommendation for exhaustion

Four specialists independently proposed the same shape, working blind to each other:

- **Systems Programmer**, **Technical Architect**, **Gameplay Programmer**, **Determinism Auditor** all recommend: keep the underlying accumulator **unbounded and monotonic** (satisfies Engine Invariants §8 by construction — a sum of non-negative deltas can never decrease, and there's no clamp to strip differentiation), and have **consumers** read a derived value through a **pure-arithmetic saturating curve**: `level = ceiling * raw / (raw + K)`. This is strictly increasing forever (so ordering between factions is preserved no matter how large `raw` grows — differentiation can be compressed but never destroyed), and uses only `+`, `*`, `/` — all exactly IEEE-754-defined, unlike `Math.exp`/`Math.pow`, which are implementation-approximated and not bit-reproducible across V8 versions. Given this project hashes full save state as its calibration floor, all four specialists flagged this as a load-bearing, non-negotiable determinism constraint, not a style preference. **Determinism Auditor recommends banning transcendental functions (`exp`/`pow`/`log`/`tanh`) in sim code at the same tier as the existing `Math.random()` ban.**

- **Canon Compliance Reviewer** independently confirmed this shape is not just acceptable but *more* canon-compliant than the current hard clamp: Engine Invariants §8 bullet 2 mandates exhaustion "must increase" under conditions (static fronts, supply strain) that persist for the whole war — a hard clamp violates this for roughly the back half of a full campaign (the increase stops while the driving conditions continue), while a strictly-increasing curve satisfies it continuously.

- **Game Designer** noted canon precedent already exists for exactly this "raw scar (irreversible) + derived readable gauge" pattern: §8 itself distinguishes "Control Strain is reversible; Exhaustion is irreversible" — a reversible companion signal alongside an irreversible accumulator is not a new invention, it's already a canon-blessed shape.

- **Determinism Auditor's one dissent, worth resolving before building anything on top of it**: the *original* pre-rescale convergence (all three factions agreeing to 14 decimal places, documented in the 2026-05-22 memo) is not fully explained by "everyone hit the same clamp" — it has two live candidate mechanisms in the code (a fallback branch that broadcasts one scalar to all factions, or the delta clip applying before any per-faction modifier). Recommends a cheap one-run instrumented test to distinguish which, before assuming any new formula avoids inheriting the same defect.

## Part 5 — what should actually drive exhaustion (historical grounding)

**Historian's core finding**: the current "static front count" driver has the causal mechanism backwards. BB-cited: holding a quiet, static front was historically the *cheap* posture (BB1 p.217, re: HVO); it was casualties, offensive activity, lack of rotation, and — critically — a documented, dated, faction-asymmetric collapse (VRS morale/desertion crisis from 1994, Belgrade's August 1994 blockade of the Bosnian Serbs specifically, ARBiH's *improving* trajectory through 1993-95 via professionalization) that actually drove exhaustion. The current formula taxes whoever holds the most map, which historically was the *side that could least afford it* (VRS held the most front with the fewest men, which is why it broke) — an inversion of the real mechanism. Proposes three concrete, historically-grounded directions: (A) exhaustion as a frontage-per-available-manpower ratio with a rotation/tour-length term, (B) patron fatigue as a primary, dated, faction-asymmetric driver (Belgrade Aug 1994, Zagreb's March 1994 Washington Agreement decision), (C) exhaustion expressed as "brittleness" — a shock/surprise cohesion-failure model rather than a flat combat-power tax, matching the documented VRS collapse pattern.

**Formation Expert's independent, concrete confirmation**: the historically-correct faction arcs (RS degrades, RBiH endures, HRHB overstretches) are *already authored* into the existing mobilization-surge curves, just never connected to exhaustion. Also found an already-computed-but-currently-discarded manpower-depletion ratio (`exhaustionRatio` in `ongoing_mobilization.ts`) that could feed exhaustion cheaply with no new tracking required. **Important caution, also from Formation Expert**: do NOT use brigade dissolution rate as an exhaustion input yet — per existing project memory, RS/ARBiH brigade destruction rates show an unexplained, un-root-caused asymmetry (0% ARBiH vs 61-63% RS), and wiring that into exhaustion would launder a known-broken signal into a second, harder-to-detect symptom layer.

**Gameplay Programmer's implementation-side finding, converging with the above**: the casualty-ceiling saturation (RBiH measured 4-5x over reference) traces to a genuine units bug, not just a bad number — the reference was set against KIA-only historical figures while the code sums KIA+WIA+MIA. A corrected reference derived from the already-committed `data/reference/historical_baseline.json` (which has real per-faction KIA and war-duration figures) would fix the casualty and duration sub-scores without inventing new numbers.

## Part 6 — scoring: relative references, not another absolute rescale

Convergent across Modern Wargame Expert (citing HoI4/Victoria 3/Twilight Struggle/COIN-series precedent for relative/rate-based war-weariness signals over absolute ones), Railroad Hunter (proposing cost measured against a passive-play counterfactual baseline — which this week's runs already produced as a byproduct), and Gameplay Programmer (proposing per-faction references derived from `historical_baseline.json`): **stop comparing accrued cost against a fixed absolute ceiling; compare it against a reference tied to the specific war/scenario being played.** This tracks scenario length and faction scale automatically rather than requiring a fourth manual rescale down the line. Formally excludes the atrocity term's references from this treatment (Part 2).

## Part 7 — verdict screen / player-facing framing

Strong three-way convergence (Architect, Narrative Designer, Modern Wargame Expert) on **not just fixing the number but restructuring what's shown**:

- Stop displaying one hero number (`pyrrhic_score`) with the letter grade as a subordinate pill. Show the cost-cap mechanism explicitly rather than burying it in a description-string footnote — right now it exists only as `"...capped by war cost (index 1.00)"` in small italic text.
- Narrative Designer's correction to the brainstorm's own framing: the cap isn't producing pure "always C" — the anchors-based earned grade for the four measured runs was closer to B, and the cap knocks it down one step. That's a materially different, more defensible mechanic than "the grade never moves at all," and the fix should make that visible (e.g. "Earned: B / Capped by war cost: C"), not necessarily loosen the cap.
- Multiple specialists independently recommend surfacing the cost trajectory *during play*, not just at the verdict — Architect and Modern Wargame Expert both cite this as the highest-leverage, lowest-risk (pure UI, zero calibration exposure) change on the table, likening it to Twilight Struggle's always-visible DEFCON track.
- Narrative Designer's strongest point: if exhaustion becomes genuinely differentiated, it unlocks a real, currently-unwritable thesis line — "you did not outfight them, you outlasted them" — and explicitly warns against turning exhaustion into a player-manageable/recoverable resource in the process, citing canon §5.4 ("anything optimizable becomes a score") as a hard constraint distinct from the engineering fix.

## Part 8 — process, sequencing, and the unresolved tension

**Product Manager**, after correcting two stale premises (the "collapse before D2" roadmap sequencing is superseded by a current R1-R9 register; the collapse pipeline is deliberately flag-gated off by design, not broken/unfinished), recommends: this work already has two open items in the current R6 calibration plan — no new roadmap lane needed. Proposes shipping a scoring-only "relative re-read" fix first (Phase 1: cheap, provably calibration-flat since scoring never writes state), with the exhaustion model-shape fix scoped as a serialized Phase 2 behind current anchor-recovery work.

**This is directly contested by two other specialists**: Gap-Finder and Canon Compliance Reviewer both independently argue exhaustion and scoring cannot actually be validated as separable, because canon (`WAR_TERMINATION_SPEC`) formally routes exhaustion into verdict grading, and mechanically, duration alone (weight 0.2) already floors the index at a B-cap regardless of exhaustion — so a scoring-only fix shipped alone will still show `C` on every full campaign and read as having failed, when the actual problem (exhaustion's dead 0.4 weight) was never touched. Gap-Finder explicitly recommends treating exhaustion-differentiation and the cost-reference retune as one indivisible lane, using the existing owner-approved flag+attribution-matrix pattern (`bundle_and_track_calibration_workflow`) rather than PM's proposed serial split.

**Resolved empirically (2026-08-06, post-synthesis)**: re-scored the four already-collected RBiH final-state saves offline (scoring is a pure read, per Scenario Harness Engineer's finding — zero cost, zero risk to test) under four reference-fix scenarios. Fixing any *single* reference alone (exhaustion, casualties, or duration) leaves every one of the four strategies capped at C — confirming Gap-Finder's "will read as inert" warning empirically, not just algebraically. But fixing all three references *together* — still purely a `scoring.ts` change, no exhaustion-accumulator redesign required — moves `baseline_historical` to A and the three activist strategies to B. So the real requirement is narrower than either side's framing: **the three `COST_REFERENCE` values must move together as one indivisible scoring-layer lane**, but that lane does *not* require the exhaustion accumulator redesign to land first. Both Product Manager (scoring is separable from exhaustion-accumulator work, zero calibration risk) and Gap-Finder (a narrow single-reference fix reads as inert) were correct about different halves of the same fact.

**Scenario Harness Engineer's process finding, independent of the above**: the 2026-05-22 fix was never actually validated against its own stated success criteria — it shipped inside a 17-commit bundle, no 40w calibration run was recorded for it despite the memo demanding one, and the resulting saturation was later observed and logged in the project ledger as a "guardrail PASS" (i.e., mistaken for healthy behavior). Also corrects the current 188w floor to 630 (not 649, which is ~2 months stale), and confirms `tools/calibration_bundle_matrix.ts` — referenced in project memory as available — does not actually exist yet.

**QA Engineer's concrete gate proposal** (the most actionable single artifact from this brainstorm): a 5-metric trajectory gate — first-saturation week, % of campaign with zero exhaustion variance across factions, terminal pairwise minimum gap, count of cost sub-scores pinned at exactly 1.0, and cross-strategy `war_cost_index` spread — built on the existing `engine_health_gate.cjs` pattern, computable today from data (`weekly_report.jsonl`) that's already emitted in every run. All five currently fail. Also found the existing unit test `tests/free_war_verdict_cost_floor.test.ts` explicitly asserts `costIndex ≈ 1.0` as *correct* behavior — that test pins the bug and must be rewritten as part of any fix, or the bug will simply be re-blessed a third time.

## Part 9 — governance requirement

**Canon Compliance Reviewer's explicit verdict**: full Pyrrhic panel required (Historian + scenario-tester/calibration + Engine/systems + Red-team), unanimous GO as signature, implementer ≠ reviewer, per CLAUDE.md's standing rule for §6-adjacent and canon-silent work. Sequence: panel decision first, then new canon text (a new §3.5 in `VICTORY_AND_PYRRHIC_SCORING.md` documenting the cost-cap mechanism explicitly, a clause in Engine Invariants §8 ruling out hard clamps, and promotion of the still-open `FORAWWV.md` H1.10 addendum on exhaustion bounds), then — only after canon is written — numeric implementation. Both prior attempts set numbers before canon existed to check them against, and both saturated.

## Explicitly out of scope for any near-term work (per convergent specialist caution)

- A third "just raise the ceiling" rescale of any kind — proven both mechanically insufficient (Part 1, Part 4) and grade-mathematically incapable of working (Part 3).
- Making exhaustion a player-recoverable/optimizable resource (Narrative Designer, canon §5.4).
- Normalizing the atrocity term's references to historical scale (multiple specialists, Part 2).
- Wiring brigade dissolution rate into exhaustion before the existing RS/ARBiH destruction asymmetry is root-caused (Formation Expert).
- Any scoring framing resembling "efficiency" or "cost-per-objective" — this trips an existing hard-enforced §6 guard (`RING_3_REFUSED_FLAGS` includes `pyrrhic_score_inverted`/`atrocity_efficiency`, found by Gap-Finder) that was previously undocumented in any brief on this topic.
- Enabling the Phase 3C collapse-gating flag before an exhaustion fix lands — its thresholds are read against the current (saturated) scale, and every faction is currently above all three collapse-eligibility thresholds simultaneously; flipping the flag first would make collapse fire for everyone at once and look like a cascading engine failure rather than a calibration issue (Game Designer, Product Manager, Technical Architect all independently flagged this sequencing hazard).
