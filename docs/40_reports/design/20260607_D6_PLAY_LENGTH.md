## The question

What play-length / session model does 1.0 ship — a single full campaign, or also quick-battle and
speed modes? And what session expectation should be set for players?

## The adopted ruling — Option A

**1.0 ships a single full April-1992 campaign.** Quick-battle and speed modes are **deferred to the
post-1.0 historical-scenario-starts lane** (the April 1993 / 1994 / 1995 alternate starts). A
**3-5 hour target session expectation** is set, tied to the existing onboarding/tutorial lane.

## Rationale

- **Focus the 1.0 experience.** One coherent, well-tuned full campaign (the April-1992 start) is a
  stronger, more honest 1.0 than a thinner spread of half-finished modes.
- **The natural home for short modes already exists.** The post-1.0 historical-scenario-starts lane
  (Apr 1993 / 1994 / 1995) is exactly where quick-battle and speed modes belong — those later
  starts already produce shorter, more contained runs, so the work composes there instead of being
  invented for 1.0.
- **Expectation-setting.** A 3-5 hour target session, surfaced through the existing
  onboarding/tutorial lane, gives players an accurate up-front sense of commitment without adding a
  new mode surface.
- **Calibration.** Zero impact — this is a scope/expectation ruling, not a behavior change. The
  calibration scenarios (40w / 52w / 188w) are unaffected.

## What this forecloses (for 1.0)

- No quick-battle mode in 1.0.
- No speed/short-session mode in 1.0.
- No alternate historical-scenario starts (Apr 1993 / 1994 / 1995) in 1.0 — those open the
  post-1.0 lane.

## Deferral note (post-1.0 / historical-scenario-starts lane)

Quick-battle and speed modes are deferred to the post-1.0 historical-scenario-starts lane:

1. The April 1993 / 1994 / 1995 starts are the substrate for shorter, contained sessions.
2. Quick/speed modes are designed there, not retrofitted onto the 1992 full campaign.
3. The 3-5 hour target expectation for the 1.0 full campaign stays owned by the onboarding/tutorial
   lane.
