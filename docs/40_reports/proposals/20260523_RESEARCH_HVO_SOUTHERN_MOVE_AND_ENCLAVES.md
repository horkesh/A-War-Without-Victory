## 1. Operation Southern Move (Južni Potez), 8–11 October 1995

### 1.1 Strategic context

Southern Move was the **final HV/HVO offensive of the Bosnian War**. It launched four days before the country-wide ceasefire of 12 October 1995 — a sequencing not coincidental. The operation served three concurrent purposes:

1. **Relieve ARBiH 5th Corps at Ključ** — ARBiH 5th Corps requested HV ground rescue (the only such request of the war) as VRS pressure on the Ključ axis threatened to unravel ARBiH gains from Operation Sana.
2. **Threaten Banja Luka without entering it** — per the Holbrooke-Tuđman understanding of 17 September, Banja Luka itself was a halt line, but credible threat to it would push Pale to Dayton.
3. **Lock in the territorial map at 51:49** — Tuđman's 19 September Split speech publicly committed Croatia to the Contact Group ratio; ground gains taken after that date had to fit inside the formula or be abandoned at Dayton.

The operation closed on 11 October. The Bosnia-wide ceasefire took effect at 00:01 on 12 October. The pause is mechanical: HV/HVO took everything they intended to keep, then stopped.

### 1.2 Order of Battle — HV/HVO offensive force

Commander: **Major General Ante Gotovina** (also commander of Mistral 2 / Southern Move dual chain).
Total HV/HVO strength: **~11,000–12,000 troops** organized in two operational groups.

| Unit | Force | Role / sector |
|---|---|---|
| **HV 4th Guards Brigade** | HV | Main effort, southwest of Mrkonjić Grad; broke through 3rd Serbian Brigade on 10 Oct; advanced onto Manjača |
| **HV 7th Guards Brigade** | HV | Southeast axis, opposite VRS 7th Motorised Brigade (Liskovica, Bjelajci, Šehovci villages) |
| **HV 1st Croatian Guards Brigade (1st HGZ)** | HV | Diversionary/supporting role with 126th HGR; relieved by HVO 3rd Guards on 11 Oct |
| **HV 126th Home Guard Regiment** | HV | Diversionary attack 8 Oct from 15–20 km west of Mrkonjić Grad |
| **HV General Staff Reconnaissance Sabotage Company** | HV | Recon/forward |
| **HVO 1st Guards Brigade** | HVO | Diversionary attack 8 Oct (with HV 126th HGR); took over northward advance from 4th GdB on 11 Oct; captured Bočac HPP |
| **HVO 2nd Guards Brigade** | HVO | Right flank of HV 7th GdB; advanced toward Bočac with HVO 1st GdB on 11 Oct |
| **HVO 3rd Guards Brigade** | HVO | Reserve; relieved 1st HGZ and 126th HGR on 11 Oct |
| **HVO 60th Guards Airborne Battalion** | HVO | Special force, attached |
| **HVO 22nd Sabotage Detachment** | HVO | Recon/sabotage |
| **HVO Herzeg-Bosnia MoI Special Police** | HVO | Attached |

**Key observation:** the HVO commitment was the same three Guards Brigades (1st/2nd/3rd) that spearheaded the late phase of Operation Mistral 2. They had not redeployed back to western Herzegovina between operations — they remained attached to Gotovina's task force.

### 1.3 Order of Battle — VRS defenders

VRS command: **Colonel Milenko Lazić**, Operational Group-2 of the 30th Infantry Division (1st Krajina Corps).
Total VRS strength on this front: **~5,500 troops**.

| Unit | Sector |
|---|---|
| **VRS 7th Kupres-Šipovo Motorised Brigade** | Main defensive effort — Liskovica/Bjelajci/Šehovci southeast of Mrkonjić Grad |
| **VRS 3rd Serbian Brigade** | Southwest of Mrkonjić Grad — broken by HV 4th GdB on 10 Oct |
| **VRS 11th Mrkonjić Light Infantry Brigade** | Mrkonjić Grad town itself / local reserve |

Force ratio: roughly **2.1 : 1** in HV/HVO favor, with overwhelming asymmetry in heavy weapons and operational tempo.

### 1.4 Day-by-day sequence

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **7 Oct** | US Air Force strikes near Banja Luka in preparation (per Brig Ante Kotromanović statement) — battlespace shaping |
| **8 Oct** | HV 126th HGR + HVO 1st GdB launch diversionary attack from 15–20 km west of Mrkonjić Grad. Poor weather (fog) limits artillery. |
| **9 Oct** | Main HV assault — 4th and 7th GdB attack. Start time slipped 6:00 → 8:50 due to fog. ~5 km advance. Town hit by ~200 artillery shells. |
| **10 Oct** | **HV 4th GdB breaks through** 3rd Serbian Brigade and outflanks VRS 7th Motorised. **Mrkonjić Grad falls.** HVO captures Podrašnica Valley and Čađavica junction. |
| **11 Oct** | HVO units relieve HV. HVO 1st/2nd GdB advance north, **capture Bočac Hydroelectric Power Station**. HV 4th GdB on **Manjača Mountain**, 25 km south of Banja Luka. |
| **12 Oct** | Country-wide ceasefire takes effect. |

**Net territorial result:** ~40 km wide × 20 km deep pocket north of Mrkonjić Grad, including Bočac HPP and Manjača crest. Bočac was "the last significant source of electricity under VRS control in western Bosnia."

The repo's OSID schema is `op:municipality:slug`. Without direct settlement-list inspection here, plausible targets:

- `op:mrkonjic_grad:mrkonjic_grad_2` — town centre (objective 1)
- `op:mrkonjic_grad:bocac_2` — Bočac HPP (objective 3) — verify exists; if not, nearest settlement OSID on the Vrbas downstream of the dam
- `op:mrkonjic_grad:podrasnica_*` or eastward — Podrašnica Valley axis
- `op:mrkonjic_grad:cadjavica_*` — Čađavica junction

- Approach line: from previously-captured **Šipovo** (taken in Mistral 2 on 12 Sept by HVO 1st GdB) — Šipovo → Mrkonjić Grad axis is the operational corridor

### 1.6 Engine corps launcher — recommendation

Both Mistral 2 and Southern Move were Gotovina-commanded HV/HVO joint operations originating from the Tomislavgrad/Livno staging area and pushing north through Glamoč → Šipovo → Mrkonjić Grad. The HVO brigades involved (1st/2nd/3rd Guards) are western-Herzegovina-raised but operating in the Tomislavgrad operational zone for the offensive.

**Launcher recommendation:** `hvo_tomislavgrad` (consistent with Mistral 2 ordering in the catalog draft).

**Not** `hvo_southeast_herzegovina` (which covers Mostar / Stolac / Čapljina far to the south).
**Not** `hvo_southwest_herzegovina` (which is the political home of the brigades but not the operational corps for fall 1995 — see §5).

### 1.7 Casualties / war crimes — Mrkonjić Grad mass grave

- Bosnian Serb sources cite **480 dead/missing** in the Mrkonjić Grad fighting overall.
- **181 bodies exhumed** in March/April 1996 from the Serbian Orthodox cemetery: **81 civilians, 97 RS soldiers, 3 police**; ages 22–90.
- Forensic finding: **102 had smashed skulls, fractures, and close-range shots to the back of the head**; 4 decapitated. The pattern indicates execution, not combat.
- Killings dated **11–12 October 1995** (i.e., after the town fell, possibly straddling the ceasefire).
- Allegations centre on HV 4th Guards Brigade soldiers.
- ICTY received the file but **declined to pursue** — returned material to RS authorities in 2006.

**Engine implication:** if the engine has a war-crime or atrocity event surface, Southern Move closure (turn ~182) is a candidate event-trigger location. Not proposed here.

### 1.8 Why it was halted

- **Tuđman 19 Sept Split speech** publicly committed Croatia to 51:49 — going beyond Banja Luka's approaches would have broken that commitment.
- **Holbrooke 17 Sept Zagreb meeting** (with Susak/Tuđman, via Wesley Clark) — explicit "stop short of Banja Luka." Susak resisted initially, then conceded.
- **Tuđman's own Eastern Slavonia calculus** — peaceful Slavonia reintegration depended on Milošević cooperation, which depended on a Dayton settlement, which required not embarrassing the Bosnian Serb leadership by taking Banja Luka.
- Ceasefire mechanically halted the offensive on 12 October.

## 2. Operation Una, 18–19 September 1995 — NEGATIVE CONTROL

### 2.1 Why this is a negative control

Una is the same political actor (HV), broadly the same window (one month earlier), the same strategic objective (threaten Banja Luka from the north), the same diplomatic patron (Holbrooke), and the same Croatian command echelon. It **failed in 48 hours with zero territorial gain and ~50 KIA**. The difference between Una and Mistral 2 / Southern Move is therefore a clean isolation of **what makes an HV/HVO operation succeed or fail**.

If the engine outputs an Una-shaped operation that succeeds, the combat model is railroading. If the engine refuses to launch Una-shaped operations at all, the model is over-fitted. The intended behavior is: **Una launches, fails fast, abortion logic kicks in within ~2 turns**.

### 2.2 Order of Battle — HV attacker (no HVO)

Total ~1,500 spearhead + ~2,500 reserve infantry.

| Unit | Strength | Sector |
|---|---|---|
| **HV 1st Guards Brigade** | ~750 | Zagreb Corps area; Novi Grad / Bosanska Kostajnica crossings |
| **HV 2nd Guards Brigade** | ~750 | Hrvatska Dubica area; Bosanska Dubica crossing |
| **HV 17th Home Guard Regiment** | — | Una River support |
| **HV Bjelovar Corps** (52nd, 121st, 125th HGR) | ~2,500 reserve infantry | Sava River — Jasenovac crossing |
| **HV 16th Artillery-Rocket Brigade** (1 battalion 155mm) | — | Fires |
| **HV 18th Artillery Battalion** | — | Fires |
| **HV 34th Engineer Battalion** | — | River-crossing (arrived late) |
| **HV GS Reconnaissance Sabotage Company** | — | Recon |

**No HVO units committed.** This is essential — Una was an HV-only operation against the Sava/Una line, with no Federation coordination, no ARBiH overlap on the south bank, and no HVO Guards Brigades available (they were already attached to Mistral 2 to the south).

### 2.3 Order of Battle — VRS defender

Command: **1st Krajina Corps, Maj Gen Momir Talić** (later LtGen) — the largest, most experienced VRS corps, HQ Banja Luka.

| Unit | Sector |
|---|---|
| **VRS 1st Novigrad (Novi Grad) Infantry Brigade** | Una River — Novi Grad |
| **VRS 11th Dubica Infantry Brigade** | Una River — Bosanska Dubica |
| **VRS 1st Gradiška Light Infantry Brigade** | Sava River — Jasenovac |
| **Serb Volunteer Guard** (Arkan's Tigers attached) | Reinforcement |
| Local TO + police auxiliaries | Bunker line, minefields, anti-tank obstacles |

**Force ratio:** approximately even or slightly favoring HV in raw count, but VRS in **prepared defenses on a major river barrier** with full minefields and artillery dialed in. Combat effectiveness ratio sharply favored VRS.

### 2.4 Crossing points and objectives

| Crossing | River | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Novi Grad | Una | Small bridgehead, pinned, withdrawn |
| Bosanska Dubica | Una | Small bridgehead, pinned, withdrawn |
| Bosanska Kostajnica | Una | Small bridgehead, pinned, withdrawn |
| Jasenovac | Sava | Failed crossing under mortar fire |

Strategic objective: seize **Mount Prosara** to enable advance toward Prijedor and then Banja Luka from the north.

### 2.5 Failure factors — diagnostic for the engine

1. **10-hour planning window.** Order drafted 17 Sept after Holbrooke meeting; attack launched ~24 hours later. No staging, no rehearsal, no force concentration.
2. **Faulty intelligence.** HV General Staff had assessed VRS 2nd Corps "completely destroyed" by ARBiH gains. ARBiH advance toward Novi Grad **halted on 18–19 September** when ARBiH encountered "14,000 previously undetected VRS troops" plus 2,000 from Serbia.
3. **No tactical surprise.** Crossings predictable to a river-line defender; VRS minefields and artillery pre-registered.
4. **Engineer assets late.** Tugboats, amphibious vehicles arrived at noon 18 Sept — after the artillery prep was already underway.
5. **River flood conditions.** High water, strong current, floating debris. One tugboat fire (faulty wiring) compounded the chaos. Engineer-boat crews refused crossings late on 19 Sept.
6. **No NATO air support.** NATO Operation Deliberate Force was **suspended** on 14 Sept (and formally terminated 20 Sept). Una launched 18 Sept during the suspension — no NATO aircraft over the battlespace, in contrast to Mistral 2 which benefited from the active campaign.
7. **No coordination with ARBiH** on the south bank. (Mistral 2 was HV+HVO jointly committed; Southern Move was HV+HVO with ARBiH 5th Corps pressing from the south. Una had neither.)

### 2.6 Casualties

| Source | KIA | WIA |
|---|---|---|
| Official HV | 49 | 180 |
| Gen. Janko Bobetko's estimate | 70 | 250 |
| Bjelovar Corps (subset) | 5 | 26 |
| VRS military | 44 KIA, 53 WIA, 3 captured | — |
| VRS police | 6 KIA, 9 WIA | — |
| Serb civilians | 54 KIA, 39 WIA, 6 captured | — |
| Danish UNCRO | 2 KIA (Dvor shelling) | — |

**Loss exchange ratio:** HV took ~50 KIA for **zero territorial gain** vs ~44 VRS military KIA. Worse than 1:1 against a prepared defender across a river — diagnostic of "do not launch this operation."

### 2.7 Aftermath — accountability

- **Maj Gen Vinko Vrbanac** blamed; career ended.
- **Chief of Staff Zvonimir Červenko** protested the hasty launch — was not informed in advance.
- **Gen Pavao Miljavac** replaced expected successor Mareković.
- In 2006 Croatian authorities investigated **40 alleged civilian killings in Bosanska Dubica area** by 1st Battalion / HV 2nd Guards Brigade.
- Croatian press "brushed aside as an aberration" within the year's successful campaign.
- Croatia **never reattempted river-crossing strategy** — pivoted to the Mrkonjić Grad approach via Southern Move.

### 2.8 Mistral 2 vs Una vs Southern Move — the discriminating variables

| Variable | Mistral 2 (Sept 8–17) | Una (Sept 18–19) | Southern Move (Oct 8–11) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Outcome** | Success — Jajce, Šipovo, Drvar, 2500 km² | **Failure** — 0 km², ~50 KIA | Success — Mrkonjić Grad, Bočac, Manjača |
| HV-HVO joint? | Yes | No (HV-only) | Yes |
| Planning window | Weeks | ~10 hours | ~3 weeks |
| Terrain | Mountain/forest | River barrier | Mountain/forest |
| Defender state | VRS 2KK fractured | VRS 1KK intact, prepared | VRS OG-2 weakened by Mistral 2 |
| NATO air support | Active (Deliberate Force) | Ended 14 Sept | Tactical US strikes 7 Oct |
| ARBiH coordination | Adjacent (5KK) | None | 5KK at Ključ, formally requested |
| Force ratio | ~2:1 attacker | ~1:1 attacker, but defender prepared | ~2.1:1 attacker |
| Engineer prep | Full | Late | Full |
| Outcome verdict | Doctrine-correct | Doctrine-violating launch | Doctrine-correct |

### 2.9 Engine implication — negative control test

If the simulation can be made to replicate:
- Mistral 2 success in week ~175
- Una failure in week ~177
- Southern Move success in week ~182

…using the **same** HV/HVO force structure with **only** the situational inputs (planning time, river crossing, NATO air, ARBiH coordination, force prep) varying, then the combat predictor (`checkLaunchFeasibility`) and operation launch logic are correctly modeling commitment risk vs preparation.

The current diagnostic in the engine health audit (`P1 COMBAT-P14`) — that the combat predictor ignores defender artillery/terrain/entrenchment — would predict a **false success** on Una. That is the failure mode to instrument against.

## 3. HVO Central Bosnia Enclaves 1994–1995

### 3.1 State after Washington Agreement (18 March 1994)

The Washington Agreement ended the Croat-Bosniak War with the territorial map essentially frozen. Central Bosnia HVO held a set of isolated, non-contiguous enclaves surrounded by ARBiH 3rd Corps territory.

| Enclave | Brigades | OZ designation | Engine corps mapping |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vitez** (Lašva valley centre, with Stari Vitez Bosniak pocket inside) | Viteška Brigade, Stjepan Tomašević Brigade (Novi Travnik HQ) | Central Bosnia OZ | `hvo_central_bosnia` |
| **Kiseljak** (south of Lašva, separate from Vitez) | 94th Brigade (Kiseljak), Kiseljak Brigade | Central Bosnia OZ | `hvo_central_bosnia` |
| **Kreševo–Fojnica** (immediately south of Kiseljak) | 95th Brigade (Kreševo), Kreševo Brigade | Central Bosnia OZ | `hvo_central_bosnia` |
| **Busovača** (between Vitez and Kiseljak) | Jure Francetić Brigade | Central Bosnia OZ | `hvo_central_bosnia` |
| **Žepče** | 111th Žepče Brigade ("XP") | Žepče OZ (semi-detached) | possibly `hvo_central_bosnia` or `hvo_north_bosnia` (verify) |
| **Usora** | 110th Usora Brigade (under ARBiH 2nd Corps command nominally) | Northwest Bosnia / ARBiH 2nd | likely `hvo_north_bosnia` |
| **Vareš** | Bobovac Brigade — **already lost November 1993** | — | not applicable to 1994–1995 |

### 3.2 Vareš loss (November 1993, pre-Washington)

For completeness — Vareš fell to ARBiH **before** Washington:
- 23 Oct 1993 — Stupni Do massacre triggered ARBiH 2nd Corps offensive
- 2 Nov 1993 — ECMM reported ABiH 2nd Corps attack
- 3 Nov 1993 — HVO civilians and soldiers abandoned Vareš, fled to Kiseljak. Bobovac Brigade HQ burned.
- 4 Nov 1993 — ARBiH 7th Muslim Mountain Brigade and 206th Zvornik Brigade entered the town.

By 1994–1995 there is no HVO presence in Vareš. The Bobovac Brigade no longer exists as a field formation.

### 3.3 1994–1995 activity — what the central Bosnia enclaves did

After Washington, the central Bosnia HVO enclaves did **not** participate in the western Federation offensive (Mistral 2, Southern Move, etc.). The Guards Brigades available for offensive use were the 1st/2nd/3rd, all based in western Herzegovina, mobilized northward into Gotovina's task force (see §5). Central Bosnia HVO performed a **defensive consolidation** role only:

1. **Reintegration into Federation command** — Federation Military Council (Vrhovni vojni savjet) established formal joint command, but in practice central Bosnia HVO and ARBiH 3rd Corps operated parallel chains in their respective enclaves/territories with minimal joint planning.
2. **Garrison and pacification** — Vitez/Busovača/Kiseljak HVO held their lines, demobilized partially, and maintained brigade structures at reduced readiness.
3. **No offensive operations.** The central Bosnia HVO did not launch operations against VRS in 1994–1995. The Lašva–Kiseljak corridor faced ARBiH 3rd Corps territory, not VRS, so there was no available VRS target.
4. **Stari Vitez (Mahala) Bosniak pocket** — siege ended with Washington Agreement.
5. **Kiseljak / Vitez separation** — physically separate enclaves; no HVO operation to link them in 1994–1995. (BB Vol. II notes the linking-up plan was abandoned for resource reasons in late 1993.)

### 3.4 Žepče enclave — special case

Žepče sat between ARBiH 2nd Corps (Tuzla) and ARBiH 3rd Corps (Zenica), surrounded but with informal VRS commercial-and-arms ties northward to RS-held Doboj. After Washington:
- The 111th Žepče "XP" Brigade remained in place, formally Federation-aligned.
- Žepče is the only HVO enclave with a direct VRS front (north toward Doboj).
- No documented HVO offensive from Žepče in 1994–1995. Defensive consolidation only.

### 3.5 Usora enclave — special case

The 110th Usora Brigade operated under ARBiH 2nd Corps tactical command from 1993 onward (an unusual subordination predating Washington). It defended the Usora valley against VRS pressure from the Doboj salient. No HVO-initiated offensive 1994–1995; it functioned essentially as an ARBiH brigade with HVO insignia.

### 3.6 Implication for `hvo_central_bosnia` engine corps

For the 1994–1995 window, `hvo_central_bosnia` should:
- **Not** generate offensive operations during fall 1995.
- Hold garrison/defensive posture across the Vitez/Busovača/Kiseljak/Kreševo–Fojnica enclaves.
- Remain isolated through Dayton — no land bridge to western Herzegovina or to ARBiH 3rd Corps.
- Have 0 contribution to Mistral 2 / Southern Move OOB.

The previously-noted HRHB-war remaining-P1s ("Central Bosnia brigade redistribution — 5/6 sectors empty," "CB operations not launching," "Kiseljak/Vitez pocket separation") are **historically correct**: the enclaves were sparse, defensive, and structurally separated. The engine should not be calibrated to make CB launch ops in this window.

## 4. HVO Mostar Reintegration 1994–1995

### 4.1 State after Washington

- **East Mostar siege lifted** — ARBiH 4th Corps controlled East Mostar; HVO controlled West Mostar (right bank of the Neretva).
- Federation Military Council established with joint command nominally.
- EU administration of Mostar from 1994 (EU Administration Mostar, EUAM) — civilian.
- Stari Most (Old Bridge) destroyed 9 Nov 1993 by HVO — not rebuilt until 2004.

### 4.2 HVO operations / activity 1994–1995 — Southeast Herzegovina OZ

The HVO Southeast Herzegovina OZ (Mostar-based, brigades listed in §1 of HVO_FULL_BRIGADE_LIST: 1st Mostar Bde, 2nd Mostar Bde, Mostar Bde, Široki Brijeg Bde, Ljubuški Bde, Grude Bde, Čitluk Bde, Čapljina Bde, Knez Domagoj Bde) had **no offensive operations** in 1994–1995:

1. Mostar was demilitarized de facto under EUAM oversight; no front line.
2. HVO Mostar units maintained garrison.
3. No VRS contact in southeast Herzegovina — the closest VRS positions were east of Stolac toward Nevesinje, but no offensive was launched in this period.
4. Some southeast Herzegovina troops attached north to Gotovina's task force for Mistral 2 (via the 22nd Sabotage Detachment and special police).

**Engine implication:** `hvo_southeast_herzegovina` should also have minimal-to-zero offensive activity in fall 1995. Garrison posture.

## 5. Western Herzegovina Guards Brigades — Absent in Fall 1995

### 5.1 The 1st / 2nd / 3rd HVO Guards Brigade attachment

Per the order of battle reconstruction in §1.2:

- **HVO 1st Guards Brigade** — committed to Mistral 2 (captured Šipovo 12 Sept) → committed to Southern Move (Bočac HPP 11 Oct)
- **HVO 2nd Guards Brigade** — committed to Mistral 2 (entered Jajce 13 Sept) → committed to Southern Move (Bočac axis 11 Oct)
- **HVO 3rd Guards Brigade** — committed to Mistral 2 → committed to Southern Move (relieved HV 1st HGZ on 11 Oct)
- (HVO 4th Guards Brigade — formed under Roso's 1993 reorganization; less documented in Mistral 2/Southern Move OOB; likely reserve / training cadre)

These three brigades were the **professional, full-time, heavy-weapons** core of the HVO. They were the only HVO units routinely committed to operational maneuver. In fall 1995 they were continuously deployed forward under Gotovina's task force from approximately **early August (Operation Summer '95 north of Tomislavgrad, 5 Aug 1995)** through **11 October**.

### 5.2 Engine implication — western Herzegovina HVO corps

The political home of these brigades is **western Herzegovina** (Široki Brijeg, Tomislavgrad, Posušje area). In the engine schema this maps to:
- `hvo_southwest_herzegovina` (political home)
- `hvo_tomislavgrad` (operational staging)

But for fall 1995 the brigades are **physically attached north** to Gotovina's task force operating in Glamoč / Šipovo / Mrkonjić Grad. They are not available for any operation launched from `hvo_southwest_herzegovina`. They are available **only** to the Mistral 2 / Southern Move task force.

This is the **HV expeditionary ghost** problem in mirror image — the HVO Guards Brigades are "ghosted out" of their home corps for the duration of the fall offensive and "ghosted in" to the joint task force. The companion design doc `20260523_HV_EXPEDITIONARY_GHOST_DESIGN.md` handles the HV side of this; the HVO side has the same structural problem.

### 5.3 What's left in western Herzegovina

After the Guards Brigades depart north, what remains in `hvo_southwest_herzegovina` and `hvo_tomislavgrad` for fall 1995:

- Local territorial brigades (Široki Brijeg, Ljubuški, Grude, Čitluk, Čapljina, Knez Domagoj, Kralj Tomislav) — garrison.
- No offensive capability against any active VRS or ARBiH front (the only active front in the immediate vicinity is the Mostar line under EUAM — frozen).

**Engine implication:** these corps should also be minimal/zero offensive activity in fall 1995. The garrison-posture default is historically correct.
