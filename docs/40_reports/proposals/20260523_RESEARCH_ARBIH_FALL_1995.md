**Date:** 2026-05-23

## 0. Executive Summary

Between **4 August 1995** (start of Croatian Operation *Oluja*) and **12 October 1995** (the Bosnia-wide ceasefire that froze the front), the strategic situation in Bosnia and Herzegovina reversed faster than at any other point of the war. The Republika Srpska Army (VRS) lost roughly **a quarter of all territory it had ever held** — falling from about 70% of BiH to roughly 49–51% — primarily in western and central Bosnia. The three principal drivers were:

1. **The collapse of the Republic of Serbian Krajina (RSK)** in Croatia (Operation *Oluja*, 4–7 August 1995), which (a) destroyed the SVK (Srpska Vojska Krajine, Krajina Serb Army) that had pinned ARBiH 5th Corps from the rear inside the Bihać pocket for three years; (b) put the HV (Hrvatska Vojska, Croatian Army) on the BiH border with intact mechanised forces; (c) flooded ~150,000–200,000 Serb refugees through western RS, collapsing rear-area morale and logistics.
2. **NATO Operation *Deliberate Force*** (30 August – 20 September 1995), triggered by the Markale II shelling (28 August 1995). 3,515 sorties; ~1,026 munitions dropped on VRS C2, ammunition storage, IADS, and bridges around the heavy-weapons exclusion zones (Sarajevo, Goražde, Tuzla). Combined with simultaneous artillery strikes by the UK/French Rapid Reaction Force (RRF) from Mt. Igman, this gutted VRS Main Staff communications and forward fire support precisely when the federation ground offensives kicked off.
3. **The ARBiH January 1995 reorganisation** from Operational Groups into seven Corps plus subordinate Divisions, the **Washington Agreement** (18 March 1994) creating the Federation and the joint VOPF (Vrhovno Operativno Politicki Forum) coordination with HVO, and the maturing of ARBiH manpower — by mid-1995 the ARBiH fielded roughly **200,000–230,000 men under arms** versus a VRS that had bled below **~80,000 effectives** with severe ammunition shortage.

The principal ARBiH operations of the period were:

| Op | Faction | Dates | Area | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sana 95** | ARBiH 5th + 7th Corps | 13 Sept – 11 Oct 1995 | Bihać → Sanski Most / Ključ / Bos. Petrovac / Bos. Krupa | ~3,500–4,000 km² captured |
| **Una 95** | ARBiH 5th Corps (505th + supporting bdes) | mid–late Sept 1995 | Una valley north of Bihać (Bos. Krupa, Otoka, Bos. Novi axis) | Krupa town captured, Novi outskirts |
| **Vlašić / Donji Vakuf push** | ARBiH 7th Corps + HVO | 13–20 Sept 1995 | Vlašić plateau, Donji Vakuf | Vlašić TV repeater + plateau, Donji Vakuf town (13 Sept) |
| **HV/HVO *Maestral 2*** | HV + HVO | 8–15 Sept 1995 | Drvar / Šipovo / Jajce / Mrkonjić Grad / Bos. Grahovo (Glamoč Aug) | ~2,500 km², Jajce liberated 13 Sept |
| **HV/HVO *Južni Potez* ("Southern Move")** | HV + HVO | 8–11 Oct 1995 | Mrkonjić Grad → outskirts Banja Luka, Sanski Most → Prijedor approach | Halted 25 km from Banja Luka by ceasefire |
| **1st Corps Treskavica / Trnovo** | ARBiH 1st + 4th Corps | 15 June – August 1995 (Treskavica), reverberating into Sept | Treskavica massif, Trnovo, Bjelašnica foothills | Treskavica captured June–July, contributed to Sarajevo siege loosening |

By 12 October the federation held a contiguous corridor from Bihać through Sanski Most to within sight of Banja Luka, threatening the RS capital. US negotiators imposed the ceasefire specifically to lock in 51/49 territorial parity for Dayton — Holbrooke famously told Tuđman and Izetbegović to stop short of Banja Luka.

## 1. Operation *Sana 95* (13 September – 11 October 1995)

The ARBiH 5th Corps' breakout from the Bihać pocket. Largest single operational success of the ARBiH in the war.

### 1.1 Force composition

**ARBiH 5th Corps (Gen. Atif Dudaković)** — HQ Bihać. By Sept 1995 the corps had been reorganised into a division-heavy structure. Order of battle (per BB2 pp. 644–649, cross-checked against Hoare ch. 7):

| Formation | Subordinates | Approx. strength |
|---|---|---|
| **501st "Bihać" Mountain Brigade** | core unit, defended Bihać town | ~3,500 |
| **502nd "Bihać" Brigade** ("Tigrovi" / Tigers) | spearhead unit; Pećigrad, Kladuša axis | ~4,000 |
| **503rd "Cazin" Brigade** | northern axis (Cazin → Bos. Krupa) | ~3,000 |
| **505th "Buzim" Brigade** ("Vitezovi" / Knights) | northern shock unit, Una axis | ~3,500 |
| **506th "Velika Kladuša" Brigade** | post-Sept '95 only — formed after collapse of Abdić's APZB | ~2,000 |
| **510th "Oslobodilačka" Bde** | mobile reserve | ~2,500 |
| **511th "Bosanska" Bde** | south flank (Plješevica) | ~2,000 |
| **517th "Slavna" Bde** | reserve / rear | ~1,500 |
| Independent units | recon coy, MUP "Bosna" det., armoured platoon (~10 T-55s/M-84 captured from Abdić & SVK), arty regt (122mm D-30, 130mm M-46, captured BM-21) | ~2,000 |

**Total committed:** ~22,000–24,000 effectives.

**Opposing forces (VRS 2nd Krajina Corps, Gen. Maj. Radivoje Tomanić → Gen. Boško Kelečević mid-Sept):**
- 11th Krupa Light Bde, 1st Drvar Light Bde, 1st Petrovac Lt Bde, 17th Ključ Lt Bde, 6th Sanska Bde — all severely understrength after Storm + Maestral attrition.
- 2nd Krajina Corps strength fell from a notional ~25,000 in 1994 to perhaps **8,000–12,000 effectives** by mid-September 1995, with very low ammunition stocks (Burg & Shoup p. 357; BB2 p. 645).

### 1.2 Phases and axes of advance

**Phase 1 — Securing the rear (5–12 September 1995):**
Collapse of Fikret Abdić's "Autonomous Province of Western Bosnia" (APZB) in Velika Kladuša pocket. Operation *Sloboda 95* / *Sana-Una* preliminary action by 505th + 506th brigades, completing the reintegration of the Kladuša area on 7 August (initial entry) and final collapse 5–7 Sept after APZB rebellion forces fled to Croatia. This freed the 5th Corps' rear for the first time since October 1993.

**Phase 2 — Breakout south & east (13 September 1995):**
Main attack along three axes:
- **Axis A — Krupa / Una valley (north):** 505th + 503rd brigades attack from Bužim toward Bosanska Krupa. Krupa town falls 16 September.
- **Axis B — Bihać–Petrovac corridor (centre):** 501st + 502nd brigades attack east along the Plješevica–Petrovac road. Bosanski Petrovac falls 14–15 September after VRS 1st Petrovac Light Bde withdraws on orders rather than be enveloped.
- **Axis C — Plješevica / Lapac (south, against SVK rump):** 511th + 510th brigades push south, link with HV across the border at Donji Lapac (taken in Storm), securing the southern flank.

**Phase 3 — Exploitation toward Sanski Most & Ključ (16 September – 11 October 1995):**
With HV/HVO *Maestral 2* simultaneously taking Drvar (10 Sept), Šipovo (13 Sept), and Jajce (13 Sept) to the south, the VRS 2nd Krajina Corps could not regroup. 5th Corps advance:
- **Ključ** falls 17 September (linkup with HV/HVO from Bos. Grahovo direction).
- **Sanski Most** falls 10 October after fighting 6–10 October, in close coordination with 7th Corps elements approaching from the east.
- **Bosanski Novi (Novi Grad)** approach: 505th reaches outskirts but does not take the town before the ceasefire.

### 1.3 Approximate timeline (compressed, BB2 + Hoare)

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 4 Aug | Operation *Oluja* (Storm) begins — SVK Krajina collapses |
| 7 Aug | Velika Kladuša falls; Abdić's APZB ends |

### 1.4 Casualties

Tier-1 sources (ICTY case files referencing 2nd KK records, BB2 p. 646) give:
- **VRS losses (Sept 13 – Oct 11):** ~1,000–1,400 KIA, ~3,000–4,000 WIA, 2,000+ POW + deserters. 2nd Krajina Corps effectively destroyed as a fighting formation; reduced to scattered remnants by 10 October.
- **ARBiH 5th Corps losses:** ~600–900 KIA, ~2,500–3,500 WIA across the whole September–October offensive (Sloboda + Sana combined).
- **Civilian displacement:** ~125,000 Serb civilians fled the Bos. Krajina region — including from Sanski Most, Petrovac, Ključ — joining the Storm exodus through Banja Luka eastward.

### 1.5 Supply — how a besieged corps sustained a 200 km exploitation

This is the single most operationally interesting question of *Sana 95* and the one most likely to embarrass a simulation that models supply as a fixed-rate baseline.

**Pre-Storm reality (Nov 1994 – Aug 1995):**
- 5th Corps under triple siege (VRS 2nd Krajina Corps east, SVK 21st Corps west/north, Abdić APZB inside).
- Ammunition rationing severe — by spring 1995 brigades were on 5–15 rounds per rifleman, with home-made mortar rounds and locally-built "Bihać" 60mm tubes (the famous Bihać workshop). Hoare ch. 7 describes "weeks of fighting with 3 mortar rounds per tube per day."
- Calorie ration in the pocket was roughly **800–1,200 kcal/day** for soldiers (Burg & Shoup p. 348).
- UN airdrops and UNHCR convoys provided humanitarian, not military, supply.

**Why it changed in August–September 1995 (in order of weight):**
1. **Captured SVK depots in Croatia (Storm, 4–7 Aug):** ~150 SVK fuel and ammunition depots in former RSK fell to HV. Tuđman authorised transfer of a substantial portion to ARBiH 5th Corps in exchange for HV use of Bihać airfield and continued anti-VRS cooperation. BB2 p. 644 cites this as the single biggest factor. Ammunition stocks (especially 7.62×39, 7.62×54R, 82mm and 120mm mortar, 122mm D-30 howitzer rounds) flowed across the border at Ličko Petrovo Selo and Donji Lapac.
2. **Opening of road communications via Croatia:** Once Storm cleared the SVK from the west bank of the Una, the 5th Corps could be resupplied by truck from Karlovac through liberated Croatian territory. The siege ended physically on ~7 August. This is the supply enabler — the offensive starting on 13 September is conducted by a corps that has had **five weeks of unrestricted resupply** through Croatia for the first time in three years.
3. **Captured VRS stocks as the offensive rolls forward:** Bos. Petrovac and Ključ depots fell largely intact (VRS abandoned them in haste); BB2 p. 647 notes 5th Corps captured significant artillery and tube ammo at Petrovac and Sanski Most.
4. **HV "operational transfer":** several batteries of HV artillery (BM-21 *Grad* and 130mm M-46) effectively operated in support of 5th Corps from Croatian territory — fired from positions inside Croatia onto VRS targets in the BiH Krajina. ICTY records (Karadžić trial, prosecution Exh. P03978) confirm cross-border HV artillery support.

**Net:** Sana 95 is not sustained by an emergent improvement in 5th Corps' production base. It is sustained by an external supply transfusion that arrives in a six-week window between Storm and the offensive's start. This is the kind of step-function event most strategic simulations cannot represent without an explicit "external sponsor" mechanic.

## 2. Operation *Una 95* (related Una valley operations, September 1995)

Often described in the literature as the **Una operational sub-axis of Sana 95**, *Una 95* (sometimes *Una-Sana 95*) was the 5th Corps' northern push down the Una valley, distinct from the eastward Sana axis but conducted by the same corps.

**Note on terminology:** The user request mentions "Drina/Una valley operations" but the Drina valley is on the opposite side of BiH; this is almost certainly an Una-valley reference. There were no major ARBiH offensives in the Drina valley in September 1995 — the Drina theatre was already lost to RS after the fall of Srebrenica (11 July) and Žepa (25 July). Goražde held but did not attempt a breakout. This report treats the user's request as the Una-valley operations.

### Force and axes:
- **505th "Buzim" Bde** + **503rd "Cazin" Bde** + elements of 510th.
- Axis from Bužim → Otoka → Bosanska Krupa (taken 16 Sept) → Bosanski Novi outskirts.
- Northern flank protected by the Croatian border itself (HV having cleared the SVK from the Croatian side in Storm).
- Crossings of the Una secured at Krupa and Otoka.
- The northern axis was the *easier* axis — VRS 11th Krupa Light Bde was a thin screening force; most VRS strength had been pulled south to deal with the Petrovac–Ključ breakthrough.

By the ceasefire (12 Oct), 5th Corps held the entire west bank of the Una up to Bos. Novi but did not cross to take Novi proper or Prijedor.

## 3. ARBiH 7th Corps Fall 1995 Operations

The 7th Corps (HQ Travnik, Gen. Mehmed Alagić) operated in central Bosnia and was the federation partner most tightly coordinated with HVO during fall 1995.

### 3.1 Force composition (BB2 pp. 631–635)

By September 1995 the 7th Corps had been organised into two divisions:
- **17th "Krajina" Division** (Gen. Fikret Ćuskić) — 7th, 17th, 27th, 37th, 77th, 707th, 717th brigades. The "Krajina" lineage refers to Bosnian Krajina origin (refugees from the lost northwest), not Croatian Krajina.
- **77th Division** — 727th, 737th, 747th, 770th, 777th brigades.
- **3rd Corps** detachments in the OG Bosanska Krajina (operational group) sometimes attached.

Total: ~25,000–30,000 effectives.

### 3.2 Operations

**Operation *Vlašić 95* and the Donji Vakuf axis (13–20 September 1995):**
- Simultaneous with Sana 95 and Maestral 2.
- 7th Corps + HVO Operative Group Vlašić attacked along three axes:
  1. **Vlašić plateau** — frontal assault to clear the Vlašić TV repeater (a 1,943 m commanding feature) and the VRS positions on Mt Vlašić. Captured 13–15 Sept.
  2. **Donji Vakuf** — taken **13 September** by 7th Corps + HVO. This was the first town to fall in the federation September offensive. Donji Vakuf had been held by VRS 19th Donjovakufska Light Bde — its loss opened the road to Jajce from the south.
  3. **Jajce axis** — 7th Corps elements link up with HVO units (HVO 1st Guards) attacking from the Kupres/Šipovo direction. **Jajce falls 13 September** to combined ARBiH/HVO force (HVO entering town centre).

**Late September push:**
- 7th Corps continues east toward Mrkonjić Grad and northeast onto the Manjača plateau but slows.
- **6–10 October:** 7th Corps elements operate alongside 5th Corps in the **Sanski Most** assault from the south/east while 5th Corps comes from the west. Coordinated reduction of Sanski Most ends 10 October.
- The 7th Corps was also positioned to push toward **Mrkonjić Grad/Kotor Varoš/Banja Luka** but the 12 October ceasefire froze them roughly 30–40 km from Banja Luka.

**Coordination with HVO:**
The Donji Vakuf / Jajce / Šipovo axes were *literally* joint operations under VOPF coordination. HVO units entered Jajce centre while ARBiH 7th cleared the eastern approaches. The 13 March 1994 Washington Agreement and subsequent joint command arrangements (which had been largely paper through 1994 due to lingering Bosniak–Croat distrust) finally functioned operationally in September 1995 because **Croatia (Tuđman) had decided the federation would attack together or not at all**. The HVO Main Staff was placed under HV operational guidance.

### 3.3 Casualties

Tier-2 estimate (BB2): 7th Corps lost ~400–600 KIA across Vlašić–Donji Vakuf–Jajce, with VRS 19th DV Lt Bde + 22nd Krajina Lt Bde combined losing ~700–1,000 KIA and unknown POW.

## 4. ARBiH 1st Corps — Treskavica / Igman / Trnovo (summer–fall 1995)

The 1st Corps (HQ Sarajevo, Gen. Nedžad Ajnadžić by 1995) action that mattered for the September shift began in **June 1995**, not September.

### 4.1 Treskavica & Trnovo (15 June – August 1995)

**Operation *Treskavica*** (1st Corps + 4th Corps coordination, 15 June – mid-July 1995):
- Aim: relieve pressure on the Sarajevo siege from the south by retaking the Treskavica massif (2,088 m), Trnovo town, and the Bjelašnica/Igman approaches.
- ARBiH committed elements of the 14th Division (Sarajevo), supported by the 4th Corps from Konjic.
- **Trnovo** was taken by ARBiH on 22–23 June, lost back to VRS by mid-July (after VRS Drina Corps counterattack — the same Drina Corps that was simultaneously reducing Srebrenica). VRS 1st Sarajevo Mech Bde + Drina Corps elements drove ARBiH off Treskavica heights by late July.
- Net effect: failed to relieve the siege but **tied down significant VRS forces in the Trnovo–Treskavica sector**, contributing to the strategic depletion that mattered in September.

### 4.2 September 1995 — Sarajevo siege loosening

The Sarajevo siege loosened in September 1995 *primarily* through external action, not 1st Corps ground operations:
- **Deliberate Force** strikes on VRS C2 nodes around Sarajevo (Lukavica, Pale).
- **RRF artillery** from Mt Igman (UK 19 Regt RA, French Foreign Legion arty) destroyed VRS gun positions ringing the city.
- VRS forced to withdraw heavy weapons from the 20 km Total Exclusion Zone after 20 September.

1st Corps did conduct **localised attacks** in September on positions around Žuč hill, Mojmilo, Stupsko Brdo and the Žuč–Hum line, but did not break out. The siege was formally lifted only after Dayton in February 1996.

## 5. Strategic Context — What Made It Possible

### 5.1 Operation *Oluja* (Storm) — 4–7 August 1995

The most under-appreciated input to the BiH fall 1995 shift. Croatian Operation *Oluja* destroyed the SVK in 84 hours across a 630 km front. Effects on Bosnia:
- **SVK 21st (Kordun) Corps** — the formation pinning Bihać's western face — disintegrated. 5th Corps' three-year western siege ended.
- **HV gained intact operational depth** along the entire BiH border from Velebit to Dvor na Uni.
- **Refugee shock to RS rear:** ~150,000–200,000 Serb refugees moved through RS-held Bosanska Krajina toward Serbia, jamming roads and bleeding morale (BB2 p. 642; ICTY *Gotovina et al.* trial judgment paras. 1738–1742).
- **VRS strategic reserves committed to refugee management and to backstopping RS Krajina** rather than holding the line.

### 5.2 NATO *Deliberate Force* — 30 August – 20 September 1995

Trigger: **Markale II** mortar massacre, 28 August 1995 (37 killed, 90 wounded; UN trajectory analysis attributed to VRS positions east of Sarajevo). NATO decision under UNPROFOR's revised dual-key arrangement (Sec.-Gen. Akashi delegated UN key to Gen. Janvier).

**Scope:** 3,515 sorties; 1,026 munitions; principal targets were the VRS IADS (radars, SAMs), C2 (Han Pijesak Main Staff facility hit), ammunition depots (Pale, Sokolac), bridges across the Drina restricting VRS lateral movement.

**Strategic effects (per BB2 ch. 13, USAF Air University Press *Deliberate Force* study):**
- VRS Main Staff communications severely degraded for 2–3 weeks at the precise moment the federation ground offensives kicked off.
- Heavy-weapons exclusion zones around Sarajevo, Goražde, Tuzla enforced — VRS forced to withdraw artillery 20 km, denuding the siege ring.
- VRS could not provide effective artillery counter-battery to either Sana 95 or Maestral 2 in the critical Sept 8–20 window.
- Combined with RRF gun-line on Mt Igman (which fired ~600 rounds counter-battery in early September), the VRS artillery advantage — its single most important combat factor for three years — was suppressed.

### 5.3 ARBiH Reorganisation — January 1995

Effective 1 January 1995 (BB2 ch. 11):
- Old Operational Group (OG) structure → seven Corps + subordinate Divisions.
- **Corps numbered 1 (Sarajevo), 2 (Tuzla), 3 (Zenica), 4 (Mostar/Konjic), 5 (Bihać), 6 (Konjic — disbanded into 4th later in 1995), 7 (Travnik).** A 6th Corps existed briefly in 1994–early 1995 and was absorbed.
- Divisions created as intermediate echelon between Corps and Brigade (e.g., 17th "Krajina" Div, 12th "Sarajevo" Div, 14th Div, 28th "Podrinje" Div in Srebrenica before its fall).
- Total ARBiH manpower under arms by mid-1995: roughly **200,000–230,000** (Hoare ch. 8, Burg & Shoup p. 332 give 250,000 inclusive of reserve).
- This was the first time the ARBiH had the staff structure to *plan and run multi-corps simultaneous offensives*. Without it, neither Sana, Vlašić, nor the coordinated Sanski Most reduction would have been runnable.

### 5.4 Washington Agreement (18 March 1994) and the HVO–ARBiH Federation

Created the **Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina** (BiH Federation entity). Critical to fall 1995:
- HVO and ARBiH placed under a joint command in principle (operationally patchy through 1994 but functional by mid-1995).
- **VOPF (Vrhovno Operativno Politicki Forum)** — joint operational forum, chaired by Tuđman with Izetbegović, where the September 1995 offensives were coordinated. Maestral 2 was a VOPF plan, not a unilateral HV op.
- Croatia's logistical pipeline through Tomislavgrad/Imotski/Split open to ARBiH for the first time.

### 5.5 Croatian Army (HV) Cross-border Participation

Direct HV participation in BiH fall 1995 (BB2 chs 12–13, Tanner ch. 13):
- **Maestral 2** (8–15 Sept) — HV Split Corps (under Gen. Ante Gotovina) operating *inside BiH* alongside HVO. Glamoč and Bos. Grahovo had already fallen to HV/HVO in operation **Ljeto 95** (Summer 95, 25–30 July 1995) — this was the *staging* for September.
- **Južni Potez** (8–11 Oct) — HV + HVO push toward Banja Luka and Prijedor. ARBiH 5th Corps + 7th Corps coordinate from the west and east.
- HV artillery and armour units operated openly in BiH. The Croat-Bosniak war (Nov 92 – Mar 94) is firmly behind them; this is a coalition war.

### 5.6 VRS Manpower, Morale, Supply Collapse

By September 1995 the VRS was in cascading failure across multiple inputs:
- **Manpower:** From a 1992 peak of ~80,000–90,000, the VRS had bled through 30+ months of attrition. ICTY *Mladić* trial documents (KDZ-456) and BB2 p. 626 put effective VRS strength at **75,000–85,000** late summer 1995 — but distribution was the problem. With Storm, ~10,000–15,000 SVK personnel had to be absorbed; refugee management consumed organisation; the corps system was over-stretched along an ~1,200 km front.
- **Ammunition:** FRY (Yugoslavia) had formally cut military aid in August 1994 (Milošević's blockade) and even though significant clandestine flow continued, VRS stocks of artillery shells, AT munitions, and AA missiles were severely depleted by mid-1995. BB2 p. 643 cites VRS 2nd Krajina Corps requesting emergency resupply that did not arrive.
- **Desertions:** From August 1995 the VRS suffered mass desertions in the Krajina corps area — soldiers whose families had fled in the Storm exodus simply went home or to Serbia. BB2 p. 645 cites 2nd KK reporting "absences" of 20–30% by mid-September.
- **Command-control:** Deliberate Force hits + the Mladić–Karadžić feud (Mladić had been formally relieved by Karadžić on 4 August but refused to step down, generating a parallel-command crisis) meant the Main Staff was paralysed at the operational moment that mattered.

## 7. Items the Current Engine Probably Cannot Represent

Flagged for the engine designer's separate synthesis. Not solutions — just historical inputs that the simulation will struggle with as-is:

1. **Cross-border HV support as a logistics transfusion to ARBiH 5th Corps.** The five-week supply window between Storm (7 Aug) and Sana 95 (13 Sept) is the entire reason the offensive was sustainable. A supply system tied to internal production + UN convoys cannot represent this step-function. Engine almost certainly has no "external sponsor delivers ammunition across border" event.

2. **NATO *Deliberate Force* as a VRS combat-capability suppressor.** Not casualties — *capability degradation*: C2 nodes destroyed, IADS gone, artillery forced out of TEZ, lateral bridging knocked down. The simulation likely has no concept of an external air campaign reducing one faction's effective combat multiplier for a 3-week window.

3. **Simultaneous multi-corps coordinated offensive across factions (ARBiH 5th + 7th + HVO + HV).** A federation-wide pulse on a 5-day window at multiple axes against a single defender. If the engine schedules operations independently per faction, it will under-represent the *concentration of pressure*. The historical effect was that VRS could not laterally redeploy because every front was active simultaneously.

4. **Supply collapse cascades on the defender.** Once Krupa and Petrovac depots fell, VRS could not move ammunition laterally because (a) bridges were down (Deliberate Force), (b) refugee columns choked roads, (c) Storm had removed the SVK that provided the rear corridor. An additive supply attrition model misses the *interaction*. The collapse was structural, not gradual.

5. **The Mladić–Karadžić parallel-command crisis** (4 Aug – late 1995). At the precise operational moment, the VRS had two commanders disputing authority. No engine I am aware of models internal command-chain rupture as a multiplier on operational tempo.

6. **Refugee flow degrading rear-area combat power.** 150,000–200,000 refugees passing through RS rear is not modelled as a combat-system input but it was a real factor in 2nd KK collapse and the inability of VRS Main Staff to commit reserves.

7. **The Velika Kladuša / Abdić rear-clearing operation (5 Aug – 7 Sept).** Without modelling APZB as a hostile pocket inside RBiH territory that has to be reduced *before* 5th Corps can attack east, the simulation cannot represent why 5th Corps could not have launched Sana 95 a month earlier.

8. **Captured-equipment cascades.** 5th Corps fielding ex-SVK T-55s, BM-21s, and 122mm D-30s captured in the Storm aftermath. If the engine has fixed equipment per brigade with no capture mechanic, the late-Sept artillery support to Sana is underrepresented.

9. **The 1995 ARBiH reorganisation itself** (Operational Groups → Corps + Divisions on 1 January 1995). If the engine models ARBiH 1992 OG structure throughout, multi-axis operational planning cannot work in 1995. The reorg unlocked *the ability to plan Sana 95 at all*.

10. **VRS internal "must-hold" priorities that diverge from territorial value.** Karadžić in early September 1995 ordered the VRS to *hold Banja Luka, the Posavina corridor, and eastern Bosnia* and accept the loss of western Bosnia rather than disperse forces. If the engine treats all RS-held municipalities as equally defensible, the historical concentration on east + corridor + Banja Luka is missed.

## 8. Cross-reference to the Existing Scripted Ops Packet

The existing `docs/40_reports/implemented/20260501_LATE_1995_SCRIPTED_OPS_PACKET.md` encodes Operation Sana with:
- `arbih_5th_corps` as primary corps
- 3 axes (Krupa Una Valley, Bihać–Petrovac, Sanski Most + Ključ)

That matches this report's force assignment and axes but is missing:
- **7th Corps** as a participating corps (Sanski Most was a 5th + 7th joint reduction; Donji Vakuf / Jajce / Vlašić are 7th + HVO, not in the packet at all).
- **Una axis as a distinct operation** vs. an axis of Sana 95 — historically this is a per-corps preference question, defensible either way.
- **The triggers preceding it:** Maestral 2 (turn ≥ 175 in packet) fires the same week as Sana; historically Maestral started 8 Sept and Sana 13 Sept, so a small turn-offset (Maestral at 175, Sana at 176) would be more historically accurate.
- **Operation *Sloboda* / Velika Kladuša reduction** (5 Aug – 7 Sept) — should precede Sana 95 by 4–5 turns to represent the rear-clearing precondition.

These are observations for the engine designer; this report is not proposing engine changes.

## 9. One-line Take

The fall 1995 collapse of RS in western Bosnia was not the outcome of a sustained ARBiH industrial-and-manpower mobilisation eventually winning out; it was the outcome of three external step-function events (Storm, Deliberate Force, the Washington Agreement coming operationally alive) intersecting with a VRS that had spent its strategic capital and could no longer cover its frontage. The ARBiH 5th and 7th Corps offensives were the *exploitation* of that window — necessary, well-executed, but not sufficient on their own. Any simulation aiming to reproduce October 1995 territorial control must model the three external inputs as first-class objects, not as gentle modifiers.
