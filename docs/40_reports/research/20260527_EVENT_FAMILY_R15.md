## 1. Cited Historical Narrative

The General Framework Agreement for Peace in Bosnia and Herzegovina (the "Dayton Accords") was initialed at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, Dayton, Ohio, on **21 November 1995** by Presidents Alija Izetbegović (Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina), Slobodan Milošević (Federal Republic of Yugoslavia, negotiating for the Bosnian Serbs under the 30 August 1995 Patriarch Paper delegation), and Franjo Tuđman (Republic of Croatia). The agreement was formally signed at the Élysée Palace in Paris on 14 December 1995.

The agreement comprises a General Framework Agreement and eleven annexes covering: Annex 1-A (military aspects of the peace settlement — IFOR deployment, separation of forces, Zone of Separation, heavy-weapons cantonment); Annex 1-B (regional stabilization, arms control); Annex 2 (Inter-Entity Boundary Line — the 51:49 territorial division between the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina and Republika Srpska); Annex 3 (elections, with OSCE oversight); Annex 4 (Constitution of Bosnia and Herzegovina); Annex 5 (arbitration); Annex 6 (human rights, with a Human Rights Chamber and Ombudsperson); Annex 7 (refugees and displaced persons — right of return); Annex 8 (national monuments commission); Annex 9 (public corporations); Annex 10 (civilian implementation — the High Representative); Annex 11 (international police task force).

The Bosnian Serb side was bound by Milošević's signature under the Patriarch Paper joint-delegation authority (see R14 worksheet §1). The substantive content was largely settled in the Geneva (8 September) and New York (26 September) framework agreements; Dayton was the implementation conference, refining the constitutional, territorial, and military annexes.

The Pale leadership (Karadžić, Mladić) was not present at Dayton. Their formal acceptance of the agreement was a discrete decision in November 1995: ratify Milošević's signature, or repudiate the delegation and reject the framework. The historical record (ICTY *Karadžić* IT-95-5/18-T §§5020–5067) shows that the Pale leadership ratified the agreement — Karadžić publicly disagreed with several provisions (notably the Sarajevo unification under Federation control, the territorial provisions for Goražde and the eastern Bosnia enclaves, and the unitary BiH state shell) but did not repudiate Milošević's authority to sign. Mladić expressed military objections to the IFOR deployment terms but issued the orders necessary for VRS compliance with the Zone of Separation and heavy-weapons cantonment.

A hardline counterfactual existed and was actively considered. ICTY *Karadžić* §§5025–5040 records Karadžić's December 1995 internal communications expressing reservations and exploring whether the RS National Assembly could reject the agreement in the ratification vote. The Assembly ultimately voted to accept the framework in late November 1995, with reservations noted but not operationalized as repudiation. The hardline path — National Assembly rejection, Pale repudiation of the Patriarch Paper, RS unilateral declaration of independence inside the recognized borders of BiH — would have invited immediate NATO ground-force escalation, FRY/Milošević disowning the RS publicly, and likely the loss of the territorial gains the 51:49 agreement secured.

UN S/RES/1031 (15 December 1995) authorized IFOR; UN S/1995/999 (the General Framework Agreement transmitted to the Security Council) records the text. The Federation Ground Offensive's halt at the Holbrooke 51:49 line (X8) was a precondition for the territorial settlement; Dayton ratified that halt and converted it into the Inter-Entity Boundary Line.

## 3. Proposed Counterfactual Options

### 3.1 `accept` — `Historical default`

Ratify the General Framework Agreement initialed by Milošević on 21 November 1995. Order VRS compliance with Annex 1-A military provisions: heavy-weapons cantonment, Zone of Separation, IFOR deployment, freedom of movement. Accept the 51:49 Inter-Entity Boundary Line, the Federation/RS entity structure, and the unitary BiH state shell with the High Representative civilian-implementation framework. Accept Sarajevo unification under Federation control (the territorial provision Karadžić publicly objected to). Material posture: alliance lock with FRY consolidated; international standing recovers from war-low baseline; sanctions relief flows (UN S/RES/1022); war ends; RS as a recognized entity inside BiH secures the territorial gains compatible with the 51:49 line.

Source provenance: documented historical RS Assembly and Pale leadership decision per ICTY *Karadžić* §§5020–5067, *Mladić* §§4750–4795. Source tier `icty_icj_un`.

### 3.2 `hardline` — `Counterfactual staff path`

Repudiate Milošević's signature; have the RS National Assembly reject the General Framework Agreement; declare RS unilateral independence inside the recognized borders of BiH; refuse IFOR deployment and Zone of Separation compliance. Material posture: alliance lock with FRY ruptures definitively (Milošević will publicly disown the Pale leadership and accept whatever territorial reversal the Federation/IFOR can impose); NATO ground-force escalation pulled forward (IFOR was authorized as Implementation Force, but the same NATO structure could pivot to active enforcement); the territorial gains achieved at the 51:49 line are at acute risk because the cessation-of-hostilities baseline is no longer guaranteed; international isolation deepens; sanctions reimpose or expand; war continues as a low-intensity but internationally-supervised conflict with the RS as the spoiler party.

Design provenance: a plausible alternative path explored in Karadžić's internal communications in December 1995 (ICTY *Karadžić* §§5025–5040) but rejected at the Assembly ratification vote. Source tier `design_counterfactual`.

**No Ring boundary applies:** Neither option authorizes new sensitive-history acts. Both are endgame posture decisions on the ratification of the Dayton framework.
