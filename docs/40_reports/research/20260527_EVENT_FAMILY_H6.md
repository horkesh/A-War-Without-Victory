## 1. Cited Historical Narrative

The HVO detention system in Herzegovina and Central Bosnia in 1993 — operated by HVO Military Police, HVO Operational Zone authorities, and HZ HB Ministry of Defence structures — became internationally visible in mid- to late-1993 as the Croat-Bosniak war intensified. The three principal facilities entered the ICTY record as central evidence of the Prlić JCE common purpose:

- **Heliodrom (Mostar military prison complex)** — repurposed JNA barracks complex south of Mostar under HVO Military Police 5th Battalion control. *Prosecutor v. Prlić et al.* IT-04-74-T Trial Judgment (29 May 2013) Vol. 3 §§1532-1740 reconstructs the Heliodrom operation: detentions from 9 May 1993 onward following the HVO offensive in West Mostar; thousands of Bosniak men detained; use of detainees for forced labor on confrontation lines (the "labor brigades" — see Trial Judgment Vol. 3 §§1645-1715); deaths from labor-line exposure, beatings, and forced exposure to ARBiH fire. The Trial Chamber found that the Heliodrom regime constituted persecution, inhumane acts, and unlawful confinement as crimes against humanity (Vol. 3 §§1700-1740; Vol. 4 §§95-130 on JCE attribution to Prlić, Stojić, Praljak, Petković, Ćorić, Pušić).
- **Dretelj (Čapljina municipality)** — repurposed JNA barracks under HVO 1st Brigade and Military Police 3rd Battalion control. *Prlić* Trial Judgment Vol. 3 §§1741-1880 documents Dretelj operations from mid-1993 onward: hunger, dehydration in summer 1993 heat (detainees held in steel hangars), beatings, sexual violence, killings. The Trial Chamber individuated specific deaths and made findings of inhumane treatment, persecution, and the JCE common purpose attribution (Vol. 3 §§1845-1880).
- **Gabela (Čapljina municipality)** — adjacent to Dretelj; same HVO command structure. *Prlić* Trial Judgment Vol. 3 §§1881-1990 documents Gabela operations: overcrowding, denial of food and water, beatings, executions; the Trial Chamber found JCE-attributable persecution and inhumane treatment (Vol. 3 §§1955-1990).

Two further facilities are referenced in the same case set and corroborate the systemic character of the regime:

- **Vojno** (Mostar municipality) — labor camp where Heliodrom detainees were sent for forced labor close to confrontation lines. *Prlić* Vol. 3 §§1620-1644.
- **Ljubuški** (military prison) — *Naletilić & Martinović* IT-98-34-T (31 March 2003) §§361-422 documents the regime and Naletilić's command responsibility.

The **Prlić Appeals Judgment (29 November 2017)** §§601-650 upheld the JCE common-purpose findings against the six accused — Jadranko Prlić (HZ HB Prime Minister), Bruno Stojić (HZ HB Defence Minister), Slobodan Praljak (HVO Main Staff commander), Milivoj Petković (HVO Chief of Main Staff), Valentin Ćorić (HVO Military Police chief), Berislav Pušić (HVO Service for Exchange) — confirming that camp operation was a JCE-coordinated component of the HZ HB war effort, not an aberrant local-commander pattern.

The international visibility of the HVO camp regime came via:

- **ICRC reports and field visits** — ICRC accessed Heliodrom, Dretelj, and Gabela in late summer / autumn 1993 and made detention-condition findings that entered ICTY evidence (Prlić Trial Judgment Vol. 3 §§1700, 1855, 1965; ICRC public communications on detention conditions in the Mostar region 1993-1994).
- **UN A/54/549** (Srebrenica fall report, 1999) §§17, 35, 102 — situates the HVO camps within the wider 1993 detention pattern; corroborates the contemporaneous international observer record.
- **UN Commission of Experts Final Report S/1994/674** Annex VIII (Detention Facilities) — documents HVO camps alongside RS and ARBiH facilities; provides the cross-faction comparative baseline used in the ICTY indictment phase.
- **Balkan Battlegrounds Vol. II** pp. 459-462, 471-475 — operational context for the HVO offensive in West Mostar and the Mostar valley campaign that produced the detentions; BB does not enter prosecutorial detail.

**The historical HRHB posture in response to international exposure was de-facto denial.** The HZ HB Presidency and HVO command (Prlić, Stojić, Praljak, Petković, Ćorić) consistently framed the detentions as wartime POW arrangements or "voluntary collection centers" through 1993-1994; the regime was wound down only under sustained US/EU pressure during the Washington Agreement negotiation phase (February-March 1994) and through ICRC-mediated releases (Prlić Trial Judgment Vol. 3 §§1990-2050 on the wind-down). Selective transfers and releases occurred but did not represent a policy shift toward transparency until the Washington Agreement framework forced the issue.

- **Historical analogy:** A partial compliance posture exists in the documented record as a transitional phase during the Washington Agreement negotiation window (February-March 1994), when ICRC access expanded gradually under US/EU pressure (Prlić Trial Judgment Vol. 3 §§1990-2050). Counterfactually `obstruct` models the HZ HB Presidency adopting this transitional posture earlier (mid-1993 rather than early 1994), without the Washington framework forcing the issue.
- **Design provenance:** `design_counterfactual` — the historical Presidency-level posture through 1993 was full denial, not partial compliance. The branch counterfactually models accelerated transparency.
- **Sensitive-history check:** Confirmed — `obstruct` authorizes no new sensitive act. It is a posture-level shift in international engagement. Per packet §3.6: option carries no `paramilitary_policy` write, no `control_change`, no `displacement_*` write, no `war_crimes_events` increment that bypasses existing systems. The camps already exist in state; this option neither closes them (that is `cooperate`) nor extends them.
- **Cost floor (Phase D required):** `international_standing: -5` (still negative — partial compliance does not erase prior exposure); `patron_confidence: -3` (Zagreb reads partial transparency as incomplete commitment to the JCE line); `cost_ledger_annotation` recording the partial-acknowledgment posture for endgame voice.

### 3.2 `cooperate` — `Counterfactual staff path`

- **Label:** `cooperate` — Acknowledge the detention system; grant full ICRC and international observer access; transfer detainees to ICRC-supervised conditions; signal commitment to Washington-track engagement.
- **Historical analogy:** The eventual Washington Agreement wind-down phase (February-March 1994 onward) approximates `cooperate` in operational fact, though the historical timing was after sustained US/EU pressure rather than HZ HB Presidency-initiative (Prlić Trial Judgment Vol. 3 §§1990-2050). Counterfactually `cooperate` models the Presidency adopting this posture pre-Washington — i.e., a Presidency-initiative transparency posture rather than patron-forced compliance.
- **Design provenance:** `design_counterfactual` — no HZ HB document or contemporary record shows a Presidency-initiative transparency posture in 1993. The branch counterfactually grounds the option in the documented Washington-phase wind-down, advanced in time.
- **Sensitive-history check:** Confirmed — `cooperate` authorizes no sensitive act and forecloses the continuation of the JCE common-purpose detention regime as a Presidency-endorsed pattern. Per packet §3.6: no `paramilitary_policy` write, no new sensitive acts.
