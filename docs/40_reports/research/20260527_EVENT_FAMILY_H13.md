## 1. Cited Historical Narrative (Conditions That Make a Third-Entity Push Plausible)

The HR HB political project from its 1991 founding through 1995 contained an ideological substrate that could, under different upstream branching, have manifested as a third-entity push at Dayton. ICTY Prlić et al. IT-04-74 reconstructs that substrate as the documented foundation of the Joint Criminal Enterprise (JCE):

- **Croatian Community of Herzeg-Bosnia (HZ HB), 18 November 1991, Grude** — proclaimed by Mate Boban under HDZ-BiH coordinated with Zagreb; defined a Croat political-territorial unit within Bosnia-Herzegovina along the 1939 Banovina Hrvatska borders. Prlić Trial Judgment vol. I §§423-461. The Banovina-borders framing was the maximalist territorial reference; the 1939 administrative-unit borders extended into central and northern Bosnia beyond any contemporaneous demographic Croat-majority area, defining the territorial ambit of the third-entity counterfactual.
- **Croatian Republic of Herzeg-Bosnia (HR HB), 28 August 1993, Mostar** — proclaimed by Boban; upgrade of the Community to a Republic with constitutive-state architecture (Presidency, Government, HVO armed forces). Prlić Trial Judgment vol. I §§556-590. The Republic-level proclamation was the institutional spine that a Dayton-era third-entity push would have built upon, had it not been institutionally subordinated by the Washington Agreement (1 March 1994).
- **ICTY JCE common criminal purpose finding (Prlić Trial Judgment vol. IV §§44-67; Appeals Judgment 29 Nov 2017 §§601-650)** — "the ultimate purpose of the JCE was to establish a Croatian territorial entity that would either be part of, or annexed to, Croatia, restoring the borders of the 1939 Banovina." This is the binding legal record of the maximalist project. The Greater-Croatia framing was tribunal-found as the underlying political objective; it was not delivered historically because the Washington Agreement (1 March 1994) subordinated HR HB into the Federation and the post-Storm 1995 strategic conditions removed Zagreb's political incentive to push for an independent Croat entity inside BiH.
- **Karadžić-Tudjman / Karađorđevo 25 March 1991 partition discussions** — Prlić Trial Judgment vol. I §§318-356; Kordić IT-95-14/2-T Trial Judgment §§465-475. The Croat-Serb partition framework predicate; the Banovina-borders return that this framework would have ratified is the territorial logic underpinning any third-entity counterfactual.
- **Tudjman Presidential Transcripts** (admitted at Prlić, Kordić, Blaškić; Prlić Trial Judgment vol. I §§357-389) — Tudjman directing HR HB as a "Croatian state-building enterprise inside BiH." The patron's documented preference for a separate Croat political vehicle (rather than a Federation arrangement) underpins the third-entity counterfactual's historical plausibility before the Washington Agreement subordinated HR HB; after Washington, Tudjman's posture pivoted to Federation cooperation but the underlying preference remained a documented residual.

**Why the historical push did not occur in 1995:**

- **Washington Agreement (1 March 1994)** institutionally pre-committed HR HB to Federation integration (B10/H9 chain).
- **Operations Storm + Mistral 2 + Sana (Aug-Sep 1995)** delivered HV-HVO-ARBiH coalition gains; Zagreb's strategic position was favorable to the Federation framework as the durable Croat-political vehicle inside BiH.
- **US diplomatic pressure (Holbrooke)** at Dayton ratified the Federation entity rather than re-opening the third-entity question.
- **HR HB leadership transition** — Krešimir Zubak elected HR HB President 5 August 1994 under Federation reform; Boban's hardline current was displaced from delegation leadership.

**Why the counterfactual is plausible analytically:**

- The ICTY-found maximalist project was an ideological substrate that did not disappear with the Washington Agreement; it remained a documented residual within HR HB political circles through 1994-95 (Prlić Trial Judgment vol. I §§591-640 on intra-HR HB dissatisfaction with Federation integration).
- A different upstream H1 branch (`croat_republic` sustained without Washington Agreement reorientation), a different H7 outcome (Zagreb-orders-ceasefire 1994 not delivered), or a different summer-1995 operational outcome (Storm-Mistral 2 not generating Federation cohesion incentives) could have left HR HB in 1995 with an institutional and territorial position from which a third-entity push at Dayton was a coherent (though still unlikely) move.
- Phase D, if authored, must wire the upstream eligibility conditions tightly so that H13 visibility requires *cumulative* counterfactual upstream branching, not a single late-game flip.
