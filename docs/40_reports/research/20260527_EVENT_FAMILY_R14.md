## 1. Cited Historical Narrative

Through August–October 1995, US Assistant Secretary of State Richard Holbrooke ran the diplomatic shuttle that produced the framework leading to Dayton. The central feature of the Holbrooke approach was to treat Slobodan Milošević in Belgrade as the negotiating principal for the Bosnian Serb side, bypassing the Pale leadership (Karadžić, Mladić) in direct contact.

The mechanism was the **"Patriarch Paper"** of 30 August 1995, signed at the Serbian Orthodox Patriarchate in Belgrade. In this document Radovan Karadžić, Momčilo Krajišnik, Nikola Koljević, Aleksa Buha, and Ratko Mladić — the entire Bosnian Serb political-military leadership — delegated full negotiating authority for the Bosnian Serbs to a six-member joint Yugoslav-Bosnian Serb delegation chaired by Milošević. The delegation was bound to vote as a unit; Milošević, controlling the Yugoslav (FRY) half of the votes, was thereby able to bind the Bosnian Serbs to any agreement he chose to sign. Patriarch Pavle of the Serbian Orthodox Church witnessed the signing, conferring religious-political legitimacy on the delegation.

Holbrooke's account (corroborated by the US negotiating record and by ICTY judgment findings) is that the Patriarch Paper was the indispensable precondition for the Dayton track: without it, every Holbrooke proposal would have required separate Pale ratification and would have been vetoed by Karadžić or Mladić. With it, Milošević could negotiate the substantive framework (Geneva Agreed Basic Principles, 8 September 1995; New York Further Agreed Principles, 26 September 1995; ultimately the General Framework Agreement at Dayton) without further Pale clearance, and could bind the RS to its terms.

The compliance posture documented in October 1995 was thus a layered decision: (i) the Pale leadership accepted the Patriarch Paper delegation rather than running the negotiations themselves (the historical default); (ii) Milošević negotiated under heavy US pressure and accepted the 51:49 territorial split, the Federation/RS entity structure, and the unitary BiH state shell; (iii) Pale ratified Milošević's agreements rather than repudiating them. Each step had a counterfactual in which Karadžić and Mladić defied the US framework, repudiated Milošević's delegation, or attempted to negotiate bilaterally outside the Holbrooke shuttle — none of which they did.

The Geneva Agreed Basic Principles (8 September 1995) established the entity structure (Federation, RS) within a single internationally recognized BiH; the New York Further Agreed Principles (26 September 1995) added constitutional and electoral framework. Both were signed by the FRY/Bosnian Serb joint delegation under the Patriarch Paper authority. UN S/1995/780 (12 September 1995) and S/1995/827 (26 September 1995) record the framework agreements.

By the time the Dayton conference convened (1 November 1995), the substantive framework was already largely set; Dayton was the implementation conference, not the framework conference. The Pale leadership's October posture — comply with Belgrade's negotiating mandate — was the decisive enabling decision.

## 3. Proposed Counterfactual Options

### 3.1 `comply_with_belgrade` — `Historical default`

Accept the Patriarch Paper delegation; permit Milošević to negotiate the substantive framework under joint-delegation authority; ratify the Geneva and New York agreements Milošević signs. Material posture: alliance lock with FRY (patron pressure direction reverses from coercion to alignment); diplomatic credibility gain proportional to the framework's domestic-political costs (51:49 territorial split below the RS military high-water mark of ~70%); opens the Dayton track (R15) with `accept` as the modal default; closes alternative-endgame trajectories (Banja Luka push, prolonged-war, RSFRY confederation track).

Source provenance: documented historical RS decision per ICTY *Karadžić* §§4985–5020, Holbrooke ch. 8–10, UN S/1995/780 and S/1995/827. Source tier `icty_icj_un`.

### 3.2 `defy_us_framework` — `Counterfactual staff path`

Repudiate the Patriarch Paper delegation (or refuse to sign it in the first place); insist that any negotiating framework requires Pale ratification; reject the Geneva 8 September or New York 26 September agreements after Milošević signs them. Material posture: alliance lock with FRY ruptures (Milošević will publicly disown the Pale leadership and signal to the US that he can no longer guarantee Bosnian Serb compliance — the basis for the eventual Dayton track); accelerated international isolation; loss of Holbrooke channel as a buffer against direct NATO/Federation pressure; high risk of Belgrade-imposed leadership change (Milošević moves to replace Karadžić, accelerating R11 in the counterfactual direction); opens the prolonged-war / no-Dayton trajectory.

Design provenance: a plausible alternative path in which the Pale leadership chose autonomy from Belgrade over the substantive concessions Milošević would extract. Not documented as the RS leadership's October 1995 posture. Source tier `design_counterfactual`.

**No Ring boundary applies:** Neither option authorizes new sensitive-history acts. Both are diplomatic posture decisions.
