4 — Bombardment Exposure Model

The key innovation for closing the ARBiH casualty gap. Brigades facing superior enemy heavy weapons take passive attrition proportional to the equipment mismatch.

effect = min(1.0,   ln(incoming_FP / own_FP)   /   SCALE)
casualties = personnel × 0.012 × effect  —  added to base frontline attrition (0.5%/week)

Why ratio-based, not linear deficit?

A linear model (deficit / divisor) gave only 1.4× differentiation between ARBiH and HVO. The log-ratio model ln(incoming/own) gives 2.07× differentiation, correctly penalizing brigades with very low equipment (no counter-battery fire, no suppression capability) much more steeply. The natural logarithm also provides diminishing returns at high ratios — going from ratio 7 to 14 doesn't double the effect.

RATE=0.012 + ratio model hits the sweet spot. RATE=0.015 closes more of the ARBiH KIA gap but drops HVO below 40k and crashes OSID match via cascade effects. The ratio-based model at 0.012 keeps HVO in-band (41k) while still recovering 85% of the ARBiH KIA target.

Design principle confirmed

No hardcoded personnel limits. Personnel totals emerge from: census demographics (initial pool size) → mobilization rate × surge × exhaustion (ongoing growth) → reinforcement rate ramp (pool → brigade transfer) → combat attrition (drain) → pool depletion (finite manpower). Four comprehensive combat mechanics now differentiate factions organically through equipment, officers, ethnic composition, and bombardment exposure.
