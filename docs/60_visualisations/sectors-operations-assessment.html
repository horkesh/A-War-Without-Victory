3 — Problems Identified
P1: No max-size splitting. The sector builder (corps_front_sectors.ts) only splits on connectivity via BFS. A 86-edge contiguous front stays as one sector. This creates jumbo sectors where 15-16 brigades share a single sector identity, making them operationally indistinguishable.

P1 No geographic splitting of large sectors

VRS 1st Krajina has 86 edges and 16 brigades in one sector. Facing both HRHB and RBiH. Impossible to assign distinct defensive tasks or launch a sector offensive with just the right 5 brigades.

Impact: Sector offensives pull all 16 brigades. No granular force allocation. Density metric meaningless at this scale.

P1 reserve_brigade_ids always empty

Line 445 of corps_front_sectors.ts: reserve_brigade_ids: []. Never populated. The corps directive tracks reserve_fraction, but the sector builder ignores it. Interior brigades (not on any front edge) get assigned to sectors but aren't flagged as reserves.

Impact: Sector offensive launch uses all brigades minus a hardcoded 15%. No sector-level reserve management. The CorpsFrontPanel UI shows "Reserve (0)" always.

P2 Sector offensives dormant in year-1

All pre-planned operations complete by ~week 5. After that, no new sector offensives launch because: RS goes to balanced at week 20, RBiH stays general_defensive through week 56. Launch requires offensive or balanced + 3 brigades + supply ≥ 0.6.

Impact: No coordinated multi-OSID operations for 35+ weeks. Brigades operate purely on directive-based individual targeting. Historically VRS conducted major named operations through 1992.

P2 No opposing-faction-aware splitting

VRS 1st Krajina sector:0 faces both RBiH and HRHB simultaneously across 86 edges. These are distinct strategic contexts (alliance dynamics, co-ethnic scoring, operational tempo) that shouldn't be in one bucket.

Impact: Sector offensives against RBiH accidentally include edges facing HRHB. Density/threat metrics average across two different conflicts.

P3 Single-sector corps can't differentiate front zones

ARBiH 5th Corps (Bihać pocket): 7 brigades, 1 sector, 20 edges. Historically this force defended multiple axes (Bihać north vs south vs Cazin approach). Single sector prevents prioritized defense.

P3 Sarajevo stacking (10 brigades, 14 edges)

ARBiH 1st Corps sector:1 has density 0.71 with threat 0.0. These brigades have nothing to do. This is a downstream effect of no reserve system + no rotation mechanic.

4 — Paradox Team Assessment
Game Designer Sector granularity and operational art

Sectors should model "defended directions", not "everything a corps touches." In real BiH war operations, a corps AO was divided into operational directions (pravac) — each with a responsible officer, assigned units, and specific task (defend, delay, attack). VRS 1st Krajina Corps had distinct Bihać, Jajce, and Donji Vakuf directions simultaneously.

Target: 3-8 brigades per sector. This is the sweet spot for:

  • Meaningful density metrics (not diluted over 86 edges)
  • Sector offensives that involve a realistic force (3-6 brigades attacking 2-4 objectives)
  • Reserve designation (1-2 brigades behind the line per sector)
  • UI readability (click a sector, see a manageable list of units)

Opposing-faction splits are mandatory. Facing RBiH and HRHB is not the same operational problem. Sector metrics (threat, density) should be per-opponent to drive correct AI decisions.

Technical Architect Splitting algorithm design

Two splitting criteria, applied in order:

  • Opposing-faction split (Phase A): After finding connected components, group edges by opposing faction. Edges facing RBiH and edges facing HRHB become separate sectors even if contiguous. Simple, deterministic, cheap.
  • Size-based split (Phase B): After Phase A, if a sector still has >MAX_SECTOR_EDGES (suggest 25-30), find a natural cut point. Algorithm: walk the edge chain, find the edge nearest the midpoint by cumulative length, split there. Each half keeps its own friendly/enemy OSID sets. Recurse if still over threshold.

Stability concern: Sectors are rebuilt every turn. If we split by midpoint, small territorial changes could cause sector boundaries to jump. Mitigate: use municipality boundaries as preferred cut points (edges that cross a municipality boundary are natural split candidates).

Reserve population: After sector assignment, interior brigades (location_osid not in any sector's enemy_osids neighbors) go to reserve_brigade_ids. Respect reserve_fraction from corps directive. Excess interior brigades beyond reserve quota stay in assigned_brigade_ids.

Gameplay Programmer Implementation approach

Changes scoped to 3 files:

  • corps_front_sectors.ts — Add splitByOpposingFaction() and splitBySize() after findSubSegments(). Populate reserve_brigade_ids in buildSectorFromSubSegments().
  • sector_offensive.ts — No changes needed. Launch evaluation already checks per-sector brigade count. Smaller sectors = more focused offensives automatically.
  • bot_corps_ai.ts — Update sector_targets population to handle more sectors per corps. May need to adjust max_attackers_per_target per sector size.

Risk: More sectors means more sector offensive evaluations per turn. Should be negligible — it's O(sectors × brigades) and we're going from ~35 to ~50-60 sectors.

Historian Historical operations in year-1

VRS conducted major named operations throughout 1992, not just the first 5 weeks:

  • Operacija Koridor (Jun-Oct 1992): Months-long Posavina corridor battle. Multiple phases.
  • Prijedor cleansing operations (Apr-Jul 1992): Systematic, sustained over months.
  • Sarajevo siege tightening (Apr 1992 onwards): Continuous operations around the ring.
  • Drina valley operations (Apr-Sep 1992): Bratunac, Srebrenica, Cerska in sequence.

The current system where pre-planned operations end by week 5 and nothing replaces them is ahistorical. VRS corps should be able to generate new sector offensives from balanced stance, not just offensive. The historical pattern was persistent operational pressure, not a single burst then static defense.

UI/UX Dev Sector panel improvements

With smaller sectors, the CorpsFrontPanel becomes more useful:

  • 3-8 brigades per sector = readable list without scrolling
  • Reserves shown separately (currently always "Reserve (0)")
  • Per-sector opposing faction badge (clear who you're facing)
  • Sector offensive status indicator (planning/executing/recovering/idle)
  • Sector name derived from geography (e.g., "Bihać Direction") instead of just "sector:corps:N"
Scenario Runner Calibration impact

Splitting sectors should not change OSID match rate. Sectors are derived state; they don't affect territorial control, combat resolution, or displacement. The concern is second-order: if sector offensives become more focused, they might capture different OSIDs. But since sector offensives are currently dormant (year-1), the first implementation phase is risk-free for calibration.

Enabling year-1 sector offensives (Phase 2) WILL affect calibration. That needs a separate 40w run with comparison against n295 baseline. Start with splitting only.

5 — Recommended Implementation
Phased approach. Phase 1 (splitting + reserves) is calibration-safe. Phase 2 (year-1 operations) requires calibration run. Phase 3 (sector naming, UI, operational art) is polish.

Phase 1: Split jumbo sectors + populate reserves

  • A. Split by opposing faction: edges facing different enemies = different sectors
  • B. Split by size: MAX_SECTOR_EDGES = 25. Walk edge chain, split at midpoint. Prefer municipality boundaries as cut points.
  • C. Populate reserve_brigade_ids: interior brigades (not on front edge) marked as reserves up to reserve_fraction. Excess stay assigned.
  • D. MAX_SECTOR_BRIGADES = 8 as secondary cap: if a sector has <25 edges but >8 brigades, split it anyway.

Calibration impact: None. Sectors are derived state. Combat/displacement unchanged.

Phase 2: Enable year-1 sector offensives

  • A. Allow balanced stance to launch sector offensives (currently requires offensive OR balanced, but RS goes balanced at week 20 — verify this path actually works)
  • B. Lower MIN_BRIGADES_FOR_OFFENSIVE from 3 to 2 for small sectors (<15 edges)
  • C. Add corps-level "operational tempo" — a cooldown between sector offensives on the same corps (prevent launching 5 ops simultaneously on 5 sectors)
  • D. Pre-planned operations should chain: after Operacija Koridor completes, 1st Krajina should evaluate launching a follow-up in a different sector

Calibration impact: Medium. Requires 40w comparison run. More targeted operations could improve OSID match rate.

Phase 3: Operational art polish

  • A. Geographic sector naming from municipality centroids (e.g., "Pravac Bihać" instead of "sector:corps:2")
  • B. Sector offensive status in CorpsFrontPanel (planning bar, execution tracker)
  • C. Map visualization: highlight active sector offensive with pulsing glow on the offensive axis
  • D. Sector rotation mechanic: brigades cycle between front and reserve within a sector over time (fatigue management)
