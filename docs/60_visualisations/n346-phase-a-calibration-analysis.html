6 Critical Bug: pool.exhausted Double-Counting
Critical Implementation Bug Detected
pool.exhausted equals pool.committed at end-state for all three factions — exact parity. This means every recruitment/spawn increment adds to both committed and exhausted. The exhaustion gating formula (committed + exhausted) / eligiblePop double-counts living soldiers, causing the gating to fire on wrong inputs.
ARBiH

committed = exhausted

79,159 = 79,159

35,618 = 35,618
committed = exhausted
How the double-counting works
Expected behavior: pool.committed tracks living soldiers currently in formations. pool.exhausted tracks soldiers permanently removed from the pool (killed, missing, captured). These should be independent counters.

Actual behavior: Seven recruitment/spawn sites increment both pool.committed and pool.exhausted simultaneously. Every soldier that enlists is immediately counted as "exhausted" before they ever see combat.

Impact on gating: The formula (committed + exhausted) / eligiblePop fires at 20% (half rate) and 35% (hard cap). With the double-count, the numerator is committed + committed = 2 × committed, making the gate activate at roughly half the intended population consumption. This partially explains why ARBiH is still 37% over target — the gate fires on garbage inputs.

Fix: Remove pool.exhausted += from all 7 recruitment/spawn paths in formation_spawn.ts. Keep only the combat-driven path in applyCasualtyPoolExhaustion().

Phase A Impact Summary
Phase A applied 6 changes targeting three calibration gaps. Military KIA improved dramatically (4.5k → 20k, +343%), nearly reaching the 23.2k target. Civilian killed dropped correctly (64k → 27k, -58%). Personnel bloat improved but remains the biggest gap, primarily due to the pool.exhausted double-counting bug — the exhaustion gating mechanism fires on incorrect inputs, allowing unlimited recruitment.

The OSID match regression (87.6% → 84.9%) is a side effect of increased battle lethality: more battles produce more territorial changes, and some of those changes diverge from historical targets. The recommendations above target both the bug fix and regional OSID corrections that should recover the match rate above 85%.
