## BB-Sourced Figures (Verified)

### ARBiH

1. **April 1992 (war start):** "the Bosnian Government probably started the war with more than 100,000 men but probably only 40,000 to 50,000 small arms and virtually no heavy weapons" [BB1 p.179]

2. **Pre-war Patriotic League:** "Izetbegovic would later state that the Patriotic League had between 30,000 and 40,000 armed personnel at the outset of the war" [BB1 p.168]

3. **August 1992:** "As early as August 1992 the ARBiH was able to field some 170,000 fighting men organized into 28 brigades, 16 independent battalions, 138 miscellaneous detachments, two artillery regiments, and one armored battalion." [BB1 p.216]

4. **Early 1993 (peak):** "By early 1993 the ARBiH had reached its peak strength of 261,500 troops. The great majority of these served in local defense units, but some were organized into specialized maneuver, reconnaissance, and sabotage forces." [BB1 p.216]

5. **Post-peak decline:** "These manpower figures would slowly decline in 1994 and 1995 due to combat losses, work deferments, and other causes." [BB1 p.216]

### VRS

1. **April 1992 (combined Serb forces at war start):**
   - JNA in Bosnia: "some 100,000 to 110,000 troops" [BB1 p.166]
   - Bosnian Serb TO: "almost 60,000 loosely organized personnel" [BB1 p.166]
   - Bosnian Serb MUP: "an estimated 15,000 police -- active, reserve, and special" [BB1 p.166]
   - Combined: ~175,000-185,000 (but significant overlap with JNA being reorganized into VRS)

2. **Initial VRS total:** "The force initially comprised over 250,000 troops" [BB1 p.177]
   - Note: This appears to include ALL Serb forces (JNA remnants + TO + MUP + volunteers) at peak mobilization, not steady-state combat strength.

3. **Also cited:** "Their Bosnian Serb adversaries could, however, if fully mobilized, field more than 200,000 troops already established in dozens of well-organized brigades and support formations" [BB1 p.180]

4. **War's end:** "falling to 155,000 troops by war's end" [BB1 p.177]

5. **Belgrade press reference (not a BB analytical figure):** "One Plane and 80,000 Men" -- title of article by Miroslav Lazanski, Belgrade Politika, 21 June 1993, referenced in BB1 p.199 (footnote 60) and p.202 (footnote 149). This appears to be a Belgrade journalist's figure for the VRS at some point, but BB cites it only as a source reference, not as its own analytical assessment.

6. **Equipment:** "equipped with 500 to 550 tanks, about 250 armored personnel carriers or infantry fighting vehicles, some 500 to 600 field artillery pieces, and 400 to 500 heavy mortars" [BB1 p.177]

### HVO

1. **War start:** "The Bosnian Croat militias, the HOS and HVO, had a combined strength of as many as 25,000 adequately armed troops with a handful of heavy weapons -- and, of course, Croatian Army support." [BB1 p.179-180]

2. **No other BB aggregate figures found for HVO strength over time.** BB does not provide a dedicated HVO aggregate strength figure for 1993, 1994, or 1995 in the pages available in this knowledge base.

## Did ARBiH Exceed VRS + HVO Combined?

### BB Evidence

BB1 p.216 establishes that the ARBiH reached 261,500 troops by early 1993.

BB1 p.177 states VRS "initially comprised over 250,000 troops" but this is a total mobilization figure (including JNA, TO, MUP, and volunteers). The 80,000 figure from Belgrade Politika [BB1 p.199 fn.60] and the 155,000 war's-end figure [BB1 p.177] suggest the steady-state VRS combat strength was significantly lower than 250,000.

The "over 250,000" initial VRS figure is best understood as a peak mobilization number that rapidly declined as:
- Non-Bosnian JNA personnel withdrew to Serbia/Montenegro (May 1992 JNA withdrawal)
- TO and volunteer forces demobilized or were reorganized
- Manpower attrition took hold

**Assessment:** By personnel count alone, the ARBiH likely exceeded the VRS in raw manpower from late 1992 onward, and almost certainly exceeded VRS + HVO combined in total mobilized personnel by mid-1993 (261,500 vs perhaps ~130,000 VRS + ~50,000 HVO = ~180,000). However, as BB1 p.216 notes, the ARBiH "did lack virtually everything else, however, required of an army: training, command expertise, adequate firepower, and true offensive capability." Numerical superiority did not translate to combat effectiveness parity.

**BB does not provide a single direct comparison table.** The assessment above is derived from combining separate BB figures for each faction. The exact timing of when ARBiH exceeded combined VRS+HVO is [UNSOURCED -- no single BB passage establishes this crossover point].

## Key Discrepancies Between OOB Masters and BB

1. **VRS initial strength:** OOB Master says "~80,000 (includes JNA elements)". BB says the JNA alone in Bosnia was 100,000-110,000, plus 60,000 TO, plus 15,000 MUP. The VRS "initially comprised over 250,000 troops" [BB1 p.177]. The 80,000 figure in the OOB Master appears to come from the Lazanski article title "One Plane and 80,000 Men" [BB1 p.199 fn.60], which is a Belgrade journalist's figure, not BB's own analysis. **This is a significant discrepancy requiring resolution.**

2. **VRS end-of-war strength:** OOB Master says "~110,000-120,000". BB says "155,000 troops by war's end" [BB1 p.177]. The OOB Master figure is lower than BB.

3. **ARBiH April 1992:** OOB Master says "~60,000-80,000 (many unarmed or lightly armed)". BB says "more than 100,000 men" [BB1 p.179] with only 40,000-50,000 armed. The OOB Master range is below BB's figure.
