# HRHB Political Goal: "What Is Herceg-Bosna?"

## ICTY-Sourced Research for AWWV Player Decision

**Purpose:** Inform the player decision where the HRHB leader chooses between united front with ARBiH, separate Croat republic, or strategic ambiguity.

---

## 1. ICTY Findings on Herceg-Bosna's Purpose

### Creation (18 November 1991)

The Croatian Community of Herceg-Bosna (HZ H-B) was proclaimed on 18 November 1991 as "a political, cultural, economic and territorial whole" in Bosnia and Herzegovina. The decision was taken at a meeting that called for "a Croatian banovina... as the initial phase leading towards the final solution of the Croatian question and creation of a sovereign Croatia within its ethnic and historical borders." (Kordic Judgment, Para. 472)

### ICTY Trial Chamber Finding (Kordic, Para. 491)

The Trial Chamber rejected defense arguments and found:

> "The HZ H-B was founded with the intention that it should secede from Bosnia and Herzegovina and with a view to unification with Croatia."

### ICTY Prlic Trial Chamber Finding (2013)

The joint criminal enterprise's purpose was:

> "To create a Croat entity, mostly within the borders of the Croatian Banovina as it existed in 1939, to enable a unification of the Croatian people, with these areas later to be either joined with the Republic of Croatia, or remain in close association with it."

The JCE operated from mid-January 1993 to April 1994, pursuing this goal "through ethnic cleansing of the Muslim population."

### Prlic Appeals Chamber Finding (2017)

The Appeals Chamber affirmed that the six convicted defendants participated in a JCE "aimed at creating a Croatian entity in Bosnia and Herzegovina that would facilitate the reunification of the Croatian people, through ethnic cleansing of the Muslim population."

Sentences upheld: Prlic 25 years, Stojic 20, Praljak 20, Petkovic 20, Coric 16, Pusic 10 -- totaling 111 years.

## 2. Zagreb's Directive to Boban

### The Tudjman-Boban Relationship

The ICTY established a clear chain of direction from Zagreb to Herceg-Bosna:

**Kordic Judgment findings (Paras. 472, 474):**
- Regular HDZ-BiH leadership meetings occurred "alternatively in Zagreb and Sarajevo"
- President Tudjman hosted the critical December 1991 strategic meeting where partition policy was formalized
- Mate Boban stated that HZ H-B would "accede to the State of Croatia but only at such time as the Croatian leadership should decide"

**Prlic Trial Chamber findings (2013):**
- Named Franjo Tudjman (President of Croatia), Gojko Susak (Defense Minister), and Janko Bobetko (HV General) as JCE participants alongside Mate Boban
- Found that Croatian Army officers were released to command HVO forces
- Croatia provided logistical support, arms, material, training, and financial support
- HVO reported activities to Croatian authorities

**Prlic Appeals Chamber clarification (2016/2017):**
- The Appeals Chamber reversed the specific finding that Tudjman, Susak, and Bobetko were JCE members, stating the Trial Chamber "made no explicit findings concerning their participation in the JCE" (Appeals Chamber decision, Para. 9)
- However, it upheld that Croatia exercised "overall control" over the HVO, establishing an international armed conflict
- It also upheld that key Croatian leadership "shared the criminal purpose to ethnically cleanse Bosnian Muslims and contributed to realizing that goal"

**Net ICTY position:** Croatia controlled the HVO and its leadership contributed to the criminal enterprise, but the precise legal characterization of Tudjman's JCE membership was contested between chambers.

### The Blaskic Verdict (2000)

The Blaskic Trial Chamber concluded that "Croatia, and more specifically former President Tudjman, was hoping to partition Bosnia and exercised such a degree of control over the Bosnian Croats and especially the HVO that it is justified to speak of overall control."

## 3. The Graz Meeting: Partition Made Explicit

On 6 May 1992, Radovan Karadzic and Mate Boban met in Graz, Austria, to discuss territorial division of Bosnia between Republika Srpska and Herceg-Bosna. The Bosniaks were deliberately excluded.

Key elements:
- Territorial demarcation based on the 1939 borders of the Banovina of Croatia
- Dispute over the Neretva valley and Mostar (referred to EC for arbitration)
- Publicly declared the division and called for end of Serb-Croat conflicts
- No formal agreement was signed; the parties parted without a treaty

This meeting is the clearest evidence that Herceg-Bosna's leadership was pursuing partition in coordination with the Serb side, even as HVO and ARBiH were still cooperating on the ground.

## 4. The "United Front" Alternative: Kljuic vs. Boban

### The HDZ-BiH Split (Kordic Judgment, Para. 472)

By autumn 1991, two factions existed within HDZ-BiH:

**Kljuic Faction (pro-cooperation):**
- Stjepan Kljuic, President of HDZ-BiH
- Position: Croats should remain within Bosnia-Herzegovina as a unified state
- Favored cooperation with Izetbegovic and the Bosnian government
- Supported sovereignty and territorial integrity of Bosnia
- Represented urban Croats, Sarajevo-based moderates, and those who saw Bosnian Croat identity as compatible with Bosnian statehood

**Boban/Kordic Faction (pro-partition):**
- Mate Boban (later replaced Kljuic as HDZ-BiH president)
- Dario Kordic (described as "the most important Bosnian Croat political figure" in central Bosnia)
- Position: division of Bosnia along ethnic lines
- Aligned with Zagreb's directive for Greater Croatia
- Represented Herzegovina hardliners and those oriented toward Croatia

### How the Partition Faction Won

The factional struggle was resolved at a meeting in Zagreb on 27 December 1991 with President Tudjman present:
- Kljuic set out his position favoring Croats remaining within Bosnia
- Boban stated that if BiH disintegrated, HZ H-B would be proclaimed as independent Croatian territory
- Tudjman sided with Boban

**February 1992:** Kljuic resigned as President of HDZ-BiH.
**March 1992:** Mate Boban became President, completing the "ousting of moderates from the leadership" (Kordic Judgment, Para. 474).

The Kordic Trial Chamber described the HDZ-BiH's evolution as: "from a supporter of a sovereign, united Bosnia-Herzegovina in which the Croat nation would be equal, to its role as implementer of a policy initiated in Zagreb, aimed at breaking up the country and negotiating border changes with the Serbs."

### What Genuine Cooperation Would Have Meant

A genuine united front (the Kljuic path) would have meant:
- **Military:** Integrated HVO-ARBiH command structure; joint defense of all BiH territory
- **Political:** Croats as full partners in the Bosnian state, not a parallel entity
- **Territorial:** No Herceg-Bosna; Croat-majority areas governed within a unified framework
- **Diplomatic:** Unified front against Serb aggression; much stronger international position
- **Practical obstacle:** This required Zagreb's consent, which was never forthcoming once Tudjman committed to the Banovina project

## 6. The Vance-Owen Plan as Catalyst

The Vance-Owen Peace Plan (VOPP, January 1993) proposed dividing Bosnia into ten ethnically-based autonomous provinces: three Serb, three Muslim, three Croat, plus Sarajevo.

The plan's impact on the Croat-Bosniak relationship was decisive:
- **Bosnian Croats accepted immediately** (2 January 1993) -- the plan validated their territorial claims
- **HVO declared unilateral implementation** (15 January) even without Bosniak agreement
- **The plan created a territorial imperative:** HVO needed to control the "Croat cantons" before any settlement
- **This triggered the ultimatum** to ARBiH forces in Croat-designated areas to disarm or leave
- **Multiple sources** identify VOPP as the proximate cause of the Croat-Bosniak war

## 7. What "Strategic Ambiguity" Actually Looked Like

During the ambiguity period (late 1991 - early 1993), Herceg-Bosna operated on two tracks simultaneously:

**Public track:**
- HVO as legitimate defense force of BiH
- Cooperation with ARBiH against Serb aggression
- Participation in Bosnian government institutions
- Joint military operations (Mostar liberation, Operation Bura)

**Hidden track:**
- Parallel political structures (HZ H-B with its own presidency, government, currency)
- Arms and supply channels from Zagreb bypassing Bosnian government
- Territorial consolidation in Croat-majority areas
- Boban-Karadzic negotiations at Graz
- HVO commanders reporting to Zagreb, not Sarajevo
- Systematic replacement of pro-cooperation Croat leaders with hardliners

The ICTY found this dual approach was deliberate. The Prlic indictment described the accused as pursuing "a two-track policy toward Bosnia and Herzegovina" where Croatian officials "sometimes made efforts to distance themselves from the Herceg-Bosna/HVO authorities publicly" while pursuing Greater Croatia objectives.

## 8. Game Decision Framework

### Option A: United Front with ARBiH

**Historical basis:** The Kljuic path -- genuine cooperation within a unified Bosnia.

**Modifiers:**
- Military strength bonus: combined HVO-ARBiH forces significantly stronger against VRS
- No Croat-Bosniak war (saves ~10,000+ casualties, preserves central Bosnia)
- Weaker patron relationship with Croatia (Zagreb withdraws support)
- Requires defying Zagreb -- risk of losing HV military support, arms pipeline, financial support
- Diplomatic bonus: unified front strengthens international position
- **Critical constraint:** This choice essentially means breaking with Tudjman. Historically, no Bosnian Croat leader who tried this survived politically (Kljuic was removed within months)

### Option B: Separate Croat Republic

**Historical basis:** The Boban path -- open partition along Banovina lines.

**Modifiers:**
- Zagreb fully supports with arms, money, HV officers, and troops
- Triggers Croat-Bosniak war (historically from January 1993)
- Opens second front: HVO must fight both VRS and ARBiH
- Ethnic cleansing campaign generates international condemnation
- Ultimately unsustainable: Washington Agreement (1994) forced reconciliation
- Risk: overextension -- HVO lacked manpower for two-front war
- Post-war: partition of Bosnia more likely but Croat share may be smaller than hoped

### Option C: Strategic Ambiguity (Historical Path)

**Historical basis:** What actually happened from November 1991 to January 1993.

**Modifiers:**
- Maintains both cooperation and parallel structures simultaneously
- Benefits from joint defense against VRS while building separate territorial control
- Delays but does not prevent the Croat-Bosniak conflict
- Risk: ambiguity collapses when forced to choose (Vance-Owen Plan as trigger)
- Moderate Zagreb support (enough to build HVO, not enough to alienate internationally)
- Timer mechanic: ambiguity has a shelf life. External events (peace plans, Serb pressure, central Bosnia demographics) will force resolution
- **Design note:** This is the richest option for gameplay -- the player manages the tension between cooperation and separation, knowing it cannot last forever
