Three pins, worked up

The pin itself is now a drawn object rather than three rectangles: a flat head with rounded shoulders, a collar, a needle that ends in a point. One glyph, reused in every study, so whichever composition wins, the drawing is already consistent. Everything stays in one ink; the only colour on the page is the board it sits on.

The lockup as a pinned notice

Independent of which composition wins: on the site header and the title screen, the wordmark can sit on a slip of paper held to the board by the three pins. The mark alone stays the three pins; the notice is only for the places where the title appears at full size.

Where I would take it

  • Study 2, convergence, is the one with an idea in it: three needles in one hole. It makes a closed triangular silhouette, which is what a mark needs at 16 pixels, and it is neutral about who the three are.
  • Study 3, the crowd, says the same thing with more mess, which suits the subject but costs legibility at small sizes.
  • Study 5 brings back the map through a front line instead of a border. Nobody's border, everybody's line. Worth keeping as a secondary device for section headers and posters even if it is not the mark.
  • Study 1 is the current afterthought with a better pin. Keep it as the fallback.
  • Whatever wins, the pins stay one ink. Colouring them would turn three pins into three flags.
