Four marks for A War Without Victory

Each mark is drawn from an object the player already handles in the game: the pinned map, the pins, the case-file stamp, the folder. The wordmark is the title screen's own setting, Plex Sans Condensed over a Plex Mono line. Every sheet shows the mark on the two surfaces the site and the game use, the lockups, and the sizes a favicon and an app icon reach.

Replaces the laurel wreath, now used only as the favicon.

A. Pinned map

from the desk map and its pins

The country as a paper cut-out held to the board by one red pin. The silhouette is derived from the game's own municipality polygons, so it is the same Bosnia the player looks at. One shape, one colour accent, reads at 16 pixels as a shape with a red dot.

B. Three pins

from the pins on the desk map

Three pushpins in the board, one per presidency, all the same ink. The idea is the three desks rather than the country. Simplest of the four and the easiest to cut in metal or stitch on a patch, but it leans on the title to say what the three are.

C. Case-file stamp

from the case file on the title screen

A rubber-stamp roundel with the country inside and the place and dates around the ring. It belongs on paper: the cover of a case file, a press kit, a poster corner. The ring text goes at small sizes, so the favicon would be the inner disc alone.

D. Case file

from the folders on every desk

A manila folder with its tab, the country knocked out of the cover. It carries the "assume responsibility" idea of the title screen: a file handed to you. The folder is a familiar icon shape, so the country inside has to do the identifying.
