## Weekly Timeline: April 1992 – January 1993

### Week 1–2 (April 6–19, 1992)

**Events:**
- **6 April:** Independence recognized; Serb barricades in Sarajevo; shots fired at civilian demonstrators near Vrace hill [BB1 p.441 footnote: some date siege from 5 April]
- **8–10 April:** Bijeljina — Arkan's Tigers seize city [GK]
- **9–10 April:** **Zvornik falls** — "capture of Zvornik on 9-10 April" by Bosnian Serb TO with JNA support (BB1 p.187)
- **Mid-April:** **Foča falls** — "Serbs captured Foca in April" (BB1 p.187)
- **Mid-April:** **Višegrad falls** to JNA/VRS [GK; BB1 p.187 confirms Drina valley fighting]
- **16 April:** Bihać established as one of seven territorial districts (okrug) under Bosnian TD Staff (BB1 p.184)
- **April:** Kupres battle [map reference BB1 TOC]
- **April:** JNA formations support SDS political takeovers in Prijedor-Sanski Most-Kljuc region — seizing government offices, police, utilities (BB1 p.181)
- **April–May:** First clashes at Bosanski Brod; HVO/HV stop Serb takeover of municipality (BB1 p.181)

**Territorial picture:** RS seizing major towns in east BiH and Krajina. Corridor region contested — HVO/HV actually pushing Serbs BACK in Posavina. Sarajevo under siege. RS approximately 30–40% by area but fragmented (severed east-west).

---

### Week 3–4 (April 20 – May 3, 1992)

**Events:**
- **Late April:** Prijedor — SDS political takeover complete (BB1 p.181)
- **2 May:** "JNA attacked the city [Sarajevo] directly for the first time and the field artillery shelling began" (BB1 p.441 footnote)
- HVO/HV reinforcements arrive in Posavina; push JNA/Serb TO out of Bosanski Brod, overrun Modriča and Derventa (BB1 p.181)
- East-west route through Posavina **severed by HVO/HV**. "The nascent Serb Republic was cleft in two" (BB1 p.181)
- VRS/JNA hold flanking positions at **Doboj, Bosanski Šamac-Pelagićevo, and Brčko** (BB1 p.182)

**Key fact:** RS was in TWO PIECES at this point. No land connection between Bosanska Krajina and eastern RS/Serbia. This is critical context for Operation Corridor 92.

---

### Week 5–8 (May 4 – May 31, 1992)

**Events:**
- **8–10 May:** **Srebrenica recaptured by ARBiH** — "Serb TO troops had initially seized Srebrenica, but on 8-10 May local Muslim forces, led by the charismatic Naser Oric, drove out the Serbs" (BB1 p.187)
- **By mid-May:** VRS opens road from Zvornik to Serb-held Šekovići — but "they spent the rest of the year blocking repeated Muslim attempts to sever this route, which succeeded at least three or four times" (BB1 p.187)
- **May:** VRS initial efforts to capture Goražde "from the direction of Cajnice in the southeast, came to naught" (BB1 p.187)
- **23 May:** VRS military operations begin in Prijedor area — "invaded several villages and towns near Prijedor, Sanski Most, and Kljuc" (BB1 p.181)
- **30 May:** Muslim/Croat counterattack seizes part of Prijedor city before being overwhelmed (BB1 p.181)
- **12 May:** VRS formally created from JNA Second Military District [GK; BB1 p.177 describes formation]

**Territorial picture:** RS ~45–55% by area. Eastern towns held but Drina valley heavily contested. Srebrenica LOST to VRS. Corridor still severed. Goražde uncaptured. Prijedor area being consolidated through ethnic cleansing.

**Key observation:** The Drina valley was NOT "largely cleared." Orić retook Srebrenica. Goražde held. Multiple ARBiH-held pockets persisted.

---

### Week 9–12 (June 1 – June 28, 1992)

**Events:**
- **Throughout June:** VRS sweeps Prijedor area of non-Serb citizens while redeploying units to other sectors (BB1 p.181)
- **Early June:** 1st Krajina Corps under General Momir Talić begins preliminary operations between Doboj and Derventa (BB1 p.182)
- **20 June:** Kotorsko and Johovac (north of Doboj) fall, clearing the path for corridor operation (BB1 p.182)
- **24 June:** **Operation "Corridor 92" launched** — 1st Krajina Corps first phase, attempting breakthrough to East Bosnian Corps NE of Gradačac (BB1 p.182)
- **26 June:** Breakthrough achieved — "Two days of heavy fighting enabled the units to link up and cut the connection between enemy-held Modrica and Gradacac" (BB1 p.182)
- **28 June:** **Modriča falls** to VRS (BB1 p.182)
- Supporting attacks near Derventa–Bosanski Brod "achieved little gain against strong HV/HVO resistance" (BB1 p.182)
- VRS preparations begin for Jajce operation "during June and July" (BB1 p.183)

**Territorial picture:** RS ~50–58%. Corridor breakthrough achieved but narrow and unsecured. Bosanski Brod still HV/HVO. Derventa still HV/HVO. RS now physically connected but tenuously.

**Key fact:** Operation Corridor 92 was "a large operation lasting over six months" (BB1 p.177) — not a single-week event.

---

### Week 13–16 (July 1 – July 28, 1992)

**Events:**
- **4 July:** VRS launches three-pronged attack toward Derventa, Bosanski Brod, Odžak, and Sava River (BB1 p.182)
- **4–5 July:** **Derventa retaken** by VRS (BB1 p.182)
- **12 July:** **Odžak seized** by VRS (BB1 p.182)
- **14 July:** VRS reaches Sava River; other units push to within 10 km of Bosanski Brod (BB1 p.182)
- HV/HVO "forced into a now constricted bridgehead around Bosanski Brod itself" (BB1 p.182)
- **Late July:** VRS "final operation" in Prijedor area — mopping up last Muslim hamlets in forested mountains (BB1 p.181)
- **Late July:** ARBiH "major offensive... seizing the passage to Gorazde and pushing VRS troops out of Trnovo" (BB1 p.187)
- Drina valley: "battles for hilltops and passes in this area [Cajnice-Goražde] sputtered on until July" (BB1 p.187)
- Jajce: "Preliminary operations in July... failed to gain significant ground" (BB1 p.183)

**Territorial picture:** RS ~55–62%. Major Posavina gains. But Bosanski Brod still holding. Jajce still held. ARBiH retaking ground in Drina valley (Trnovo).

**Key observation:** VRS losing ground in one theater (Drina valley — Trnovo) while gaining in another (Posavina). This multi-front strain would characterize the entire war.

---

### Week 17–20 (July 29 – August 25, 1992)

**Events:**
- **Mid-August:** Jajce campaign "began in earnest" — VRS launches first major attack, advancing to within 2 km of town (BB1 p.183)
- VRS deploys 7,000–8,000 troops from 30th Infantry Division, 1st Krajina Corps. 20–30 tanks, 30–50 field artillery >100mm (BB1 p.183)
- Defenders: "over 5,500 highly motivated HVO and Bosnian Territorial Defense troops" (BB1 p.183)
- Attacks around Bosanski Brod during August achieve "only limited success" (BB1 p.182)
- Drina valley: ARBiH attacks from late August begin retaking territory near Višegrad (BB1 p.187)

**Territorial picture:** RS ~58–63%. Jajce under sustained pressure. Corridor expanding but Bosanski Brod bridgehead still resisting.

---

### Week 21–24 (August 26 – September 22, 1992)

**Events:**
- **Early/mid-September:** Jajce — VRS seizes "another piece of ground to the southwest, pushing to within a kilometer of Jajce" (BB1 p.183)
- Simultaneous HVO-TO counterattack north of Bugojno and Novi Travnik to divert VRS "failed to gain any ground or affect the VRS assault" (BB1 p.183)
- **Mid-September:** HVO and ARBiH troops **cut the corridor link south of Orašje**. "VRS troops were able to reopen the route" (BB1 p.182)
- **27 September:** VRS begins new attack on Bosanski Brod bridgehead — "only small initial gains" (BB1 p.182)
- Drina valley: ARBiH offensive continues near Višegrad — "series of Bosnian Army attacks from late August to November retook key territory around Visegrad" (BB1 p.187)

**Territorial picture:** RS ~60–65%. Jajce nearly surrounded. Corridor contested. Drina valley ARBiH counteroffensive progressing.

---

### Week 25–28 (September 23 – October 19, 1992)

**Events:**
- **4 October:** VRS shifts attack sector at Bosanski Brod, breaks through HV/HVO lines (BB1 p.182)
- **6 October:** **Bosanski Brod falls** to VRS (BB1 p.182)
- **9 October:** Croat-VRS ceasefire signed at Jajce (BB1 p.183)
- **Mid-October:** Corridor **cut again** by HVO/ARBiH (BB1 p.182)
- **21 October:** Croat and Muslim units at Jajce main supply route "opened fire on each other" (BB1 p.183)
- Drina: ARBiH offensive near Višegrad continues — "placed ARBiH troops less than 3 kilometers from a key hydroelectric dam on the Drina River and within the same distance of the town itself" (BB1 p.187)

**Territorial picture:** RS ~63–67%. Bosanski Brod and Jajce about to fall = near-peak RS extent.

---

### Week 28–32 (October 20 – November 16, 1992)

**Events:**
- **25 October:** VRS launches final push on Jajce, "attacking hard along all three axes" (BB1 p.183)
- **29 October:** **Jajce falls** — "VRS troops entered the center of town... some 40,000 panicked civilians made their way out of the enclave to Travnik" (BB1 p.183-184)
- **Early November:** HVO units **cut the corridor road again** (BB1 p.182)
- **November:** VRS offensive to clear Orašje pocket and secure corridor permanently **"failed, and HV/HVO forces drove the Serb attack units back in heavy fighting and terrible weather"** (BB1 p.182)
- Drina: Orić continues "steadily widening government control in the area" (BB1 p.187)

**Territorial picture:** RS **~65–68%** — peak for 1992. But this is precarious: corridor still 3 km wide, under constant threat.

---

### Week 33–40 (November 17 – January 25, 1993)

**Events:**
- **Mid-to-late November:** VRS attempts to cut Goražde supply corridor — "achieved only rare and temporary successes" (BB1 p.187)
- **Mid-to-late December:** VRS 1st Krajina and East Bosnian Corps attack west of Brčko, "driving them back about 2 to 3 kilometers, giving the Serbs a bit more room" (BB1 p.182)
- **14–15 December:** Orić launches preliminary attack east of Bratunac — Voljavica, Bjelovac, Sikirića (BB2 p.404)
- **24 December:** Orić's main assault begins against Bratunac–Konjevic Polje road (BB2 p.404)
- **7 January 1993:** **Orić's troops overrun Kravica** on Orthodox Christmas (BB2 p.404)
- Orić's force: 4,000–6,000 men in three brigades, ~3,000 armed (BB2 p.404)

**Territorial picture:** RS **~63–67%**. Slight contraction from peak due to:
1. Corridor cut multiple times, VRS November offensive failed (BB1 p.182)
2. Orić's Srebrenica offensive expanding ARBiH-held territory (BB1 p.187, BB2 p.404)
3. VRS unable to take Goražde supply route
4. Corridor "remained a bare 3 kilometers wide at its narrowest point, southwest of Brcko town. Widening this portion of the vital route would have to wait until 1993" (BB1 p.182)

## Key Historical Facts

### VRS Force Structure (1992)
- "Initially comprised over 250,000 troops falling to 155,000 troops by war's end" (BB1 p.177)
- "500 to 550 tanks, about 250 armored personnel carriers or infantry fighting vehicles, some 500 to 600 field artillery pieces, and 400 to 500 heavy mortars" (BB1 p.177)
- "Main Staff and five regional corps (later joined by a sixth) with some 80 maneuver brigades and regiments" by end 1992 (BB1 p.177)
- Air Force: "20 fighter-bombers, 15 light attack and observation helicopters, and 15 transport helicopters" (BB1 p.177)
- "formed through the melding of two distinct forces, superimposing the structure of the JNA's Second Military District over the disorganized, SDS-controlled Bosnian Serb Territorial Defense" (BB1 p.177)
- First priority: "convert the mob of TO personnel into properly organized, well-led light infantry brigades, while simultaneously reining in many of the virtually autonomous volunteer units" (BB1 p.177)

### VRS Strategic Objectives (1992)
Six main campaigns, in rough priority order (BB1 p.177):
1. **Posavina Corridor** — "most important" — link western BiH to eastern BiH and Serbia. "Over six months" of operations.
2. **Jajce** — capture town + two hydroelectric plants "vital to economic viability of Serb-controlled western Bosnia"
3. **Western Bosnia ethnic cleansing** — Prijedor/Sanski Most/Ključ — "to secure the area against a Muslim-Croat military threat that existed only in Serb propaganda"
4. **Sarajevo siege** — "seize parts of the city for a Bosnian Serb capital and, more important, maintain pressure on the Bosnian Government"
5. **Drina valley** — "smaller operations swept the length of the Drina valley against strong Bosnian Government resistance, often alternating between the defensive and offensive"
6. **Bihać** — "unsuccessfully attempted to secure one of the 'borders' of Republika Srpska along the Una River"
7. **Herzegovina** — "purely defensive mode, defending the 'borders' against HV/HVO operations around Mostar and Trebinje"

### Posavina Corridor — Key Characteristics
- "The perpetual threat that Bosnian Army and Bosnian Croat Army forces might retake the corridor... forced the VRS to station a sizable portion of the troop reserves from its best corps to safeguard it" (BB1 p.183)
- End of 1992: "corridor remained a bare 3 kilometers wide at its narrowest point" (BB1 p.182)
- Corridor was cut at least 3 times (mid-Sept, mid-Oct, early Nov) by HVO/ARBiH forces (BB1 p.182)
- November VRS offensive to permanently secure Orašje pocket **failed** (BB1 p.182)
- "None of the subsequent clashes in the area would be on the scale of the 1992 campaign" (BB1 p.183)

### Drina Valley — Key Characteristics
- **NOT a VRS rear area in 1992** — active, contested theater with seesaw fighting
- Srebrenica: recaptured by Orić 8-10 May; Orić "steadily widening government control" through 1992 (BB1 p.187)
- Zvornik-Šekovići route cut by ARBiH "at least three or four times" (BB1 p.187)
- Goražde: VRS couldn't capture it in May; ARBiH opened supply route in late July (BB1 p.187)
- ARBiH counteroffensive near Višegrad Aug-Nov retook key territory (BB1 p.187)
- Cerska-Kamenica pocket survived through 1992 into Feb-Mar 1993 (BB2 p.404-406)
- VRS "decided to make the capture of the Drina valley its strategic focus for 1993" (BB1 p.187) — proving it was NOT captured in 1992

### Jajce Campaign — Key Characteristics
- 7,000–8,000 VRS troops (30th Infantry Division, 1st Krajina Corps) vs 5,500+ HVO/TO (BB1 p.183)
- VRS advantage: 20-30 tanks, 30-50 field guns >100mm (BB1 p.183)
- "Slow campaign" — careful step-by-step VRS doctrine (BB1 p.183)
- Campaign: mid-August to 29 October = ~10 weeks
- Fall precipitated by: (1) HVO-VRS ceasefire 9 Oct, (2) Croat-Muslim infighting 21 Oct, (3) VRS final push 25-29 Oct
- "over 5,500 highly motivated HVO and Bosnian Territorial Defense troops" — defenders fought well, "yielding only after four days of heavy fighting" (BB1 p.183)
- 40,000 civilians fled to Travnik (BB1 p.184)

### Bihać Pocket 1992
- ~2,000 sq km (4% of BiH) (BB1 p.184)
- ~250,000 residents, ~90% Muslim (BB1 p.184)
- 5th Corps (from "Una-Sanska Operational Group") formally established 20 October 1992 under Ramiz Dreković (BB1 p.186)
- Violence came ~2 weeks later than rest of BiH (BB1 p.184)
- Strong local defense thanks to existing TD structure + large Muslim majority (BB1 p.184)

## Observations for Sim Calibration

### What the sim should reproduce:
1. **RS in two pieces** until late June — corridor operations are existentially important
2. **Drina valley as active warzone** throughout 1992 — NOT a VRS rear area
3. **Srebrenica recaptured by ARBiH** in early May — major ARBiH success in Drina
4. **Jajce falls late** (w28) — slow VRS campaign, defender infighting contributes
5. **Corridor never fully secure** — 3 km wide at narrowest, cut repeatedly
6. **RS territorial peak ~65-68%** reached only after Jajce (w28), NOT earlier
7. **RS slight contraction** Nov-Dec from failed operations + Orić's offensive
8. **VRS tempo declining** from September onward — multi-front strain, exhaustion
9. **ARBiH meaningful counteroffensives** from July onward (Trnovo, Višegrad area, Srebrenica expansion)
10. **50,000+ troops** engaged in Posavina alone — VRS's largest 1992 campaign

### What the standup got wrong:
- "RS reaches ~45% by end April" → More like 30-40%, and RS was severed
- "Drina valley largely cleared by May" → Flatly wrong; Srebrenica recaptured by ARBiH in May
- "Operation Corridor 92 June (w9-12)" → Started 24 June, lasted through December+
- "RS reaches ~60-65% by June" → More like 50-58% at end June
- Monotonically increasing RS territory → Actually peaked ~w28-32, then slightly contracted
