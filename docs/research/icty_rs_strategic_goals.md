# RS Six Strategic Goals: ICTY-Sourced Research Document

**Purpose:** Foundational reference for an AWWV player decision event. The player (as RS political leader) chooses which of the 6 strategic goals to adopt. Each goal maps to specific military operations and consequences.

**Primary Sources:** ICTY Trial Judgments (Karadzic IT-95-5/18, Mladic IT-09-92, Krajisnik IT-00-39, Krstic IT-98-33), ICJ Bosnia v. Serbia Judgment (2007), expert testimony of Robert Donia.

**Secondary Sources:** Balkan Insight, IWPR, SENSE Agency trial reporting, Foreign Policy.

---

## 1. The Six Strategic Objectives: Exact Wording

Adopted at the **16th Session of the Assembly of the Serbian People in Bosnia and Herzegovina**, held in Banja Luka on **12 May 1992**. Published in the Official Gazette of Republika Srpska on 26 November 1993 (retroactively). Executed (signed) by Momcilo Krajisnik, President of the RS National Assembly.

The document title is: **"Decision on Strategic Objectives of the Serbian People in Bosnia and Herzegovina"**

Source: ICJ, Application of the Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide (Bosnia and Herzegovina v. Serbia and Montenegro), Judgment of 26 February 2007, para. 372; multiple ICTY indictments and judgments.

### The Six Objectives (Exact Formulation)

> **1.** Establish State borders separating the Serbian people from the other two ethnic communities.
>
> **2.** Set up a corridor between Semberija and Krajina.
>
> **3.** Establish a corridor in the Drina River valley, that is, eliminate the Drina as a border separating Serbian States.
>
> **4.** Establish a border on the Una and Neretva Rivers.
>
> **5.** Divide the city of Sarajevo into Serbian and Bosnian Muslim parts and establish effective State authorities in both parts.
>
> **6.** Ensure access to the sea for Republika Srpska.

Source: ICJ Judgment para. 372, citing ICTY evidence; Karadzic Trial Judgment (24 March 2016); Borovcanin Indictment Annex; Tolimir Indictment; Beara Indictment; Popovic Indictment.

## 2. Assembly Discussion and Context

### Karadzic's Presentation

At the 16th Session, Karadzic presented the goals and explained:

- "It is very important but there are things that are more important than others or more feasible than others." (Karadzic Trial Judgment Summary)
- Krajisnik, presiding, stated: **"The first goal is the most important one and in relation to all other goals, all other goals are sub-items of the first one."** This establishes the hierarchy: separation is the master objective; goals 2-6 are territorial means of achieving it.
- Regarding the third goal (Drina valley), Karadzic stated it was "territorial and expressed the will of the Bosnian Serbs to have their strategic interests on both sides of the Drina River."

### Expert Testimony: Robert Donia

American historian Robert Donia testified as an expert witness at the Karadzic trial (1 June 2010) and the Mladic trial. Key findings from his testimony:

- The **overarching objective** was "separation" -- to dramatically alter the ethnic distribution of Bosnia-Herzegovina by attaching human residents to territory in a fundamentally different way than existed in 1991.
- The first goal "failed to specify the separation method, but all four forms of separation were specified in the remaining goals" -- territorial, physical, institutional, and human.
- The goals were not spontaneous; the SDS pursued them even before formal adoption, through speeches by Karadzic and other officials.
- The SDS implemented the goals through a **"municipality strategy"** -- rewriting municipal borders to create territories where Serbs held majorities, implemented through **"Variant A and B" instructions** distributed 19 December 1991.

### The Pre-War Apparatus: Variant A and B

The "Instructions for the Organisation and Operation of the Organs of Serb People in Bosnia and Herzegovina in Emergency Conditions" (19 December 1991):

- **Variant A**: For municipalities with a Serb majority -- seize power through crisis staffs.
- **Variant B**: For municipalities with a Serb minority -- establish parallel Serb institutions, prepare for separation.
- At a meeting in February 1992, Karadzic demanded municipal leaders "expedite the implementation of Variant A in order to gain effective power over every millimeter of our land."
- Crisis staffs became the local executive organs that implemented ethnic cleansing once war began.

Source: ICTY Karadzic Trial; Donia testimony (100601IT transcript); Balkan Insight (15 April 2019).

## 3. Mladic's Genocide Warning

### The Quote

At the same 16th Session on 12 May 1992, General Ratko Mladic (newly appointed VRS commander) addressed the assembly:

> "We cannot cleanse, nor can we have a sieve to sift so that only Serbs would stay, or that the Serbs would fall through and the rest leave. I do not know how Mr. Krajisnik and Mr. Karadzic would explain this to the world. **People, that would be genocide.**"

Source: RS Assembly session minutes, cited in ICTY Mladic trial proceedings; Balkan Insight (20 September 2013); IWPR (Mladic trial reporting); testimony of General Manojlo Milovanovic at The Hague.

### Context and Interpretation

- **Who was present:** The full Bosnian Serb Assembly, including Karadzic (President), Krajisnik (Assembly Speaker), Koljevic, Plavsic, and assembled delegates. Mladic addressed them as the newly appointed military commander.
- **Timing:** This was 12 May 1992 -- one month after armed conflict began (6 April 1992). The VRS was formally established on this same day.
- **Interpretation per ICTY:** Historian Robert Donia testified that Mladic's reference to genocide "stands out as a particularly harsh warning against taking these actions and [he is] fearful of the consequences of it. He's using the word here for its shock value because it doesn't appear in very many other contexts with this application."
- **Did it change the vote?** No. The strategic objectives were adopted. The assembly proceeded to formalize them as official policy.
- **Did anyone object?** No record of formal objection in the assembly minutes. The goals were adopted by acclamation.

### The Contradiction: Warning vs. Implementation

The Foreign Policy analysis (15 June 2012) documents the shift:

- **May 1992:** After visiting Kalinovik, Mladic ordered the removal of JNA artillery threatening Muslim villages, seemingly honoring promises to protect civilians.
- **August 1992:** The same artillery pieces were repositioned and used to bombard those same Muslim settlements.
- **By summer 1992:** Kalinovik municipality saw systematic violence -- mass detention, sexual assault, killings of male detainees, torture, forced deportation, destruction of mosques.
- **April 1995 (50th RS Assembly session):** Mladic himself stated that military operations "proceeded from the six strategic goals." By this point, the warning had been completely superseded by dedicated implementation.

Source: Foreign Policy (15 June 2012); Balkan Insight (20 September 2013); SENSE Agency.

## 4. Goal-by-Goal: Military Implementation

### Goal 1: Separation of Peoples

**What it drove:**
- The overarching ethnic cleansing campaign across all of claimed RS territory (October 1991 - November 1995).
- Municipality-level takeovers via crisis staffs established under Variant A/B.
- Systematic pattern across 20+ municipalities: (a) ultimatum to surrender weapons, (b) shelling, (c) infantry assault, (d) separation of men from women/children, (e) detention camps (Omarska, Keraterm, Trnopolje, Manjaca, Luka), (f) killings, (g) expulsion of remaining population.

**ICTY Findings:**
- Karadzic Trial Judgment (24 March 2016): Found guilty of participating in the "overarching joint criminal enterprise" (JCE) whose objective was "to permanently remove Bosnian Muslims and Bosnian Croats from Bosnian Serb claimed territory" (paras. 3521, 3524, 5996).
- This JCE operated from at least October 1991 to 30 November 1995.
- Karadzic bore both direct (Article 7(1)) and command (Article 7(3)) responsibility.

**Corps involved:** All VRS corps participated in the ethnic cleansing campaign within their respective zones.

**Moderate alternative:** Civic Bosnian Serbs, including those aligned with Ante Markovic's Union of Reform Forces (whose Bosnian branch was led by Nenad Kecmanovic), advocated remaining within a multiethnic Bosnia-Herzegovina. Kecmanovic briefly served as the Serb member of the BiH Presidency (June-July 1992) before the SDS consolidated control. The Social Democratic Party (SDP) also represented a non-nationalist alternative but was marginalized by the institutional dominance of ethnic parties.

### Goal 2: Posavina Corridor (Semberija to Krajina)

**What it drove:**
- **Operation Corridor 92** (24 June - 6 October 1992): The largest single VRS operation of the war.
- Objective: Re-establish road link between Banja Luka (western RS) and eastern RS/Serbia through the narrow Posavina region.
- Captured approximately 760 km2 of territory, including Modrica, Derventa, Odzak, and areas around Brcko.

**Corps involved:**
- **1st Krajina Corps** (General Momir Talic): Main effort. Organized into four Tactical Groups, 16th Krajina Motorized Brigade, and Operational Group Doboj.
- **East Bosnia Corps** elements: Supporting role from the eastern end.

**ICTY Findings:**
- Operation Corridor 92 was directly linked to Strategic Objective 2. ICTY proceedings documented ethnic cleansing in northeastern Posavina during and after the operation.
- The corridor remained strategically critical throughout the war -- its narrowest point near Brcko was under constant threat.

**Moderate alternative:** A negotiated transport/communications corridor through BiH territory with international guarantees, as proposed in various peace plans (Cutileiro Plan, Vance-Owen Plan). This would have avoided the military conquest and ethnic cleansing required to physically hold the corridor.

### Goal 3: Drina Valley / Eliminate the Drina as Border

**What it drove:**
- The ethnic cleansing of Eastern Bosnia (Bijeljina, Zvornik, Visegrad, Foca, Rogatica, Bratunac, Srebrenica region) in spring-summer 1992.
- The prolonged siege and attacks on the Srebrenica, Zepa, and Gorazde enclaves (1992-1995).
- Ultimately, the Srebrenica genocide (July 1995).

**Key Directives:**
- **Operational Directive 4** (19 November 1992, issued by Mladic): Ordered the Drina Corps to "inflict the heaviest possible losses on the enemy, and force him to leave the Birac, Zepa and Gorazde areas together with the Bosnian Muslim population. First, offer the able-bodied and armed men to surrender, and if they refuse, destroy them."
- **Directive 7** (8 March 1995, issued by Karadzic as Supreme Commander): Directed the VRS to "complete the physical separation of Srebrenica from Zepa as soon as possible" and to "create an unbearable situation of total insecurity with no hope of further survival or life for the inhabitants of Srebrenica."
- **Directive 7.1** (31 March 1995, issued by Mladic): Implementing order for Directive 7, directing the Drina Corps to "conduct active combat operations around the enclaves."

**Corps involved:**
- **Drina Corps** (Commander: General Milenko Zivanovic, then General Radislav Krstic from 13 July 1995): Primary responsibility for Eastern Bosnia and the enclaves.

**ICTY Findings:**
- Krstic Trial Judgment (2 August 2001): Established the link between the strategic objectives and the Srebrenica operations.
- Karadzic convicted of genocide at Srebrenica (para. 6005 -- Article 7(3) responsibility for failing to punish killings by subordinates).
- Mladic convicted of genocide at Srebrenica (22 November 2017, upheld on appeal 8 June 2021).
- The "Srebrenica JCE" (July-November 1995) was found to be a separate joint criminal enterprise targeting the elimination of Bosnian Muslims from Srebrenica through killing men/boys and forcibly removing the rest.

**Moderate alternative:** Acceptance of international protections for Serb minorities in the Drina valley within a unified or cantonized Bosnia, as envisioned by the Lisbon/Cutileiro Agreement (February 1992) which proposed ethnic cantons with minority protections. The Drina as a state border between BiH and Serbia would remain, but with open crossing and economic integration.

### Goal 4: Borders on Una and Neretva Rivers

**What it drove:**
- Operations in the Bihac pocket (northwestern Bosnia) to push toward the Una River.
- Operations in Herzegovina to push toward the Neretva River.
- This was the most geographically ambitious goal, claiming massive territorial expansion.

**Corps involved:**
- **2nd Krajina Corps**: Operations around Bihac pocket, attempting to reach the Una.
- **Herzegovina Corps**: Operations in eastern Herzegovina toward the Neretva.
- **1st Krajina Corps**: Western Krajina operations.

**ICTY Findings:**
- This goal was largely unrealized militarily. The Bihac pocket held throughout the war despite repeated sieges. Herzegovina operations were constrained by the RS-HRHB dynamic (initially cooperative, later conflictual).
- The goal established the framework for VRS operations in western and southern Bosnia.

**Moderate alternative:** Accept existing inter-entity boundaries based on the ethnic composition of municipalities rather than river-based borders, which would have required conquering large non-Serb majority areas.

### Goal 5: Division of Sarajevo

**What it drove:**
- The **Siege of Sarajevo** (5 April 1992 - 29 February 1996): The longest siege of a capital city in modern warfare (1,425 days).
- Systematic sniping and shelling campaign against the civilian population.
- The Sarajevo-Romanija Corps maintained positions on hills encircling the city.

**Corps involved:**
- **Sarajevo-Romanija Corps (SRK)**: Primary responsibility. Held the siege ring with forces positioned on Trebevic, Pale, Ilidza, Grbavica, Vogosca, Ilijas, and Hadzici.
- Commanders convicted: General Stanislav Galic (terror against civilians), General Dragomir Milosevic (terror against civilians).

**ICTY Findings:**
- **Sarajevo JCE** (April 1992 - November 1995): A campaign of sniping and shelling against the civilian population with intent "to spread terror" (Karadzic Trial Judgment paras. 4632-4634).
- SRK deliberately sniped at civilians including trams, "on an almost daily basis throughout the entirety of the conflict."
- Starting late May 1992, SRK units used heavy weapons (80mm and 120mm mortars, artillery) from surrounding hills.
- Two Markale market shellings: 5 February 1994 (67+ killed), 28 August 1995 (43 killed).
- Karadzic convicted of terror against civilians in Sarajevo. Sentenced to life imprisonment (increased from 40 years on appeal, 20 March 2019).
- Four individuals convicted specifically for Sarajevo siege crimes: Galic, D. Milosevic, Karadzic, Mladic.

**Moderate alternative:** Power-sharing arrangements for Sarajevo with guaranteed representation for all communities, as proposed in multiple peace plans. A unified Sarajevo under joint governance rather than physical partition. The Dayton Agreement ultimately kept Sarajevo unified under Federation control.

### Goal 6: Access to the Sea

**What it drove:**
- Operations in eastern Herzegovina attempting to reach the Adriatic coast through the Neum corridor area.
- This was the least achievable goal given the geographic and military realities.

**Corps involved:**
- **Herzegovina Corps**: Responsible for southern operations.

**ICTY Findings:**
- This goal was never militarily achieved. The Herzegovina Corps was occupied with operations against both ARBiH and HVO forces and never reached the coast.
- It remained aspirational throughout the war and was abandoned in peace negotiations.

**Moderate alternative:** Negotiated port access through Croatian territory (similar to the Neum corridor arrangement that exists today) or through Montenegro, rather than military conquest of coastal territory.

## 5. Translation into VRS Directives

### The Directive Chain

The ICTY found that **Mladic issued nine operational directives** implementing the six strategic goals into orders for VRS combat operations throughout the war (Mladic Trial, prosecution case).

Key directives documented in ICTY proceedings:

| Directive | Date | Issuer | Primary Goals Addressed | Key Orders |
|-----------|------|--------|------------------------|------------|
| Directive 1 | June 1992 | Mladic/Main Staff | Goals 1-6 | Initial VRS operational framework |
| Directive 2 | ~1992 | Main Staff | Multiple | Continuation of operations |
| Directive 3 | ~1993 | Main Staff | Multiple | Operational adjustments |
| **Directive 4** | **19 Nov 1992** | **Mladic** | **Goal 3 (Drina)** | **Drina Corps to force Muslims from E. Bosnia; "destroy" those who refuse surrender** |
| Directive 5 | ~1993 | Main Staff | Multiple | Operational guidance |
| Directive 6 | ~1993-94 | Main Staff | Multiple | Continuation |
| **Directive 7** | **8 Mar 1995** | **Karadzic** | **Goal 3 (Drina/Srebrenica)** | **"Create unbearable situation" in Srebrenica; separate Srebrenica from Zepa** |
| **Directive 7.1** | **31 Mar 1995** | **Mladic** | **Goal 3** | **Implementing order for Dir. 7; Drina Corps active combat around enclaves** |
| Directive 8 | 1995 | Main Staff | Multiple | Late-war operations |

Note: Directives 1-3, 5-6, and 8 are referenced in ICTY prosecution cases but their full text was less prominently featured in public trial reporting than Directives 4, 7, and 7.1. The table above reconstructs the known directive chain; exact dates for less-documented directives require access to the full trial record.

## 6. ICTY Command Responsibility Findings

### Karadzic (IT-95-5/18)

- **Convicted:** 24 March 2016 (Trial); Life sentence confirmed on appeal 20 March 2019.
- **Counts:** Genocide (Srebrenica), persecution, extermination, murder, deportation, inhumane acts (forcible transfer), terror (Sarajevo), unlawful attacks on civilians, taking of hostages.
- **Acquitted:** Genocide in municipalities outside Srebrenica (insufficient evidence of specific intent to destroy, though widespread persecution was proven).
- **Role:** President of RS, President of SDS, Supreme Commander of VRS armed forces. Constitutional power to "organise and implement plans for defence, order mobilisation, command and control the army."
- **Four JCEs:** Overarching (ethnic cleansing), Sarajevo (terror), Srebrenica (genocide), Hostages (UN personnel).
- **Key paragraphs:** 3521, 3524 (overarching JCE objective), 4632-4634 (Sarajevo JCE), 5996, 6002-6005 (Srebrenica findings, Article 7(3) failure to punish).

### Mladic (IT-09-92)

- **Convicted:** 22 November 2017 (Trial); Upheld on appeal 8 June 2021.
- **Counts:** Genocide (Srebrenica), persecution, extermination, murder, deportation, inhumane acts, terror (Sarajevo), unlawful attacks, taking of hostages.
- **Role:** Commander of VRS Main Staff. Issued operational directives implementing the six strategic goals. Direct command authority over all corps.
- **Key finding:** Mladic stated at the 50th RS Assembly session (April 1995) that VRS operations "proceeded from the six strategic goals." Despite his May 1992 warning about genocide, he became the primary military implementer of the goals.

### Krajisnik (IT-00-39)

- **Convicted:** 27 September 2006.
- **Counts:** Deportation, persecution, forced transfer (other counts including extermination dropped on appeal).
- **Sentence:** Reduced from 27 to 20 years on appeal (17 March 2009).
- **Role:** President of the RS Assembly. Signed the Decision on Strategic Objectives. Key member of the expanded Presidency that oversaw implementation.

## 7. The Moderate Alternative: What Could Have Been

### The Civic Position

Several paths existed that moderate Bosnian Serb politicians advocated:

1. **Ante Markovic's Reform Communists:** The Union of Reform Forces, whose Bosnian branch was led by Nenad Kecmanovic, advocated remaining within a multiethnic, market-oriented Yugoslavia and then a multiethnic Bosnia. Kecmanovic briefly served as Serb member of the BiH Presidency (June-July 1992) but was marginalized.

2. **SDP (Social Democrats):** Advocated civic (non-ethnic) governance. Won significant support in 1990 elections but were overwhelmed by the nationalist triad (SDS/SDA/HDZ).

3. **Cutileiro/Lisbon Plan (February 1992):** Before the war, EU mediator Jose Cutileiro brokered agreement on ethnic cantons within a sovereign BiH. All three sides initially accepted, then Izetbegovic withdrew (reportedly under US pressure). This represented the last chance for a negotiated ethnic separation without war.

4. **Serb intellectuals in Sarajevo:** Many Bosnian Serbs (estimated 50,000-80,000) remained in government-held Sarajevo throughout the war, rejecting the SDS program. They formed no organized political movement but their existence disproved the SDS claim that coexistence was impossible.

### What "Adoption of Goals" Meant in Practice

The strategic goals were not merely aspirational. Their adoption at the 16th Session constituted:
- **Official state policy** of Republika Srpska
- **Military doctrine** for the VRS (translated into operational directives)
- **Justification framework** for ethnic cleansing at the municipal level
- **Negotiating position** for all peace talks (used to reject plans that didn't satisfy the goals)

A player choosing to reject or modify the goals would face:
- Loss of support from hardline SDS delegates
- Potential removal by the assembly
- Military insubordination (Mladic and corps commanders were committed to territorial expansion)
- Pressure from Belgrade (Milosevic initially supported the goals, though he later pivoted to pragmatism)

A player choosing to adopt the goals with restraint (e.g., pursue through negotiation rather than force) would face:
- The Variant A/B apparatus was already in motion at the municipal level
- Paramilitaries (Arkan's Tigers, White Eagles) operated semi-independently
- Corps commanders had operational autonomy
- The momentum of ethnic cleansing, once started, proved impossible to control

## 10. Event Design Notes (For AWWV Implementation)

### Decision Structure

The player event should present the goals as a package (as they were historically), but allow the player to:
1. **Adopt all six** (historical path -- triggers full VRS offensive posture)
2. **Adopt selectively** (e.g., prioritize corridor + Sarajevo, deprioritize sea access -- changes corps directive priorities)
3. **Adopt with restraint** (pursue through negotiation -- reduces aggression modifiers but risks hardliner coup)
4. **Reject** (civic alternative -- triggers SDS assembly revolt, potential player removal)

### Consequences Mapping

Each goal should map to:
- **Corps directives** (which corps gets offensive/balanced/defensive stance)
- **Aggression modifiers** (faction-level and corps-level)
- **International reputation** (affects patron support, sanctions)
- **Internal stability** (assembly loyalty, military obedience)
- **Ethnic cleansing trigger** (automatic consequence of goals 1, 3, 5 when pursued militarily)

### Mladic's Warning as Sub-Event

Present Mladic's warning as a moment of player choice:
- **Acknowledge the warning** (slight restraint on implementation, but goals still adopted)
- **Dismiss the warning** (full speed ahead -- slight morale boost to hardliners)
- **Amplify the warning** (attempt to moderate the goals -- risks Mladic's loyalty)

### Historical Accuracy Notes

- The goals were adopted unanimously with no recorded dissent
- Mladic's warning did not prevent him from becoming the primary implementer
- The goals remained official policy throughout the war
- All peace plan positions were evaluated against achievement of the goals
- The Dayton Agreement (November 1995) gave RS approximately 49% of BiH territory -- partially satisfying goals 1-4, rejecting goals 5-6
