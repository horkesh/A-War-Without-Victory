BB1, printed folio 147-148  (KB page 183-184; repo citation "BB1 p.183")
Chapter 26, "The VRS Assault on Jajce, July-November 1992"
================================================================================

"While operations in the Posavina continued, the VRS began preparations during June and July to
eliminate the large Croat-Muslim salient at Jajce, northwest of Travnik. ... The VRS was able to
deploy some 7,000 to 8,000 of its troops from the 30th Infantry Division of the 1st Krajina Corps."

"Preliminary operations in July, however, failed to gain significant ground and the VRS switched
instead to a straightforward attack toward Jajce itself."

"This slow campaign began in earnest in mid-August when the VRS launched its first major attack ...
advancing to within 2 kilometers of the town. The second step came in early/mid-September ...
pushing to within a kilometer of Jajce from that direction."

"On 21 October, Croat and Muslim units holding the main supply route to Jajce opened fire on each
other. The VRS took advantage of the discord and launched a final push to seize the enclave on
25 October, attacking hard along all three axes. ... VRS troops entered the center of town on
29 October while a bedraggled column ... some 40,000 panicked civilians made their way out of the
enclave to Travnik."

BB2, printed folio 329-330  (pdftotext scan 348-349)
Annex 29, "Operation 'Vrbas 92': The VRS Assault on Jajce, July-November 1992"
================================================================================

"The 30th Division chose three primary attack routes toward Jajce town, running from the north,
the west, and the south/southwest."

"The third route, from the south/southwest, began at the initial frontline some five to seven
kilometers outside of town. This route actually was split into two parallel routes, one running
along the Selinac River valley and the other along the Vrbas River valley FROM THE DIRECTION OF
SERB-HELD DONJI VAKUF (SRBOBRAN)."

"Two light infantry brigades, the 19th at Donji Vakuf and the 22nd at Mount Vlasic near Travnik,
protected the flanks of the assault forces."

"... the HVO and TO hoped to disrupt its preparations with an attack in central Bosnia against VRS
positions between Donji Vakuf and Mount Vlasic, north of Travnik. Launched about 9 September, it
fizzled out on 12 September with little or nothing gained."

BB2, printed folio 332  (pdftotext scan 351)
Annex 29 — the campaign's last act, and where the 1st Krajina Corps STOPPED
================================================================================

"The last act in the campaign came in mid-November. VRS forces had occupied nearly all of the
former enclave, but the stub of the neck that had led into it still stuck out stubbornly near
Turbe. Eliminating this stub would place VRS forces in a position to pressure not only Turbe but
Travnik, if they wanted that option. So, on 12 November, 30th Division forces began grinding away
at the awkward salient at Karaula, and erased it from the map 18 November. HERE THE VRS HALTED,
APPARENTLY CONTENT WITH ITS GAINS."

BB2, printed folio 465  (pdftotext scan 484)
Annex 50, "'Just Out of Reach': Donji Vakuf, 1994"
================================================================================

"Before the war, Donji Vakuf had had a population of some 25,000 residents, a little over half of
them Muslim and the remainder Croats and Serbs. THE BOSNIAN SERBS, HOWEVER, TOOK OVER THE TOWN
EARLY IN 1992, driving thousands of Muslims from their homes to join the thousands of other
refugees in the Travnik area. The triumphant Serbs renamed the town 'Srbobran' and incorporated it
into their expanding republic. After the Bosnian Government's capture of Bugojno in July 1993,
however, the Serb-Muslim frontline had been pushed back to within a few kilometers of the
outskirts of Donji Vakuf."

"On the Bosnian Serb Army side, the 19th Krajina and 11th Mrkonjic Grad Light Infantry Brigades of
the 1st Krajina Corps's 30th Light Infantry Division, commanded by Colonel Jovo Blazanovic, bore
the weight of the defense of the Donji Vakuf area. The 19th Brigade was deployed directly in front
of the town ... Still further north was the 30th Division's other brigade, the 1st Sipovo Light
Infantry Brigade, facing the town of Turbe west of Travnik."

"... the newly established 7th Corps ... Composed largely of Muslims evicted from the Donji Vakuf
area (including the displaced 770th 'Donji Vakuf' brigade)"

BB2, printed folio 466  (pdftotext scan 485)
Annex 50 — Prusac is the BOSNIAN ARMY's forward position, 1994
================================================================================

April 1994: "Alagic's forces pressed the attack from two directions, advancing FROM PRUSAC to the
south and Bugojno to the east."

November 1994: "But despite a day of heavy shelling, the Bosnian Army COULD NOT PUSH PAST PRUSAC,
7 km south of Donji Vakuf. The ARBiH and VRS tried to push each other off the strategic high
ground over the next few days, but neither could dislodge the other."

Same passage: "On 4 November the 7th Corps mounted determined infantry assaults against the 19th
Brigade defenders of Koscani (about 7km southwest of Donji Vakuf), Kopcici, and Urije." — Koscani
is SERB-held in November 1994; it is a member settlement of op:donji_vakuf:pribraca_2.

ORDER-OF-BATTLE TABLES — what they are, and what they are dated
================================================================================

BB2, printed folio 277-279: "Skeleton Order of Battle, Bosnian Serb Army, June 1992-October 1995".
A SPAN, not a snapshot. Entries relevant here:
    16th Krajina Motorized Brigade, HQ Banja Luka
    11th Mrkonjic Light Infantry Brigade, HQ Mrkonjic Grad
    19th Krajina Light Infantry Brigade, HQ Srbobran (Donji Vakuf)
    22nd Krajina Infantry Brigade, HQ Knezevo (Skender Vakuf)
    31st Light Infantry Brigade, HQ Srbobran (Donji Vakuf)
    43rd Prijedor Motorized Brigade, HQ Prijedor
    5th Kozara Light Infantry Brigade, HQ Prijedor-Omarska
The table's own start date, June 1992, already carries the Serb renaming "Srbobran" for Donji
Vakuf — the takeover predates the table.

BB1, printed folio 459-461 (KB page 496-498): "Appendix G — Skeleton Bosnian Serb Army Order of
Battle, JULY 1995". This is the table the Operation Donji Vakuf comment cites as "BB1 p.498" for a
November-1992 roster. It is a JULY 1995 snapshot and cannot establish 1992 deployment.

BB1, printed folio 198  (pdftotext scan 235)
Chapter 45, "The ARBiH Captures Bugojno, 18-25 July [1993]" — the front south of Donji Vakuf
================================================================================

"Until then [mid-1993], the town [Bugojno] was jointly defended FROM THE BOSNIAN SERBS by the
HVO's 'Eugen Kvaternik' Brigade and the Bosnian Army's 307th 'Bugojno' Mountain Brigade.
Interethnic relations remained relatively good until the first Croat-Muslim fighting in Gornji
Vakuf in January 1993, after which they became extremely tense."

Read with BB2 p.465 ("After the Bosnian Government's capture of Bugojno in July 1993 ... the
Serb-Muslim frontline had been pushed back to within a few kilometers of the outskirts of Donji
Vakuf"), this establishes a STATIC Serb-vs-Croat/Muslim front north of Bugojno through the
1992-93 winter — a defended confrontation line, not a VRS advance.
