# Local occupation post-measurement diagnosis

Verdict: the retained artifacts do not demonstrate that the new local route stole an active or queued historical commitment, selected an unavailable donor, or directly changed the Farz formation pool. No source correction is supported by this evidence.

Six operations have the new route's observable signature: they are new relative to the retained baseline, commander-named, single-formation operations, they attack at `started_turn + 1`, and their only battle is against modeled militia with no physical brigade. They are Odmazda (Arapuša, t21→22), Bastion (Baljvine, t31→32), Stjena (Jezero, t32→33), Javor (Donji Korićani, t35→36), Zaslon (Lupnica, t36→37), and Džihad (Putićevo, t83→84). All six captured through logged combat. The operation AAR format does not serialize `minimum_viable_participants`, `minimum_assembled_participants`, or `preparation_sub_phase`, so the retained run cannot independently print those fields after completion; the combined new-only/one-brigade/start+1 signature is the available attribution evidence.

The temporal log shows each donor free in the turn before admission. The only apparent edge is `rs_3rd_banja_luka_light_infantry`: it was in recovery from a probe at t35 and entered Zaslon planning at t36. That is a completed normal recovery transition, not simultaneous ownership. None of the six local operations uses a 2nd Corps brigade or one of Farz's four participants. The only ARBiH local donor, `arbih_330th_liberation`, completed its authored Central Bosnia Counteroffensive at t69 and did not enter Džihad until t83. Queue membership itself is not serialized in the weekly or AAR artifacts, so artifact-only proof of every negative queue state is unavailable; there is no overlap in the completed operation chronology.

Farz used the same four 3rd Corps brigades in both runs and captured Briješnica Donja at t169 in both. The capturing formation changed from `arbih_327th_vitezka_mountain` to `arbih_328th_mountain`; both captures belong to Operation Farz, not a 2nd Corps operation. The new run admitted Farz at t162 instead of t163 because the immediately preceding Operation Vlasic Ridge ended at t161 instead of t163. Vlasic captured all three objectives with five participants and four attacks in the new run; the old run captured none with four participants and two attacks. This explains the immediate scheduling difference, but the retained artifacts do not isolate which earlier topology/allocation divergence supplied Vlasic's fifth participant.

The 2nd Corps continued probing Briješnica throughout both runs. Its probes use brigades absent from the local-operation list. Their combat results diverged well before Farz: the new run suffered repeated failures against stronger VRS defenders, while the old run recorded repeated victories. That is consistent with a downstream battlefield-state cascade, not direct local-route commitment theft.

Evidence sources: both runs' `operation_aars.json`, `weekly_report.jsonl`, `brigade_temporal_log.jsonl`, and `final_save.json`. Structured findings are in `measurement-diagnosis.json`.
