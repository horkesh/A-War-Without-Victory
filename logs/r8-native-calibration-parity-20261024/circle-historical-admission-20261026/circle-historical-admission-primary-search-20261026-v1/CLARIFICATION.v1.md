# Clarification v1: Tactical Group II and the 7 July Foča Tactical Group order

This clarification narrows two statements in `finding.md`; it does not alter that file or its `citations.json`.

## 1. Tactical Group II, 31 July 1992

**Direct record fact.** In the 24 May 2000 Kunarac transcript, witness Alija Alić identifies himself as Tactical Group II's chief of staff (p. 4023). He says Serb forces controlled Rogoj in July (p. 4025). Asked whether Tactical Group II tried to take it on 31 July, he answers yes and says: **“We started the attack simultaneously”** on Trnovo, with Rogoj Pass as the objective (p. 4026). He later identifies captured materiel as taken from Serbian forces (p. 4027). His stated purposes were to sever the Sarajevo–Kalinovik–Foča road, connect with forces in eastern Bosnia, enable refugee passage, and create conditions to lift the siege of Sarajevo (pp. 4024–4025). [Official transcript](https://www.icty.org/x/cases/kunarac/trans/en/000524it.htm).

A different witness in the 20 March transcript says Army of Bosnia-Herzegovina personnel captured documents after that army “took over Trnovo” on 31 July (pp. 392–393). [Official transcript](https://www.icty.org/x/cases/kunarac/trans/en/000320it.htm).

**Limited inference.** These passages establish that Tactical Group II attacked Serb-held Rogoj while simultaneously attacking Trnovo, and separately place an Army of Bosnia-Herzegovina takeover of Trnovo on the same date. They strongly identify Tactical Group II as fighting on the Bosnian-government side, but the cited pages do not state its exact formal chain-of-command label. They do not establish that Tactical Group II alone captured Trnovo, the participating brigades, a Donje Žešće/Tošići capture chain, or an operation named Circle/Krug.

**Calendar inference.** The documented 31 July action falls four days after the production label `t16 = 27 July 1992`, within the seven-day span beginning on that label. This supports chronological compatibility only. It does not prove that an authored Circle launch at t16 represents this action.

## 2. Foča Tactical Group order, 7 July 1992

**Direct record fact.** The 20 March transcript describes Exhibit 2 as a 7 July order from the Foča Tactical Group commander, translated as an **“order to break the siege of Gorazde.”** It is signed by Colonel Marko Kovač and bears the stamp of the Territorial Defence of the Serbian Autonomous District of Herzegovina (pp. 394–395). The Mladić Trial Judgment identifies Kovač as Foča Tactical Group commander and says he ordered named units to assist in breaking through that siege (vol. I, ¶181, p. 97). The listed formations include Foča Light Infantry Brigades and explicitly Serbian-designated units; the surrounding judgment describes Foča Tactical Group reporting to the VRS Main Staff through the Herzegovina Corps. [Judgment](https://www.icty.org/x/cases/mladic/tjug/en/171122-1of5_1.pdf).

**Limited inference.** This is a Serbian-side order. The English phrase alone does not identify who was besieging whom, the intended beneficiary of the breakthrough, or whether it meant ending the siege of the RBiH-held city. It should therefore be described literally as a Serbian-side Foča Tactical Group order whose translated objective was to “break the siege of Goražde,” without recasting it as a VRS effort to lift a siege imposed by VRS forces. It is separate from the 31 July Tactical Group II evidence and supplies no ARBiH order of battle.

The admission verdict remains **UNRESOLVED_NAMED_OPERATION_AND_AGGREGATE_CONTROL**. No brigade, aggregate-control, or capture/retention conclusion follows from this clarification.
