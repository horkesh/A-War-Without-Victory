# Independent canon review supplement: REQUEST-OP preparation and delivery

**Review date:** 2026-10-26  
**Source:** `33f99fc5aac9a620a7bb0000cf233d86f270ce24`  
**Scope:** source/canon review of the retained V4 negative result only. No engine, test, build, native, or retry was run. No gameplay source was edited.

## Verdict

**The retained launch is not evidence that anti-paralysis violated its governing contract.** Anti-paralysis deliberately ends the preparation wait and opens a forced attempt at the preparation deadline. It may bypass the ordinary `force_staging` sub-phase's 60% wait, but it does **not** bypass the later participant/opening-attack gates. The ordinary REQUEST-OP path did not set `force_launch`; `was_force_launched` records that the president overruled the CO's advice and pays the command-friction consequence. Those are separate facts.

**The 60% value is not a canon launch rule and cannot be promoted into one without an owner/canon decision.** Applying it ahead of `anti_paralysis_launch` would change the existing forced-attempt mechanic. The completed first-rejection trace instead proves a selector/consumer mismatch: REQUEST-OP admitted eight brigades below the existing canonical 500-person offensive floor, while the normal dispatcher correctly held those combat-ineffective brigades in defense before operation routing. The smallest canon-aligned correction is to apply that existing floor in the regular REQUEST selector before its reachable-force count and fail closed when fewer than three eligible brigades remain. It must preserve every current ownership, route, control/alliance, enclave, supply, disruption, commitment, player-order, and transit guard. No threshold, timer, movement, combat, or control rule changes.

## Governing contract

1. **Force over objection is legitimate command authority, not a readiness waiver.** The player is the president and may request an operation even against the CO's military judgment; the CO still owns brigade assignments, axes, tempo, preparation, and launch execution (`Game_Bible_v0_9_0.md:290-300`; `Rulebook_v0_9_0.md:525-536`; `Systems_Manual_v0_9_0.md:970-978`). REQUEST-OP injects an ordinary `sector_attack`, tags `was_force_launched` only when an objection was overridden, and does not set `force_launch` (`src/sim/turn_phases/war_phases.ts:883-902,938-982`). The negative run therefore represents a legitimate presidential override of advice followed by the ordinary operation lifecycle.

2. **Anti-paralysis deliberately overrides the sub-phase wait, not the outer launch gates.** Engine Invariants require one full planning turn, make `planning_duration` the march/preparation and anti-paralysis budget, and state that expiry opens the existing forced-attempt or named-recovery path while readiness and attack-executability remain binding (`Engine_Invariants_v0_9_0.md:471-475`). Systems Manual §7.6 expressly says the system forces launch at `preparation_max_turns` to prevent indefinite preparation and repeats the forced-attempt/recovery rule (`Systems_Manual_v0_9_0.md:419-443`). Source implements that decision before the normal sub-phase switch (`operation_preparation.ts:1412-1423,1473-1507`), then the lifecycle still checks participant readiness and opening-attack executability unless `force_launch` is true (`sector_offensive.ts:1668-1731`). In the retained result, one approach-ready/executable axis was enough for those existing gates; this is the current multi-axis contract (`sector_offensive_launch_helpers.ts:620-665,1344-1441`).

3. **The 60% preparation count is a broad operation-sector proxy.** `ASSEMBLY_THRESHOLD = 0.6` exists in source, not in the governing canon. During the ordinary `force_staging` case, the count includes the operation staging OSID, first objective, every `territory_osid` in the resolved operation sector, and that sector's sub-segment friendly OSIDs; it is therefore neither exact arrival at Delijaš nor presence anywhere in the corps' full front (`operation_preparation.ts:987-1040,1578-1598`). The lifecycle resolves the operation sector from same-corps sectors overlapping the objective, preferring a sector containing staging and then participant overlap (`sector_offensive.ts:1251-1298`). Rulebook requires "sufficient forces ... staged near the objective" but supplies no 60% numeric rule (`Rulebook_v0_9_0.md:319-340`). The ordinary branch also has its own five-turn assembly timeout. Consequently, the observed 5/10 versus 6/10 explains which branch was skipped; it does not establish a canon violation.

4. **An offensively eligible REQUEST-OP roster is owed a real planning-movement handoff.** Rulebook says Force Staging is the phase in which participating brigades move into position, and the CO owns brigade assignments and launch execution (`Rulebook_v0_9_0.md:319-340,525-536`). The movement authority contract assigns strategic intent to T1, tactical translation to T2, and physical movement to T3; active-operation staging and approach destinations are a higher-priority authority exempt from routine sub-segment scope, while ordinary corps, enclave, player-override, and route legality remain binding (`docs/20_engineering/MOVEMENT_AUTHORITY.md:8-19,23-40,45-111,139-152`). Source implements that handoff for an active, attack-capable `sector_attack` participant in planning: it finds the participant's operation approach set, emits a column march toward the nearest legal approach, falls back to axis/operation staging only when no approach exists, and then holds defensive posture (`bot_brigade_eval_attack.ts:211-289`). The generic 500-person attack floor is canonical (`Rulebook_v0_9_0.md:281-289`; `formation_constants.ts:76-82`).

   The floor applies to every REQUEST participant, not only an assigned brigade already on its own sector front. `evaluateGarrisonAndDetachments` may hold an on-front below-floor formation first, but its off-front exception only passes control onward (`bot_brigade_eval_hold.ts:23-36`). `evaluateSectorMarch` exempts any active-operation participant from routine sector marching so the operation evaluator can own the route (`bot_brigade_eval_front.ts:169-179,228-235`). `evaluateSectorAttack` then tests `MIN_ATTACK_PERSONNEL` before its planning/execution split, and its off-front escape is disabled for an active sector-operation participant (`bot_brigade_eval_attack.ts:211-230`). A reserve participant likewise bypasses the reserve hold because of its operation commitment (`bot_brigade_eval_hold.ts:39-73`) and reaches the same universal sector-attack floor. The normal consumer therefore cannot route or attack any below-floor REQUEST participant regardless of line/reserve placement. This behavior is not a missing movement owner.

5. **REQUEST-OP does not owe exact staging-cell arrival by every selected brigade before a deadline attempt.** The selector proves physical friendly/allied connectivity to one own-controlled, target-adjacent staging OSID and builds a canonical 3-12 brigade roster (`Systems_Manual_v0_9_0.md:976-978`; `war_phases.ts:790-879`). It explicitly does not alter movement. The injected op becomes visible to normal brigade order generation later that turn, after that turn's physical movement step; physical movement therefore begins on later movement steps (`war_phases.ts:1599-1635,2101-2113,2710-2718`). Canon requires a genuine staff routing/execution handoff, not teleportation and not guaranteed simultaneous arrival.

## Disposition of the negative finding

- **Accepted as legitimate:** overriding the CO's `delay` recommendation; recording `was_force_launched`; paying the existing command-authority consequence; reaching the anti-paralysis decision deadline; a failed ordinary assault; preserving the 704 score and all three protected losses as an honest negative result.
- **Not established as a defect:** anti-paralysis occurring at 5/10 under the source-only 60% proxy; failure to reach Delijaš specifically; lack of simultaneous ten-brigade concentration; failure to capture Tošići.
- **Confirmed selector/consumer defect:** the regular REQUEST selector admitted eight formations with 315-491 personnel even though the canonical consumer floor is 500. At T16 only the 441st (1,528) and 443rd (1,383) cleared the floor; the minimum-three regular force therefore did not exist. The 442nd's first rejecting predicate was the ordinary below-floor defend gate, and the same floor explains all eight non-movers. The two above-floor comparisons prove that the movement owner exists: the 441st received and completed its ordinary operation-authorized march, while the already-positioned 443rd attacked. This is neither an anti-paralysis defect nor a legitimate ten-brigade delivery failure.

## Smallest allowed implementation scope

The allowed correction is limited to applying `MIN_ATTACK_PERSONNEL` in `planDirectiveOperation` before staging-candidate reachable-count and minimum-three selection. On the retained T16 input, only two formations remain and the existing planner must fail closed through its existing `no_available_force` result. This aligns admission with the ordinary consumer and with the canonical generic attack floor; it does not authorize bypassing the defend-only gate. The authored historical-operation selector already applies the same existing floor before admission (`operation_opportunities.ts:1800-1808`), which supports the boundary without generalizing authored-operation exceptions into REQUEST-OP.

It must not:

- add or change an assembly percentage, `preparation_max_turns`, `planning_duration`, assembly timeout, or anti-paralysis reset;
- require every participant to reach the exact staging OSID or Delijaš;
- relax roster eligibility, availability, same-corps ownership, alliance/control connectivity, enclave policy, health, disruption, supply, commitment, or player-order precedence, or bypass the below-floor defensive gate;
- change attack prediction, combat power, casualty resolution, control/capture authority, or calibration targets;
- manufacture movement, attack, or capture evidence.

Aligning disruption or in-transit admission is outside this correction because neither caused the retained failure and the bounded trace did not establish their selector/consumer effect. Changing anti-paralysis or making 60% mandatory would require an explicit owner decision and a canon amendment reconciling the forced-attempt clause with a new assembly floor.
