# The Ceasefire That Was Not Broken Within the Window

**Ring 2 — narrative observation. Path-not-taken. Ghost entry.**

The Bosnian War's ceasefire history is a record of agreements signed and violated. *Balkan Battlegrounds* II catalogues the pattern across the war's principal axes: the Vance–Owen-era cessations of late 1992 and early 1993, the central-Bosnia local agreements that punctuated the Bosniak–Croat war from October 1992 through the Washington Agreement of March 1994, the Sarajevo total-exclusion-zone agreement of February 1994, and the cessation arrangements that culminated in the Dayton General Framework Agreement, initialled on 21 November and signed in Paris on 14 December 1995. *Prosecutor v. Galić* (IT-98-29-T) and *Prosecutor v. Dragomir Milošević* (IT-98-29/1-T) adjudicated the campaign of sniping and shelling against Sarajevo's civilians during the siege, including periods covered by ceasefire and exclusion-zone agreements. The pattern is one of agreements honoured intermittently and locally, broken under operational pressure, and renewed under international auspices.
