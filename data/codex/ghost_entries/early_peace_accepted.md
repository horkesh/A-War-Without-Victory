# Vance–Owen / Owen–Stoltenberg: The Peace Plan That Was Accepted

**Ring 2 — narrative observation. Path-not-taken. Ghost entry.**

The international diplomatic record of the Bosnian War contains a series of formally tabled peace proposals — the Cutileiro plan in early 1992, the Vance–Owen Peace Plan in early 1993, the Owen–Stoltenberg plan in summer 1993, and the Contact Group plan in mid-1994 — each of which proposed a constitutional and territorial settlement before the war reached its 1995 endpoint. Each was rejected, withdrawn, or negotiated past. The Vance–Owen plan was rejected at the Bosnian Serb Assembly in Pale on 5–6 May 1993, a decision later examined in the Karadžić trial in *Prosecutor v. Karadžić* (IT-95-5/18-T) for what it revealed about the political leadership's commitment to a settlement on those terms. The Owen–Stoltenberg plan was tabled in August 1993 and likewise refused.

In this campaign, one of those earlier plans was accepted. Either Vance–Owen was accepted in the recorded turn window of weeks fifty through seventy, or Owen–Stoltenberg was accepted in the window of weeks seventy through ninety. The acceptance was mutually exclusive with the December 1995 Dayton Peace Agreement, and Dayton therefore did not occur in this run.

What an earlier settlement would have meant in detail is not for this entry to assert. The historical plans were, by the assessment of their negotiators and subsequent commentators, neither perfect instruments nor cost-free. They embedded the front lines that existed when they were drawn and left unresolved questions that the war then continued to answer through force. The terms accepted in this campaign apply to whatever territorial and political situation prevailed at the moment of acceptance.

The historical Tribunal record on the rejection of Vance–Owen, including the May 1993 Pale Assembly vote, remains in the canonical Codex. Acceptance in this campaign does not displace that record; it diverges from it.
