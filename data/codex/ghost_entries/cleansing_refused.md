# Paramilitary Deployment: The Authorisation That Was Never Given

**Ring 2 — narrative observation. Path-not-taken. Ghost entry.**

In the historical war, ethnic cleansing was carried out as policy. The Trial Chambers of the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia documented its execution across the Drina valley, Prijedor, the Lašva valley, Mostar, and beyond, in judgments including *Prosecutor v. Karadžić* (IT-95-5/18-T), *Prosecutor v. Krajišnik* (IT-00-39-T), *Prosecutor v. Lukić & Lukić* (IT-98-32/1-T) on Višegrad, *Prosecutor v. Kunarac et al.* (IT-96-23-T & IT-96-23/1-T) on the Foča rape camps, and *Prosecutor v. Prlić et al.* (IT-04-74-T) on the joint criminal enterprise in Herceg-Bosna. Paramilitary formations — Arkan's Tigers, the White Eagles, HOS detachments, and others — operated in coordination with regular army and police units, conducting the killings, expulsions, sexual violence, and detention that gave the campaigns their particular character.

In this campaign, the player faction's standing policy was set to refuse paramilitary deployment for the entire run. No paramilitary capture was authorised. The war crimes counter for the player faction stands at zero through turn one hundred and beyond.

The historical convictions stand. The Tribunal's findings on what was done in Višegrad, Foča, Prijedor, Ahmići, Stupni Do, and the scheduled municipalities of the Karadžić indictment remain in the record. This entry does not relativise those crimes, suggest that other factions' atrocities were less grave, or claim a moral comparison favourable to the player.

What is recorded here is the narrow operational fact: when the standing order was put — repeatedly, across the run — the answer given was always to deny. The campaigns of expulsion that ICTY judgments document at the level of policy were, in this case and at this command level, not ordered.
