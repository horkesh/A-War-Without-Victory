# Equipment Quality: The Collapse Recorded in This Run

**Ring 2 — narrative observation. Divergence note. Ghost entry. CoS analytical assessment.**

In the canonical record, the equipment-quality trajectory across the war's three faction tracks bent in characteristic ways. The Army of Republika Srpska's per-brigade combat power, inherited at scale from the Yugoslav People's Army's deployed arsenal in May 1992 and sustained by Federal Republic of Yugoslavia logistical and personnel support, including the 30th Personnel Centre, examined in *Prosecutor v. Perišić* (IT-04-81-T; conviction reversed on appeal, IT-04-81-A), declined gradually under attrition, maintenance load, and cohort rotation but did not catastrophically fail before the war's end. The Croatian Defence Council, after the Washington Agreement of March 1994, received reinfusion through the Croatian Army cadre-mentorship and equipment channels documented in *Balkan Battlegrounds* II. The Army of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina rebuilt its equipment baseline through the embargo-and-pipeline period and into the late-war reform pulse described in the canonical Codex.

In this run, the equipment-quality-collapse audit flag had been set by the audit turn.

The mechanism is recorded in the run's per-brigade attrition, replacement, equipment-quality-modifier, and cohort tables. The downstream effect is visible in the late-run combat-power readings against opposing forces.

This entry does not claim that the collapse altered the war's outcome, prevented atrocity, or produced any specific operational consequence beyond what the run's own ledgers record. The historical baseline against which this run diverges remains in the canonical Codex.
