# Negotiation Capital: The Floor Reached Without Settlement

**Ring 2 — narrative observation. Divergence note. Ghost entry.**

In the historical war, the international diplomatic register passed through a sequence of plans — Cutileiro in early 1992, Vance–Owen in early 1993, Owen–Stoltenberg in summer 1993, the Contact Group in mid-1994, and finally the Dayton Peace Agreement in December 1995. Each plan was, in negotiation-capital terms, an attempt to convert accumulated diplomatic standing — international credibility, alliance trust, refusal-to-be-the-spoiler reputation, accumulated humanitarian record — into a settlement instrument. The plans that failed left their negotiating parties with reduced capital on each rejection, and the cumulative depletion is part of why Dayton, when it came, was on terms harder than those Vance–Owen had offered. The Trial Chamber findings in *Prosecutor v. Karadžić* (IT-95-5/18-T) on the May 1993 Pale Assembly vote address the political mechanics of one such rejection.

In this campaign, on the player faction's track, the cumulative depletion was sharper. By the audit turn, the negotiation-capital-exhausted flag had been set on the player faction's ledger, and no peace plan in the canonical sequence — Vance–Owen, Owen–Stoltenberg, Contact Group, Dayton — registered as accepted within the run's recorded acceptance window. The capital depletion proceeded faster than the historical pattern projects, and the floor was reached without any of the canonical settlements being signed.

What that meant for the run's terminal trajectory is recorded in its own ledgers. This entry does not assert that earlier acceptance would have produced a better outcome on any specific dimension; it does not assert that the failure to accept reflected any single decision; and it does not displace the canonical Codex on the historical plans, their terms, or the documented mechanics of their rejection.

What is recorded here is the divergence point: in this run, on the player faction's track, the diplomatic capital ran out without a plan being signed.
