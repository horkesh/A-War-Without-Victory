# The Posavina Corridor That Was Not Opened

**Ring 2 — narrative observation. Path-not-taken. Ghost entry.**

In the historical war, Operation Corridor opened on 24 June 1992 and, by mid-July, had linked Bosanska Krajina with eastern Republika Srpska and the territory of the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia through Serbian-controlled ground, although the strip near Brčko remained narrow, about three kilometres wide at points at the end of 1992. *Balkan Battlegrounds* I (pp. 177–182) document the operational arc and its consequences. The corridor became the principal supply artery sustaining the western part of Republika Srpska for the remainder of the war, and the northern front line at Brčko became a long-running attritional emergency for all forces contesting the Sava axis. The corridor's establishment is recorded as a strategic-level event in the canonical military Codex.

In this campaign, the corridor breakthrough did not occur within the historical operational window. The corresponding event in the scenario timeline did not fire by the audit turn. The corridor-secured flag remained unset.

What that meant for the western part of Republika Srpska, for the Sava-axis front, and for the supply tempo of forces operating on either side of the disputed strip is recorded in the run's territorial, supply, and casualty ledgers and propagated through them. This entry asserts the operational divergence and nothing further.

The historical record on the corridor — its strategic value, the cost of establishing it, the cost of contesting it, the geographic logic that made it both indispensable and vulnerable — remains in the canonical Codex. This run diverges from that record at the predicate-firing point, not in the canonical assessment of what the corridor was.
