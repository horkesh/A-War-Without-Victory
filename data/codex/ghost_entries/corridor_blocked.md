# The Posavina Corridor That Was Not Cut

**Ring 2 — narrative observation. Path-not-taken. Ghost entry.**

In the historical war, Operation Corridor opened in late June 1992 and, by mid-July, had widened the narrow Posavina strip near Brčko sufficiently to link Bosanska Krajina with the territory of the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia through Serbian-controlled ground. *Balkan Battlegrounds* I Chapters 16–17 document the operational arc and its consequences. The corridor became the principal supply artery sustaining the western Republika Srpska entity for the remainder of the war, and the northern front line at Brčko became a long-running attritional emergency for HVO and ARBiH forces along the Sava axis. The corridor's establishment is recorded as a strategic-level event in the canonical military Codex.

In this campaign, the corridor breakthrough did not occur within the historical operational window. The combined predicate — Republika Srpska control of the Brčko operational settlement above the audit threshold and the JNA-withdrawal flag set — was not satisfied within the turn window in which Operation Corridor would historically have triggered, and the corresponding event in the scenario timeline did not fire by the audit turn. The corridor-secured flag remained unset.

What that meant for the western Republika Srpska entity, for the Sava-axis front, and for the supply tempo of forces operating on either side of the disputed strip is recorded in the run's territorial, supply, and casualty ledgers and propagated through them. This entry asserts the operational divergence and nothing further.

The historical record on the corridor — its strategic value, the cost of establishing it, the cost of contesting it, the geographic logic that made it both indispensable and vulnerable — remains in the canonical Codex. This run diverges from that record at the predicate-firing point, not in the canonical assessment of what the corridor was.
