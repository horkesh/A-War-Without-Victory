# The Embargo That Was Not Worked Around

**Ring 2 — narrative observation. Path-not-taken. Ghost entry.**

UN Security Council Resolution 713, adopted on 25 September 1991, imposed an arms embargo on the territory of the former Yugoslavia. The resolution did not, in operational practice, hold uniformly. The historical record contains the documented Croatian arms-pipeline mechanism through which materiel transited to the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina and to the Croatian Defence Council; the documented patron-supplied flow from the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia to the Army of Republika Srpska through logistical and personnel support, including the 30th Personnel Centre, examined in *Prosecutor v. Perišić* (IT-04-81-T; conviction reversed on appeal, IT-04-81-A); and the third-party arms channels — including the Iran-channel pipeline — that operated alongside the formal embargo regime through 1994 and into 1995. The Tribunal record and *Balkan Battlegrounds* II document the pattern.

In this campaign, on the player faction's track, no third-party arms channel registered as active in the run's ledgers, and no pipeline-attenuation event subsequently fired (which would have been a downstream consequence of a prior pipeline activation).

The historical embargo-and-pipeline pattern remains in the canonical Codex. The questions it raised in international debate at the time — whether the embargo was equitable in its operational effect given the asymmetric distribution of the Yugoslav People's Army's arsenal, whether enforcement was uniform across the parties, whether the pipeline channels altered the conduct or duration of the war — are not adjudicated by this entry. This entry does not displace the Tribunal record on the patron-supply mechanism.

What is recorded here is narrow: in this run, on the player faction's specific equipment-quality track, the third-party channel did not activate. The cost is recorded in the run's own ledgers.
