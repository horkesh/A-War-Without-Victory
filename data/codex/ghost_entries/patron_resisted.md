# The Patron That Was Refused

**Ring 2 — narrative observation. Path-not-taken. Ghost entry.**

The factions that fought in Bosnia did not fight alone. The Federal Republic of Yugoslavia under Slobodan Milošević supplied, paid, and at intervals directed the Army of Republika Srpska through the 30th and 40th Personnel Centres of the General Staff in Belgrade. The Republic of Croatia under Franjo Tuđman supplied and politically directed the Croatian Defence Council. The relationship was not advisory. The Trial Chamber in *Prosecutor v. Perišić* (IT-04-81-T) made detailed findings on the systematic provision of personnel, ammunition, and command continuity from the Yugoslav Army to the VRS through the personnel-centre mechanism. *Prosecutor v. Prlić et al.* (IT-04-74-T) addressed the political and military relationship between Zagreb and Herceg-Bosna and entered findings consistent with sustained Croatian state involvement.

The pressures applied through these patron channels — for territorial absorption, for specific operations, for political alignment with the patron's external diplomacy — were not always uniform with the war aims of the Bosnian-side leadership being pressured. The historical record contains points at which those pressures were absorbed, redirected, and at times resisted, but the dominant pattern was alignment.

In this campaign, the leadership of the player faction declined the patron's pressure on at least three recorded occasions. The pressure-refusal counter on the relevant capital ledger stands at three or above. The diplomatic register of those refusals is recorded in the political ledger; the military and territorial consequences propagated through the run as the simulation determined.

This entry does not claim that resistance to the patron prevented atrocity, secured the war, or produced a favourable outcome. It records that the option of refusal was, in this campaign, exercised more than the historical record reflects.
