# The Patron That Was Refused

**Ring 2 — narrative observation. Path-not-taken. Ghost entry.**

The factions that fought in Bosnia did not fight alone. The Federal Republic of Yugoslavia under Slobodan Milošević supplied and paid the Army of Republika Srpska through the 30th Personnel Centre of the General Staff in Belgrade. The Republic of Croatia under Franjo Tuđman supplied and politically directed the Croatian Defence Council. The relationship was not advisory. The Trial Chamber in *Prosecutor v. Perišić* (IT-04-81-T) made findings on the provision of personnel, ammunition and logistical support from the Yugoslav Army to the VRS through the 30th Personnel Centre (Perišić was acquitted on appeal in 2013). *Prosecutor v. Prlić et al.* (IT-04-74-T) addressed the political and military relationship between Zagreb and Herceg-Bosna and entered findings on Croatian state involvement in Herceg-Bosna.

The pressures applied through these patron channels — for territorial absorption, for specific operations, for political alignment with the patron's external diplomacy — were not always uniform with the war aims of the Bosnian-side leadership being pressured. The historical record contains points at which those pressures were absorbed, redirected, and at times resisted.

In this campaign, the leadership of the player faction declined the patron's pressure on at least three recorded occasions. The pressure-refusal counter stands at three or above. The diplomatic register of those refusals is recorded in the political ledger.

This entry does not claim that resistance to the patron prevented atrocity, altered the war's outcome, or produced a favourable outcome. It records that the option of refusal was exercised in this campaign.
