# The Federation That Did Not Fracture

**Ring 2 — narrative observation. Path-not-taken. Ghost entry.**

In the historical war, the alliance between the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina and the Croatian Defence Council collapsed during the autumn of 1992 and the spring of 1993. By the time the Croat–Bosniak war began in earnest, central Bosnia had become a second front: HVO and ARBiH formations that had recently coordinated against the VRS turned on one another in Gornji Vakuf, Vitez, Travnik, and the Lašva Valley. The fracture was not inevitable, but it was political — driven, the ICTY found, by the leadership of the Croatian Community of Herceg-Bosna and its sponsors in Zagreb, who pursued a partition project incompatible with multi-ethnic Bosnian statehood.

In this campaign, that fracture did not occur. The federation that history achieved only at Washington in March 1994 — under American pressure and after a year of inter-allied bloodletting — held instead from the war's first months. The alliance threshold did not fall through the floor that triggers open conflict, and the Croat–Bosniak war event did not fire by week seventy.

The historical record of the fracture remains intact. The Trial Chamber in *Prosecutor v. Prlić et al.* (IT-04-74-T) found a joint criminal enterprise within the leadership of Herceg-Bosna directed at the ethnic restructuring of designated municipalities. Those convictions stand and are not displaced by counterfactual divergence in this campaign.

What is recorded here is narrower: in this run, the formations that historically faced one another at Ahmići and Stupni Do remained pointed outward. The internal front that history opened was not opened. The cost of the war that did continue is recorded in its own ledger.
