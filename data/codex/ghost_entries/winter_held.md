# Winter Supply: The Distribution That Held

**Ring 2 — narrative observation. Path-not-taken. Ghost entry.**

The two coldest periods of the war — the winter of 1992–93 and the winter of 1993–94 — produced documented strain on every faction's supply system. Distributed logistics improvised against fuel shortages, diesel rationing, mountain-road closures, and the additional draw on warm-weather equipment that cold-front operations imposed. *Balkan Battlegrounds* II Chapter 31 documents the pattern across central Bosnia, the Sarajevo siege ring, and the eastern enclaves: cohesion losses from improvisation, supply depots drawing harder than design tolerances, and operational tempo bending around the weather.

In this campaign, that strain was not absent; it was contained. The seasonal supply-attrition predicate was tested across the turn window in which the cold-weather draw would normally have triggered, but the joint condition — supply below the audit threshold, war exhaustion above the audit threshold, and morale below the audit threshold — was not met simultaneously for the player faction. The winter-supply-attrition flag did not register on the player faction's ledger by the recorded audit turn.

The mechanism that produced the divergence is recorded in the run's supply, exhaustion, and morale ledgers. It is not the case that the cold weather was warmer or the rationing was easier. It is the case that, by the joint audit-threshold reading, one of the three preconditions remained outside the trigger window when the others fell into it.

This entry does not claim that supply was abundant, that troops were comfortable, or that the war was less hard for being fought through these winters. It records that the audit predicate that historical patterns would normally fire did not fire in this run, and that the cohesion and supply costs documented in the canonical Codex did not register on the player faction's specific ledger at this audit turn.
